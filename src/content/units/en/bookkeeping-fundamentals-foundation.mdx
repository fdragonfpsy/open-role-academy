## Why this matters

Every number in a financial report starts as a single recorded transaction: a sale, a purchase, a payment. If those records are incomplete or wrong, everything built on them is wrong too. **Bookkeeping** is the systematic recording of a business's financial transactions. In finance and accounting, this is the base layer of your work.

## Accounts

An **account** is a record for one kind of thing, for example "Bank", "Office supplies expense" or "Sales revenue". Each account has a left side and a right side. In accounting language the left side is **debit** and the right side is **credit**. These words do not mean "good" or "bad" and have nothing to do with a bank card; they only say which side of the account an amount is written on. Which side increases an account depends on the account type: asset and expense accounts increase on the debit side, while liability, equity and revenue accounts increase on the credit side.

## Double entry

Most companies use **double-entry bookkeeping**: every transaction is recorded in at least two accounts, and the total of the debit amounts equals the total of the credit amounts. This keeps the books in balance and makes many mistakes visible, because an entry that does not balance is wrong by definition. The English and German Wikipedia articles on double-entry bookkeeping describe the principle and its history.

## Receipts as evidence

A booking without evidence is a claim, not a record. The **receipt** or **voucher** (German: Beleg) is the document behind the entry: an invoice, a bank statement, a cash receipt. Good practice is: no booking without a receipt, and the receipt shows date, parties, what was bought or sold and the amount. Keep receipts in a way that lets someone else find the receipt belonging to any entry.

## The journal entry

A **journal entry** (German: Buchungssatz) states which accounts are debited and credited and by how much. German textbooks often write it as "Account A an Account B", meaning debit A, credit B.

## Worked example

Illustration only; the amounts are invented for practice. A company buys printer paper and pays at once from its bank account. The paper is an expense of 50.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Office supplies expense | 50 | |
| Bank | | 50 |

The expense account increases on the debit side, the bank account (an asset) decreases on the credit side. Debits (50) equal credits (50). The receipt for the paper is attached to the entry. ## Key takeaways

- Bookkeeping records every financial transaction in a systematic way; reports depend on it.
- An account has a debit (left) and a credit (right) side; the words are technical, not judgements.
- Double entry: each transaction hits at least two accounts and debits equal credits.
- No entry without a receipt that shows who, what, when and how much.
- A journal entry names the accounts debited and credited and the amounts.
