## Why this matters

Most wasted analysis work does not come from wrong calculations. It comes from answering a question nobody asked. A few minutes spent framing the question save days of rework.

## Start with the decision

Requests often arrive as "Can you look at our customer data?" Ask what decision depends on the answer, who makes it and by when. A question is useful when different possible answers would lead to different actions.

## Make the question specific

A good analytical question names:

- The measure: what exactly is counted or compared.
- The scope: which customers, products, region and period.
- The comparison: against what, for example last year or another group.

"Why are we losing customers?" becomes "Which customer segments cancelled more often in the last 12 months than in the 12 months before, and in which month did the change start?"

## Check what is needed

- Which data would answer the question, and does it exist and is it accessible?
- What would count as a clear answer, such as a number, a ranking or a yes or no?
- What is out of scope for now?

Write this down in a short brief and confirm it with the requester before you start.

## Tell the finding in one sentence

A finding states what you saw, how big it is and why it matters, in plain words. Start with the answer, then give the evidence. Avoid reporting every step you took.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A team lead asks, "Are our support emails getting worse?" The analyst asks which decision is behind it. The answer: whether to add a person to the team. The question becomes "Has the median time to first reply increased over the last six months, and does it exceed the 24-hour target?" The brief lists the data needed (ticket timestamps), the output (one chart and one sentence) and the deadline. The finding reads: "The median first reply time rose from 9 to 14 hours but stays below the 24-hour target, so an extra hire is not yet urgent."

## Key takeaways

- Begin with the decision the analysis supports.
- A good question names measure, scope and comparison.
- Confirm a short brief with the requester before you analyse.
- State the finding in one sentence, answer first.
