## Why this matters

At advanced level you shape how a team handles data, not only how you handle your own. Good governance makes analysis faster and safer: everyone knows where data comes from, who may use it and when to stop and ask. This is topic-level guidance for the EU and Germany, not legal advice.

## Lineage and metadata

Data lineage records where data came from and which steps changed it. Metadata describes datasets: owner, meaning of columns, refresh time, sensitivity and permitted purposes. A simple data catalogue that lists this for each dataset lets reviewers trace a number back to its source and lets newcomers find out whether they may use it.

## Access: least privilege

- Give people the minimum access needed for their role and task.
- Prefer role-based groups over individual permissions.
- Separate raw personal data from analysis-ready or aggregated data, and let most analysts work on the latter.
- Review access regularly and remove it when a role changes.
- Keep a log of who accessed sensitive datasets.

## Assess a new use

Before a new analytics use of personal data, ask:

- What is the purpose, and is it compatible with why the data was collected?
- Which columns are truly needed (minimisation)?
- Can the work be done with pseudonymised or aggregated data?
- Who will see results, and could small groups be identified?
- Is the data shared outside the organisation or combined with external data?
- Is the use novel, large-scale or about sensitive topics?

If the answer to the last three raises doubt, escalate. The data protection officer (DPO) advises on and monitors data protection compliance; Legal interprets what the rules mean for your case. Bring a short description of purpose, data and safeguards so they can decide quickly.

## Balance privacy and speed

Do not treat governance as a stop sign. Offer fast routes: pre-approved aggregated datasets, a standard request form and a named contact. If governance is slow and unclear, people will build private copies, which is the larger risk.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented scenario. A product team wants to combine app usage logs with support tickets to predict who will cancel. The analyst lists purpose, columns and recipients, and notes that free-text tickets may contain personal details and that the plan involves profiling individual customers. Because the use is new and touches many customers, the analyst escalates to the DPO with a one-page description. Meanwhile, the team prepares a pseudonymised sample with only the needed fields, so that the approach can be tested without exposing unnecessary data.

## Key takeaways

- Lineage and metadata make analyses traceable and reusable.
- Least privilege and separate raw from aggregated data reduce risk.
- Use a checklist for new uses of personal data and escalate novel, large or sensitive cases.
- Fast, clear governance prevents private data copies.
