## Why this matters

Users do not care about servers or licences — they care whether they can work: send e-mails, open the customer database, join a video call. **IT service management (ITSM)** looks at IT from this angle. It is the set of activities to design, deliver, operate and control **IT services** for customers, with a process approach that focuses on customer needs and continual improvement.

## Services, not just technology

A **service** delivers value to users, for example "e-mail", "laptop workplace" or "access to the CRM". Behind each service there is technology, people and processes. Thinking in services helps IT ask the right questions: Who uses this? What do they need? What happens if it fails?

## Incidents and service requests

Two kinds of tickets reach IT every day:

- An **incident** is an unplanned interruption to an IT service or a reduction in its quality — e-mail is down, the VPN is very slow.
- A **service request** is a planned, routine request — a new laptop, access to a shared folder, a second monitor.

The distinction matters: incidents need fast restoration of service, requests need a reliable, often standardised fulfilment process.

## The service desk

The **service desk** is the single point of contact between users and IT. It records incidents and requests, routes them to the right team, follows them up and keeps users informed. A good service desk is reachable, friendly and transparent about what happens next.

## Service-level agreements

A **service-level agreement (SLA)** is an agreement between a service provider and its customers about measurable service targets, for example:

- **availability** of a service, e.g. during business hours;
- **response time** — how quickly work on a ticket starts;
- **resolution time** — by when an incident must be fixed.

SLAs make expectations clear on both sides and give a basis for reporting.

## Worked example

On Monday morning, Leon at the service desk receives two tickets: "Cannot print — whole floor affected" and "Need a headset for my new colleague". He records the first as an incident with high priority and informs the floor that the team is working on it. The second becomes a standard service request, fulfilled within the agreed three working days.

## Key takeaways

- ITSM manages IT as services focused on customer needs.
- Incident = unplanned disruption; service request = planned routine request.
- The service desk is the single point of contact and keeps users informed.
- SLAs define measurable targets such as availability, response and resolution time.
