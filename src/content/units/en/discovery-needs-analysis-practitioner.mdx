## Why this matters

At practitioner level you run discovery in a planned way and turn what you learn into a basis for the proposal. The aim is a shared, written understanding of the customer's goals, problems and how they decide, so that the later presentation matches it.

## Prepare

OpenStax calls the research before contact the pre-approach: find out what the customer's company does, what they use now and who decides, and enter it in the CRM. Then write three to five questions you most need answered, rather than a long script. Know which facts you already have so that you do not ask what you could have looked up.

## A question path

A useful structure (practice insight, one of several in use) moves from the present to the future:

1. **Situation:** "How do you currently handle returns?"
2. **Problem:** "Where does that cause trouble?"
3. **Impact:** "What does that cost you, in time, money or customers?" Let the customer name the numbers.
4. **Desired outcome:** "What would a good result look like in six months?"

The Fragetechnik article notes that the choice of question form controls how much you steer the conversation; for discovery, favour open questions and use closed ones to confirm. Probe with "Can you give me an example?" Silence after a question gives the customer room to think.

## Who decides, and how

OpenStax describes a buying centre: several people take part in business purchases, and the B2B buying process runs through problem recognition, need description, specification, supplier search, proposals, selection, order details and review. Ask: "Who else will be involved?", "What steps come before a decision?", "What must a solution do for you to say yes?" Note decision criteria in the customer's own words and distinguish must-have from nice-to-have.

## Listening and summarising

The Active listening article describes giving the speaker confirmation that their point has come across; summarising does exactly that. Summarise by theme and ask for confirmation, then agree the next step. If you notice that you are not talking to someone with influence, ask for an introduction instead of pitching to the wrong person.

## Notes and data

Record what is needed for the opportunity: needs, criteria, process, next step. Keep personal remarks out unless they are business relevant. Under the EU GDPR, personal data should be limited to what is necessary for the purpose (data minimisation). Ask your data-protection contact how long notes are kept.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. Discovery with a logistics firm. Situation: returns are checked manually by two clerks. Problem: errors cause wrong refunds. Impact: the manager says about ten refunds a week are wrong, each needing 30 minutes to correct. Outcome: "fewer than two a week". Process: the operations head recommends, the finance director signs above a threshold. You summarise, confirm, and agree to a demo where the finance director is present.

## Key takeaways

- Prepare with research and a short list of must-answer questions.
- Probe from situation to problem to impact to desired outcome.
- Let the customer quantify the impact and state decision criteria.
- Map the buying group and the decision process.
- Summarise, confirm and keep notes limited to what you need.
