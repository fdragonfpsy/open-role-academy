## Why this matters

Customers have rights over their data, and they often voice them to the service team first: "Send me everything you have on me" or "Stop emailing me". How you react in the first minutes decides whether the company can answer in time. This unit covers the topic level for the EU; national details, for example in Germany, belong to your data protection officer.

## The rights you will meet most

Under the GDPR, a person can ask the company for:

- Access (Art. 15): confirmation whether data about them is processed and a copy of that data with further information.
- Erasure (Art. 17): deletion of data in certain cases. The Article lists grounds and exceptions, so whether it applies is not for you to decide.
- Objection (Art. 21): in particular, a person can object at any time to the use of their data for direct marketing, and this is easy to act on, so route it immediately.

## Your role in a request (our own process)

Our own four steps:

- Recognise: treat anything that sounds like a request about "my data" as one, whatever words the customer uses.
- Record: note date, channel and exact wording, and open the case your company uses for this.
- Route: pass it on at once to the privacy team or the data protection officer; do not search and send data on your own.
- Reassure: tell the customer that the request is received, that it will be handled and who will reply. Make no promise about the result.

## Time and identity

The company must respond without undue delay and in any event within one month of receipt of the request; in complex cases this can be extended by two further months, and the customer must be told about the extension within the first month. If there are reasonable doubts about who is asking, the company may request additional information to confirm identity. So a quick handover to the right team matters, because the clock runs from receipt, not from when it reaches the right desk.

## Recording calls and chats

Recording a contact needs a legal basis, and customers must be informed about the processing when their data is collected. The details for recordings, especially in Germany, are specific, so follow the announcement and the script your company provides and send questions to the data protection officer. If a customer says "do not record me", do not argue; offer the alternative your company defines.

## Keep notes and exports lean (our own)

Write what the next colleague needs. Do not copy full ID numbers or payment data into free-text fields, and do not email exports with customer lists unless the approved process says so.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer writes in chat: "Delete everything about me and stop sending me newsletters." The agent logs the wording, passes the case to the privacy team the same day and replies: "Your request has reached our privacy team. They will answer you; you do not need to repeat it." The agent does not say "everything will be deleted", because some data may have to be kept for other reasons, and the team will decide.

## Key takeaways

- Customers can ask for access, erasure and objection; you recognise, record, route and reassure.
- Respond within one month; the clock starts on receipt.
- Identity doubts can be checked before data is given out.
- Follow your company's recording notice; ask the data protection officer about details.
- Keep notes and exports to what is necessary.
