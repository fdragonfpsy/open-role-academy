## Why this matters

Selling without a process means every deal is improvised: you cannot tell where a deal stands, what to do next or why you win or lose. A sales process is a repeatable sequence of steps that guides a prospect from first contact to purchase. A pipeline is the view of all your deals sorted by the step they are in. This unit gives you the vocabulary; it is a generic B2B picture, and your company's stages may carry other names.

## The stages

A widely taught model, described in OpenStax Principles of Marketing, has seven steps: prospecting and qualifying, pre-approach (research), approach (first contact), presentation, handling objections, closing and follow-up. Many companies compress this into fewer stages in their CRM, for example "new", "qualified", "proposal sent", "negotiation", "won" and "lost". The names matter less than two rules:

- each stage describes something that has happened with the customer, not just something you did;
- a deal is in exactly one stage at a time.

## Lead, opportunity, customer

A **lead** is a contact or company that might need your offer. Once someone has a real need, a way to buy and a conversation with you, it becomes an **opportunity** (a deal). When they have bought, they are a **customer**, and the process continues with follow-up, because delivery, satisfaction and repeat business belong to the sale too. The German article on the Vertriebsprozess likewise counts after-sales service and complaint handling as part of the process.

## Pipeline and funnel

Your pipeline lists open deals by stage with their value and expected close date. The "funnel" picture, described in the Purchase funnel article (awareness, interest, desire, action), shows that fewer people reach each later stage. In practice you use the pipeline to see which deals need attention today.

## The next-step habit

Every open deal needs a **next step with a date**: "send the offer on Thursday", "call back after the budget meeting on the 14th". A deal without a next step is at risk of being forgotten. Practice insight: write the next step into the CRM right after each conversation, not at the end of the week.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. You sell online-meeting equipment to small offices. On Monday you list four deals: the lead "Office A" has downloaded a leaflet (stage: new lead); "Office B" asked for a price list and said they need ten headsets by March (qualified); "Office C" received an offer last week (proposal sent) but has not replied; "Office D" ordered (won). For Office C you set the next step "call on Wednesday to ask whether the offer arrived and who decides". For Office A you decide to ask two qualifying questions before investing more time.

## Key takeaways

- A sales process is a repeatable sequence from prospecting to follow-up; a pipeline shows all deals by stage.
- Stages describe what happened with the customer, not only your own activity.
- Lead, opportunity and customer are different states; the work continues after the sale.
- Every open deal needs a dated next step.
