## Why this matters

A quote (or offer) is where the customer first sees a number. Mistakes here are costly: a missing item, a wrong total or a discount nobody approved can lose margin or trust. This unit gives you the basics. It uses an invented example and does not state tax rates or legal requirements.

## What a quote contains

A clear quote typically names: the customer and contact, what is offered (items or services, quantities), unit and total prices, delivery and payment terms, the date of the offer and how long it stays valid, and who at your company is the contact. Refer to your company's template and terms. Context note (Germany/EU): in business-to-business sales, prices are commonly shown net, with value-added tax (VAT) listed separately. Which details are required and which rate applies is a question for your finance or legal team; do not guess.

## How prices are set

The English Wikipedia article on cost-plus pricing describes a price set by adding a fixed percentage, a markup, to the unit cost. The article on value-based pricing describes setting the price by the perceived or estimated value to the customer, not by production cost. Preispolitik (German Wikipedia) names the measures a company uses to influence prices and includes the terms and conditions policy, such as bonuses, discounts and cash discounts. In practice sellers combine both views: cost sets the floor, value and the market set the range.

## Markup and margin

They are not the same:

- **Markup** = profit divided by cost.
- **Margin** = profit divided by price.

Invented example: cost 80, markup 25 percent, price 100, profit 20. Margin = 20 / 100 = 20 percent. A markup of 25 percent therefore gives a margin of 20 percent.

## Discounts and profit

A discount reduces the price, but your cost stays the same. Take the price of 100 with a cost of 80. A ten percent discount gives 90, so profit falls from 20 to 10, half of it. Discounts need a reason, an approval limit and something in return (see negotiation). Never promise a discount above your authority.

## Worked example

A customer asks for 20 licences. You check the price list, confirm the quantity, add a one-year support option as a separate line and set the validity period according to company policy. The total before tax is shown with VAT as a separate line, using the rate your finance team gives you. The customer asks for ten percent off; you check your approval limit, which allows it only against a two-year term. You reply with that condition and note it on the quote.

## Key takeaways

- A quote names customer, items, prices, terms, validity and contact.
- Cost-plus builds on cost; value-based builds on the customer's value.
- Markup is profit over cost; margin is profit over price.
- A discount cuts profit sharply; stay within approvals.
- For tax and legal content, ask finance or legal; do not guess.
