## Why this matters

Sales results depend on what the team knows about customers. The OpenStax chapter defines customer relationship management (CRM) as the means through which companies track, manage and analyse customer interactions. A CRM system is the shared memory of the sales team: who the contacts are, what was discussed, which deals exist and what comes next.

## What goes into a CRM

Basic records are **contacts** and **companies**, **deals** (opportunities) with stage, value and expected close date, and **activities** such as calls, emails and meetings with notes and a next step. The OpenStax chapter distinguishes operational CRM (automating marketing, sales and service), analytical CRM (collecting and analysing data) and collaborative CRM (sharing customer data). As a new seller you mostly work with the operational part.

## Good data habits

- Enter data **soon after** the conversation, while you still remember details.
- Fill the **required fields** completely; a deal without a value or close date is invisible to the forecast.
- Avoid **duplicates**: search before you create a contact or company.
- Always write a **next step with a date** on an open deal.
- Keep notes factual and professional. Customers can ask what is stored about them, so write nothing you could not defend.

## Data protection

In the EU, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies to personal data in a CRM. On EUR-Lex you can read the principles in Article 5(1): data should be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently; collected for specified purposes; limited to what is necessary (data minimisation); accurate and kept up to date; kept no longer than necessary; and processed securely. For you this means: store only what you need for the sales purpose, correct wrong data, do not copy customer lists to private tools and pass requests about data (for example access or erasure) to your data protection contact. Details depend on your company's policies and on legal advice.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. After a call, a seller enters the contact in the CRM. A search shows the company already exists, created by a colleague, so she adds the contact there instead of making a duplicate. She creates a deal with a value, a stage "Qualified", an expected close date and the next step "send quote by Friday". Notes contain what the customer needs, not personal opinions. Later the customer asks for deletion of their data; the seller does not delete anything on her own but forwards the request to the data protection contact, as the company policy says.

## Key takeaways

- A CRM is the shared memory of the team.
- Enter data promptly, completely and without duplicates.
- Every open deal needs a next step with a date.
- Store only necessary personal data; keep it accurate.
- Pass data subject requests to your data protection contact.
