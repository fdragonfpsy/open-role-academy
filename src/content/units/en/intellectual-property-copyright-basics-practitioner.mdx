## Why this matters

Everyday questions are rarely about registering a patent. They are about whether we may use something, who owns what we created and how to share it. This unit gives a method for those questions. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Who is the author, who owns the rights

The German article on Urheberrecht states that, in principle, copyright itself cannot be transferred in Germany; instead, rights of use are granted. It also describes that the author remains the author even if the work was made on commission or in an employment relationship; at most, rights of use pass to the commissioning party. How far rights of use pass in an employment or commission context depends on the contract and the circumstances. Check the contract: it should say what is granted, for which uses, for how long and whether it is exclusive. Other countries handle this differently, so international projects need a check.

## A method for usage questions

1. What is it (text, image, software, logo, data)? 2. Who is the author or rights holder? 3. Is there a licence or contract that covers my use? 4. Does my use fit the licence conditions (commercial or not, changes, attribution, share-alike)? 5. If no licence applies, is there an exception? 6. If still unclear, ask counsel or choose other material. Write down the sources and licence texts you checked.

## Creative Commons conditions

The Creative Commons licences combine conditions: attribution (credit the author), non-commercial (no commercial use), no derivatives (no changes) and share-alike (adaptations under the same licence). The Creative Commons site describes the licences as standard ways to share works legally on stated conditions. Each combination means different permissions. A non-commercial licence is not suitable for a company brochure, and a share-alike licence can require that an adaptation be shared under the same licence. Also consider that the licensor's CC licence only covers what the licensor owns; a work may contain third-party parts.

## Exceptions and limits

The Wikipedia article on limitations and exceptions to copyright describes that laws allow certain uses without permission, for example quotation or private copying, under conditions that differ by country. In Germany and the EU, exceptions are narrow and have conditions; do not treat them as a general right to use. Check the conditions with counsel before relying on one in business.

## Trade secrets and other protection

The Wikipedia article on trade secrets describes them as information that is not generally known, has commercial value because it is secret and is subject to reasonable steps to keep it secret. Confidential customer lists or processes can fall under it. Practical measures are access control, confidentiality clauses and marking sensitive material. Trade secret rules protect against unlawful acquisition and use, not against independent development; details need counsel.

## Record keeping

Keep a record of licences and permissions for materials used in products and publications, including the licence version, source and date. This supports audits and answers to rights holders.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A marketing team wants to translate and shorten a blog post from a partner and publish it on the company website. The post carries a Creative Commons licence with a no-derivatives condition. The team applies the method: the author is the partner's employee; the licence permits sharing but not adaptations; a translation and shortening are changes; no exception obviously applies. The team asks the partner for written permission and meanwhile links to the original post with a short summary in its own words instead of copying the content.

## Key takeaways

- In Germany, authors remain authors; rights of use are granted by contract.
- Check the contract for scope, duration and exclusivity of the rights of use.
- Use a short method: what, who, licence, fit, exception, ask.
- Creative Commons conditions can forbid commercial use or changes.
- Exceptions are narrow and differ by country; trade secrets need reasonable protection steps.
- This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.
