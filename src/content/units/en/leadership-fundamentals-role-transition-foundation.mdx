## Why this matters

Many team leads were promoted because they were very good at the work itself. The new role is different: your results now come mainly through other people. This unit explains the shift at concept level. It is education, not advice for a specific situation.

## From doing to enabling

As an expert you are measured on your own output. As a lead you are also measured on what the team achieves: clear priorities, a workable division of work, people who know what is expected and can ask for help. Your own expert tasks do not disappear at once, but they should no longer crowd out the lead tasks. A common trap is to keep doing the interesting expert work and to treat leadership as something for "spare time".

## Leadership and management

The OpenStax chapter on the nature of leadership defines management as a process of planning, organising, directing and controlling, and leadership as a social influence relationship between people who depend on each other to reach goals. It also notes that effective leaders often need management skills and the other way round. Wikipedia describes leadership as a contested term with many definitions. For a new lead the practical message is: you need both, the structure (plans, rules, follow-up) and the influence (trust, direction, example).

## Role clarity

Role clarity means that everyone knows what they are responsible for and what they are not. Unclear or conflicting expectations can lead to role conflict (the German Wikipedia article on Rollenkonflikt introduces the topic). In the first weeks, clarify:

- **With your manager:** what you are accountable for, which decisions are yours, what success looks like in three to six months.
- **With your team:** how you want to work together, how you will communicate, what they can expect from you.
- **With peers:** who decides what at the boundaries between teams.

## Early credibility

Listen first, ask questions, keep small promises and make few big changes before you understand the context. Be open about what you are still learning.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented person and team. Mira, an expert in a six-person support team, becomes team lead. In week one she meets each team member for 30 minutes and asks what helps them and what gets in their way. She agrees with her manager that she owns the weekly work plan but not the budget. She hands one of her favourite complex tickets to a colleague and spends the freed hour on planning. After a month she asks the team what she should do more or less of.

## Key takeaways

- A lead achieves results mainly through others; own expert output is no longer the main measure.
- Leadership (influence) and management (planning, organising, follow-up) belong together.
- Clarify role, decision rights and expectations early with your manager, team and peers.
- Build credibility through listening, small kept promises and openness about learning.
