## Why this matters

Day to day, HR meets employment law in concrete questions: a request to reduce hours, a contract that should end on a certain date, a new shift model, a complaint about unequal treatment. This unit shows how working time, leave, part-time and fixed-term work, equal treatment, collective agreements and works councils fit together in Germany and the EU, and where you look up the current rules. It contains no day counts, hour limits, thresholds or section numbers on purpose: check the current statute, collective agreement or works agreement. HR staff do not give legal advice; involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Working time and rest

Working time is the period in which an employee has to perform work; according to the German article, breaks usually do not count. The English article notes that many countries regulate the working week by law, for example through rest periods, annual holidays and maximum hours. In Germany there is a dedicated working time statute, and collective agreements can also contain working time rules. The concrete limits belong to the current statute and the applicable collective agreement, not to your memory. Whenever you plan shift models or overtime rules, look up the current text first.

## Leave and sick pay

A statute on annual leave and a statute on continued pay during illness exist in Germany (the German article lists both). What entitlement a person has depends on the statute, the contract and any collective agreement; these can build on each other. Always check all layers and the current wording.

## Part-time and fixed-term work

The German Part-Time and Fixed-Term Employment Act regulates both topics. It implements EU directives and aims to promote part-time work, set the conditions under which fixed-term contracts are allowed and prevent discrimination of part-time and fixed-term employees. As concepts: part-time means working fewer hours than the normal working time; employees can, under conditions set by the statute, request a reduction. A fixed-term contract ends without dismissal when the agreed period or purpose is reached; the statute distinguishes fixed terms with an objective reason from those without. The English article adds that many countries limit fixed-term contracts to protect against job insecurity. The conditions, in particular for repeated fixed terms, are detailed: look them up and ask counsel before extending.

## Equal treatment

Equal treatment is a guiding idea across sources: Germany has a general equal treatment statute, and the part-time and fixed-term statute prohibits disadvantaging those groups. In HR processes this means decisions on hiring, pay, promotion and termination need objective, documentable reasons.

## Collective agreements and works councils

A collective agreement is a written contract negotiated by unions with an employer or employers' association; it regulates terms and conditions of employment. In Germany it is legally binding for those it covers, and how many employees are covered varies strongly by sector. The works council is the elected employee representation in a workplace; its legal basis is the Works Constitution Act. A works agreement is a contract between employer and works council with binding rules for the workforce. If a collective agreement applies, it can limit what works agreements may regulate. So, in each process, ask: does a collective agreement apply, is there a works council, and does it have a say here?

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real figures. An employee asks to work fewer hours. Step 1: classify the request, for example a permanent reduction. Step 2: look up the current statute and check the contract, any collective agreement and any works agreement. Step 3: check whether the works council needs to be informed or has a role. Step 4: if the employer wants to refuse or the case is disputed, give counsel the documents. Step 5: document the request, the reasons and the decision.

## Key takeaways

- Working time, leave and sick pay are regulated by statutes and often supplemented by collective agreements; look up current rules.
- Part-time and fixed-term work are regulated by a dedicated statute implementing EU directives; details are strict.
- Equal treatment requires objective, documented reasons.
- Always ask: which collective agreement, which works agreement, which works council role?
- This is Germany/EU orientation only; HR staff do not give legal advice.
