## Why this matters

A customer asks the same question that a colleague answered last week, or a promise is forgotten because it lived only in someone's head. A ticketing system and a knowledge base prevent both.

## What a ticket is

Customer support requests are frequently managed in ticketing systems, which log each customer issue as a separate record that can be categorised, assigned and tracked to resolution. Customer relationship management (CRM) software likewise tracks and stores contacts such as phone calls, emails, live chats and service requests. Our own rule: nothing is only in your head or inbox; every request gets a ticket.

## Capturing a good ticket (our own sample)

Our own sample set of fields (your system will differ):

- Who: customer name and contact, plus the channel they used.
- What: a one-line title and a description in the customer's own words.
- Facts: order number, product, dates, error messages.
- Category: the topic, such as delivery, invoice or login.
- Next step: who does what by when.

Write so that a colleague can continue without asking the customer again.

## Statuses

Our own simple status set: new, in progress, waiting for customer, solved. Change the status as soon as the situation changes, because the status tells everyone what to do next: "waiting for customer" means the ball is with the customer, so nobody else should chase internally.

## Knowledge base and FAQ

FAQ stands for frequently asked questions: a collection of often asked questions with their answers on a topic. They became known on the internet, where discussion groups made FAQ lists to relieve the forums, and sometimes FAQs are written in advance without waiting for repeated questions. Before you ask a colleague, search the knowledge base with the customer's words and with the product name. If you do not find an answer, note the search terms, so the gap can be filled.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer writes: "I cannot log in since the update." You create a ticket: title "Login fails after update", channel email, description in the customer's words, category Login, status "in progress". A knowledge search for "login update" shows an article with two steps. You send them, set the status to "waiting for customer" and write the next step: "check reply on Thursday".

## Key takeaways

- Every request gets a ticket; nothing lives only in your head or inbox.
- Capture who, what, facts, category and next step.
- Keep the status current; it tells everyone what happens next.
- Search the knowledge base first and note gaps.
