## Why this matters

Every service contact is also a piece of feedback about the company. A team that listens to this feedback and answers it helps customers stay. This unit explains the basic ideas and gives you one habit to use right away.

## Retention and loyalty

Customer retention is the ability of a company to keep its customers over a period, so that they return, keep buying or do not leave for a competitor. Customer loyalty is an ongoing positive relationship between a customer and a business. In general it is easier and less expensive to keep a loyal customer than to win a new one, which is why service and marketing teams care about it.

## Satisfaction is a start, not a guarantee

Satisfaction means that what the customer got matches what they expected. Satisfaction is a strong driver of retention, but the link varies with customer, product and industry, so some satisfied customers still leave. Customers also stay for other reasons, such as the effort of switching: fees for ending a contract, the time it takes to learn a new provider, or personal ties. Programmes that reward purchases, such as bonus cards, can raise retention, but a loyalty that rests only on rewards can be fragile; a lasting loyalty grows from satisfaction.

## Where feedback comes from

You meet feedback in many places:

- Surveys after a contact or purchase, such as a satisfaction rating.
- Free comments in surveys, reviews and social media.
- What customers say in tickets, calls and chats, including complaints.
- Behaviour: returns, cancellations and repeated contacts about the same issue.

Complaints are feedback too: customers who complain give the company a chance to put things right.

## Close the loop (our own habit)

Our own habit has three steps for any feedback that a customer gave you or that you notice:

- Thank: acknowledge the feedback in one sentence.
- Act or explain: fix the case if it is yours, or say honestly what you will pass on and what you cannot change.
- Pass it on: write it down in the place your team collects feedback, with the facts and no personal details that others do not need.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer rates a delivery 3 out of 5 and writes: "Fine, but I was never told that it would come a day later." The agent answers: "Thank you for saying so. You should have been told. I have noted this for our delivery team and I will send you tracking updates for your next order." The agent also adds a line to the team's feedback list: "Late delivery notices missing, 1 more case". The customer is heard, and the team gains a data point.

## Key takeaways

- Retention is keeping customers over time; loyalty is a lasting positive relationship.
- Satisfaction helps retention but does not guarantee it.
- Feedback comes from surveys, comments, tickets and behaviour.
- Close the loop: thank, act or explain, pass it on.
