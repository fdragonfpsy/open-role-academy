## Why this matters

At an advanced level you are no longer only passing data on. You are responsible for the system around payroll: whether the numbers agree, whether pay is structured fairly, whether providers are managed well and whether pay data is protected. This unit teaches the concepts for Germany/EU. It does not give rates, thresholds or legal details; for current legal duties consult legal counsel, the data protection officer and the authorities.

## Reconciling payroll to HR and finance

The German article on the Entgeltabrechnung says the pay statement also leads to the booking of personnel costs. That connects three views of one reality: the **HR view** (who is employed, on what terms), the **payroll view** (what was paid) and the **finance view** (what was booked as personnel cost). A regular reconciliation compares them:

1. Headcount and employment terms in HR against the people paid in the run.
2. Total gross pay of the run against the booked personnel costs, explaining every difference (timing, accruals, corrections).
3. Open items from the previous period, each with an owner.

Differences are traced to the source and fixed there, not smoothed over in one system.

## Pay structures and job grading

A **pay structure** defines the building blocks of pay: base pay, additions, variable pay and benefits. The German article on Arbeitsbewertung (job evaluation) describes procedures that determine the value of a job as the primary parameter for pay differentiation. The aim is to classify different jobs with comparable criteria so that approximately fair pay results; jobs are documented in a job description and rated against requirement types. The article names summary methods (for example assigning jobs to pay groups using standard descriptions) and analytical methods (a catalogue of criteria with weighting), and says such systems are a basic part of pay agreements. If your company is covered by a collective agreement, as described in the English article on collective agreements, the grading and pay groups may follow from it; if not, the company builds its own system. Either way, grading attaches to the job, not to the person.

## Pay transparency and fairness

The German Entgelttransparenzgesetz page states the aim of enforcing equal pay for women and men for equal or equivalent work, and describes instruments such as an individual right to information, company procedures to check equal pay and reporting duties. The page also notes that an EU directive of 2023 requires adjustments of that law. Which employers are covered and which rights apply changes, so do not quote thresholds from memory: check the current law with legal counsel. As principles, you can already work on: documented grading criteria, consistent entry of people into pay groups, and regular checks for unexplained differences.

## Working with providers and tax advisors

The English payroll article notes that payroll can be done in-house or outsourced to a payroll provider, and that legislation complexity is one reason to outsource. The German article adds that payroll is often run by a service provider or tax advisor, especially in small and medium-sized companies. Outsourcing moves the work, not the responsibility. Agree in writing: who delivers which data and by when (the cut-off), who checks results, how errors are reported and fixed, and how pay data is protected. Questions of tax and social insurance go to tax advisors or the provider's specialists.

## Controls

- **Four-eyes principle:** a preventive control in which sensitive steps need agreement by at least two people, reducing risk of errors and misuse. Apply it to changes of bank details, pay and payment files.
- **Access rights:** only people who need pay data for their task may see it; review access regularly (access control).
- **Audit trail:** a record of who changed what and when, so corrections are traceable.
- **Data protection:** pay data is personal data. Involve the data protection officer (DPO) for processing, provider contracts and retention questions.

## Worked example

All figures are invented. The reconciliation shows gross pay of 120000 units in the payroll run and 118000 units booked as personnel cost, so 2000 units are unexplained.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Headcount HR vs. run | One more person paid than active in HR |
| Cause | A leaver entered late; paid 2000 units too much |
| Fix | Correct master data at source, payroll corrects, finance informed |
| Control added | Leaver list compared with the run, four-eyes sign-off |

## Key takeaways

- Reconcile HR, payroll and finance regularly and trace differences to their source.
- Pay structures and job grading make pay explainable; grading attaches to the job.
- Pay transparency and equal pay are principles first; check the current law with counsel.
- Outsourcing payroll does not outsource responsibility.
- Four-eyes, access rights and an audit trail are core controls; involve the DPO for pay data.
