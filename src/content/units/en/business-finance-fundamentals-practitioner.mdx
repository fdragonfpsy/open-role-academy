## Numbers you meet at work

At practitioner level you take part in discussions about budgets, prices, investments and savings. You do not need to be an accountant — but you should be able to read the main financial statements and do a few key calculations yourself.

## Income statement and balance sheet

- The **income statement** (profit and loss statement) shows income and expenses **over a period**, for example a year, and the resulting profit or loss.
- The **balance sheet** is a **snapshot at a reporting date**: what the company owns (**assets**) and how this is financed — through **liabilities** (debts) and **equity** (the owners' capital).

The balance sheet always balances: **assets = liabilities + equity**. A company with assets of 2 million euros and liabilities of 1.4 million euros has equity of 0.6 million euros.

## Contribution margin and break-even

The **contribution margin** per unit is the selling price minus the variable costs per unit. It shows how much each sale contributes to covering fixed costs — and, beyond that, to profit.

Example: price 50 euros, variable costs 30 euros → contribution margin 20 euros. With fixed costs of 40,000 euros a year, the **break-even quantity** is 40,000 / 20 = **2,000 units**. Every unit beyond that adds 20 euros of profit.

## Profit is not cash

Profit and cash flow differ in **timing**. A sale on invoice counts as revenue when it is made, but the money may arrive weeks later. Conversely, an investment is paid at once but spread over several years as an expense. That is why a profitable company can still run short of cash. **Liquidity** — being able to pay bills when they are due — needs its own planning.

## Worked example

The sales team wants to cut the price of a product from 50 to 45 euros to win volume. Mehmet calculates: the contribution margin falls from 20 to 15 euros, so break-even rises from 2,000 to about 2,667 units. Sales would need to grow by a third just to keep the same contribution. The team decides to test the price cut with one customer group first.

## Key takeaways

- Income statement = period; balance sheet = snapshot; assets = liabilities + equity.
- Contribution margin = price − variable costs; break-even = fixed costs / contribution margin.
- Profit and cash differ in timing — watch liquidity separately.
