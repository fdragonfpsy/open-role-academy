## Why this matters

Most invoices match. The time and the risk sit in the few that do not. A practitioner needs a clear rule for each kind of discrepancy, so that nobody argues about who acts, and so that good suppliers are paid on time.

## Kinds of discrepancy

- Quantity: less (or more) was delivered than billed. Owner: the person who confirmed receipt checks the record, the buyer talks to the supplier.
- Price: the invoice price differs from the order. Owner: the buyer, who knows what was agreed.
- Item or terms: wrong article, unit or payment terms. Owner: the buyer, with accounts payable.
- No order: an invoice without a purchase order. Owner: the requester explains why there was none.
- Duplicate: the same invoice twice. Owner: accounts payable.

## Rules (our own suggestions)

- Tolerance: small differences, for example below a fixed amount or percentage set by finance, are released automatically; everything else is blocked. The values are for your company to decide.
- No order, no pay: orders come before delivery; a retrospective order is an exception that is recorded.
- Services have no physical receipt, so the requester confirms the service in the system.
- Always clear a blocked invoice in writing with the supplier, and keep the reference.

## Call-offs and framework contracts

A framework contract regulates only the conditions of the relationship, such as delivery and payment terms, quality and prices; the individual orders under it are concluded separately and refer to the framework. Each call-off order should carry the framework reference, so that the price check can use the agreed price.

## Payment terms and discount deadlines

Payment terms include the due date, the payment period and cash-discount rules. A typical wording is "14 days 2 percent, 30 days net". If the discount deadline passes while an invoice is blocked, the buyer may lose the discount. A discount taken after the deadline is not valid and the supplier can reclaim it. Where a discrepancy is minor, pay the undisputed part on time and clarify the rest.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A purchase order is for 100 units at 12.50 euros (1,250 euros). The warehouse booked 90 units. The invoice bills 100 units at 12.80 euros (1,280 euros). The invoice is blocked for two reasons. First, 10 units were not received: 10 x 12.80 = 128 euros. Second, the price is 0.30 euros too high on the 90 units received: 27 euros. Together 155 euros, which is the difference between 1,280 and the correct 1,125 euros (90 x 12.50). The buyer asks for a corrected invoice of 1,125 euros or a credit note of 155 euros, and tells the supplier the remaining 10 units are still expected. If a discount deadline is near, the 1,125 euros is paid in time.

## Key takeaways

- Classify discrepancies (quantity, price, item or terms, no order, duplicate) and give each an owner.
- Set tolerances and a no-order-no-pay rule, and record every exception.
- Call-off orders refer to the framework contract so that the agreed price can be checked.
- Do not lose discount deadlines because of a blocked invoice: pay the undisputed part on time.
