## Why this matters

Prevention works when rules are concrete and when people trust the reporting channel. This unit covers both sides at concept level. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Gifts and hospitality rules

A workable rule names what is allowed, what needs approval and what is never allowed. Typical elements are: modest value and occasional frequency, no benefits during tenders or negotiations, no cash or cash equivalents, transparency to the manager, and a register of offered and accepted items. Context matters: the same dinner can be harmless in one situation and risky in another. Public officials are usually subject to stricter rules, so check with the compliance function before offering anything to them. Concrete thresholds are for your policy, not for this unit.

## Third parties

Agents, consultants, distributors and other intermediaries can be a channel for improper payments. Typical measures are a risk-based review before engagement (who owns them, do they have relevant experience, any links to officials), a written contract with clear tasks and compliance commitments, payments that match documented services, and ongoing monitoring. Treat unusually high fees, vague services and requests for payments to other countries or accounts as red flags that need an explanation.

## Facilitation payments

The Wikipedia article on facilitation payments describes small payments to officials to speed up routine, non-discretionary actions. It notes that legal treatment differs between countries. Many organisations forbid them in their policy regardless of local practice, because the line to bribery is unclear and the payments are hard to account for. Follow your own policy and escalate demands for such payments to compliance.

## Internal reporting channels

The European Commission page on whistleblower protection describes that Directive (EU) 2019/1937 requires effective channels for confidential reporting internally and externally, proper follow-up of reports and protection against retaliation. The German article on the Hinweisgeberschutzgesetz states that the German act implements this directive and distinguishes internal reporting offices that companies must provide from external reporting offices run by public bodies. Which organisations must set up channels and the exact obligations are matters for the current statute and counsel.

## Running the channel

In practice a channel needs: an easy way to report, a trusted and impartial office, confidentiality of the reporter's identity and of those named, an acknowledgement to the reporter, a fair and documented review, feedback on follow-up within a reasonable time and a clear ban on retaliation such as dismissal, demotion or exclusion. Keep only the data needed and restrict access; data protection rules also apply to report files.

## Handling a report

Receive and log the report, assess whether it is plausible and who must be involved, protect the reporter, investigate fairly with the rights of the accused in mind, decide measures, inform the reporter as far as allowed and close with lessons learned. Avoid trying to find out who the reporter is.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A report comes in through the internal channel: a manager appears to have asked a consultant to invoice for services that were not delivered. The reporting office confirms receipt, logs it under a case number and limits access to two people. It checks the invoices against the contract and finds that no delivery is documented. It involves legal counsel, interviews the people concerned fairly and informs the reporter that the case is being handled. After the review the company ends the engagement and adds an approval step for consultant invoices. The reporter's identity was never shared with the manager.

## Key takeaways

- Gift and hospitality rules need clear what-is-allowed, approval and register elements.
- Third parties need risk-based checks, clear contracts and payments that match services.
- An internal channel needs confidentiality, follow-up and a ban on retaliation.
- The HinSchG implements EU Directive 2019/1937 in Germany; details need the current text.
- Do not try to identify the reporter.
- This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.
