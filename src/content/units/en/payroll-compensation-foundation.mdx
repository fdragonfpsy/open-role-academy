## Why this matters

Pay is the one HR topic that every employee checks every month. A wrong payslip erodes trust quickly, and the cause is often not the calculation but wrong or late data. This unit gives you the concepts you need as a newcomer in HR. It deliberately contains no tax rates, contribution rates, ceilings, allowances or deadlines: they change and differ by case, so always use the current figures from the authorities, your payroll provider or the payroll accountants.

## Gross and net

The English article on payroll describes **gross pay** as the total payment an employee earns before any deductions or taxes are taken out; it can include things like bonuses or holiday and sick pay. **Net pay** is what remains after all required and voluntary deductions. The German article on the Entgeltabrechnung (pay statement) puts it the same way: it consists of all entitlements (gross) and all deductions, and the difference is the net amount.

## What sits between gross and net

For Germany, the pages we cite name two main categories of statutory deductions: **income tax** (Lohnsteuer, plus church tax where it applies) and **social insurance contributions**. German social insurance has five branches: health, long-term care, pension, unemployment and accident insurance. The German article says that contributions are, as a rule, paid in equal parts by employees and employers, with exceptions; accident insurance is borne by the employer alone. For you this means: part of the cost of an employee never appears as a deduction on the payslip, because the employer carries it in addition to the gross pay. Other deductions, such as voluntary ones, can exist too. Which items apply to whom is the job of payroll accountants, not of HR generalists.

## The payslip

A payslip (German: Entgeltabrechnung or Lohn- und Gehaltsabrechnung) is a periodic statement, in text form, of the pay for a period; the interval, usually monthly, is set in the employment contract, a works agreement or a collective agreement. Its purpose, as the German article puts it, is that employees can check the statement and understand why they receive the amount paid. Typical sections are:

- **Identification:** employer and employee, pay period, start of employment.
- **Earnings:** base pay and additions, each listed separately.
- **Deductions:** taxes and social insurance contributions, listed by type.
- **Net amount** and, often, information on employer contributions or leave.

Each country has its own rules on what a payslip must contain; check the current requirements instead of relying on memory.

## What data payroll needs

Payroll can only calculate what it is told. The German article lists data such as the identity data of employer and employee, the start of employment, tax attributes (for example the tax class that exists in Germany), leave data and benefits in kind. In practice, you pass on or maintain, among other things: new hires and their master data, changes to contract or pay, absences, and leavers. The English article adds that payroll software relies on approved data from other tools such as time recording. If data is late or wrong, the payslip is wrong. The **cut-off date** after which changes only count for the next run is set by your company or payroll provider; ask for it and keep to it.

## Confidentiality

Pay data is personal data about an employee, and its handling is covered by employee data protection (Arbeitnehmerdatenschutz). Treat it as need-to-know: share it only with people whose task requires it, and never discuss an individual's pay in corridors, chats or open mailboxes. If someone asks for an explanation of their own payslip, route them to the payroll accountants or the HR lead.

## Worked example

All figures are invented and no real rate is used. Mia's payslip for one month shows:

| Item | Amount (units) |
|---|---|
| Gross pay | 3000 |
| Income tax and church tax (example amount) | 400 |
| Employee share of social insurance contributions (example amount) | 400 |
| Net pay | 2200 |

Check: 3000 - 400 - 400 = 2200. Mia asks why her net pay is lower than the offer letter suggested. You do not explain the tax class or contribution rules yourself. You collect her question, check that her master data in the HR system is complete and correct, and pass the case to the payroll accountants.

## Key takeaways

- Gross pay is before deductions, net pay is what remains afterwards.
- Statutory deductions in Germany include income tax and social insurance contributions; the employer carries additional contribution costs.
- A payslip lets employees verify what they are paid and why.
- Payroll needs accurate master data, changes, absences and leavers, delivered before the cut-off date set by your company or provider.
- Pay data is confidential: need-to-know only.
- HR does not give tax or legal advice; involve payroll accountants or tax advisors. For current rates and rules use official sources.
