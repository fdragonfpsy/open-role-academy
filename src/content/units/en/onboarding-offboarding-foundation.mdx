## Why this matters

People remember how they were welcomed, and how they were sent off. Onboarding and offboarding are the two ends of the employee journey, and HR coordinates both. This unit gives you the basic map. It deliberately contains no probation periods, notice periods, deadlines or section numbers, because these depend on current law, collective agreements, works agreements and the individual case. Check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## Onboarding and offboarding

The English Wikipedia article describes onboarding as the mechanism through which new employees acquire the knowledge, skills and behaviours they need to become effective members of an organisation; outside American English it is often called induction. The German article describes it as the hiring of new staff plus all measures that help their integration, and names offboarding as the counterpart. The English article on offboarding describes it as the separation process when an employee leaves, which may include knowledge transfer, an exit interview, the return of company property and steps from HR, IT or legal functions.

## Preboarding

The German article says onboarding can begin before the contract is signed or the first day arrives: early contact, and preparing the workplace and tools such as computer, badges and team information. This phase is often called preboarding. A short, friendly message with the practical details for day one (where to go, who to ask for, what to bring) already reduces uncertainty.

## The first-day checklist

Turn the idea into a list that you can tick off before the person arrives:

- **Documents**: the paperwork that must be completed, prepared and sorted so that nothing is asked for twice. Which documents are required is a question for current law and your company rules.
- **Equipment**: workstation, laptop, phone or other tools ordered and ready.
- **Access**: accounts, email and building access requested in time, with only the access the role needs.
- **Welcome**: a named contact, an agenda for the day, an introduction to the team and a first lunch or coffee.

The German article on Einarbeitung adds that checklists for managers and a personal welcome are typical measures at the start.

## The buddy idea

The English onboarding article says newcomers learn by asking colleagues and supervisors about the job, norms and procedures, and it notes that a "buddy" the newcomer can ask questions of can matter for getting up to speed. A buddy is a colleague who answers everyday questions and explains unwritten rules. A buddy is not the line manager and does not assess performance. The German article on Einarbeitung points out that the buddy role is debated because it can blur responsibilities, so agree clearly who is responsible for what.

## A respectful exit

The offboarding article recommends treating offboarding as a positive experience and acknowledging the person's contributions. Basics: communicate clearly who does what, return equipment and end access in an orderly way, and treat the person with dignity whatever the reason for leaving. Details of dismissals, deadlines and references are legal topics: check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. "Employee A" will start next week. You send a welcome message with the practical details, ask IT for a laptop and accounts, and ask the manager to name a buddy. On day one the manager greets A, the buddy shows the office and answers questions, and the checklist shows that all steps are done. Months later, "Employee B" leaves: you use a similar list for returning the laptop, ending access and saying goodbye.

## Key takeaways

- Onboarding brings people in; offboarding handles the exit; both are part of one journey.
- Preboarding prepares day one before it happens.
- A first-day checklist covers documents, equipment, access and welcome.
- A buddy helps with everyday questions; the manager stays responsible.
- Exits deserve respect and order. Legal details: check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel.
