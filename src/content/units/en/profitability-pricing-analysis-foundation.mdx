## Why this matters

Controllers are often asked: "Is this product worth selling?" and "How much do we need to sell to cover our costs?" Two tools answer most first questions: the **contribution margin** and the **break-even point**. Both rest on the split between variable and fixed costs from cost accounting.

## Contribution margin

The **contribution margin** (German: Deckungsbeitrag) is revenue minus variable costs. It is what a sale "contributes" towards covering fixed costs and, after that, profit.

- Contribution margin per unit = price per unit - variable cost per unit.
- Contribution margin ratio = contribution margin / revenue.
- Total contribution margin = contribution margin per unit x units sold.

Operating profit = total contribution margin - fixed costs. Note that the contribution margin is not profit: a product with a positive contribution margin can still lose money for the company if the contributions do not cover the fixed costs.

## Break-even point

The **break-even point** (German: Gewinnschwelle) is the sales volume at which profit is zero.

- Break-even units = fixed costs / contribution margin per unit.
- Break-even revenue = fixed costs / contribution margin ratio.

The standard model assumes that price and variable cost per unit stay constant and that fixed costs are fixed over the volume range considered. State these assumptions when you present a result.

## Margin of safety

The **margin of safety** shows how far sales can fall before the company drops below break-even: (actual or planned units - break-even units) / actual or planned units. A small margin of safety means a small drop in sales leads to a loss.

## Margin versus markup

Both compare price and cost, but with different bases. **Margin** = (price - cost) / price. **Markup** = (price - cost) / cost. A product bought for 80 and sold for 100 has a margin of 20 % and a markup of 25 %. Mixing the two is a classic source of pricing errors, so always say which one you mean.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A product sells for 50 EUR and has variable costs of 30 EUR per unit. Fixed costs are 12,000 EUR per month.

- Contribution margin per unit = 50 - 30 = 20 EUR; ratio = 20 / 50 = 40 %.
- Break-even units = 12,000 / 20 = 600 units; break-even revenue = 12,000 / 0.40 = 30,000 EUR.
- At 750 units: profit = 750 x 20 - 12,000 = 3,000 EUR. Margin of safety = (750 - 600) / 750 = 20 %.

## Key takeaways

- Contribution margin = revenue - variable costs; profit = contribution margin - fixed costs.
- Break-even units = fixed costs / contribution margin per unit.
- The margin of safety shows how much sales can fall before a loss.
- Margin is a share of price, markup a share of cost: never mix them up.
