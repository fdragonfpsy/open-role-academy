## Why this matters

When several reports show different numbers for the "same" KPI, management stops trusting all of them. At advanced level, controlling designs the rules that keep reports consistent: who owns a report, how a KPI is defined, where its data comes from and how changes are made.

## Elements of report governance

- **Ownership:** every report and KPI has a named owner who answers questions and approves changes.
- **Definitions:** a KPI dictionary states the formula, the data source, the unit, the sign convention, the update frequency and the exclusions. One definition is used everywhere, or a difference is named explicitly.
- **Lineage:** you can trace a number back through the report, the data model and the ETL steps to the source. Data lineage makes impact analysis possible: if a source field changes, you know which reports are affected.
- **Access:** access rights follow the need to know and are reviewed regularly; the principle of separating duties applies to changes and approvals.
- **Change control:** changes to reports, calculation logic and mappings are requested, tested, approved and documented, and can be rolled back.

## Data quality

Data quality is commonly described by dimensions such as accuracy, completeness, consistency and timeliness (the exact list varies by source). Controls can be built in: validation rules at entry, completeness checks (are all cost centres present?), consistency checks across systems, and monitoring of load times. A data owner for each master data domain, together with a documented process for creating and closing cost centres, prevents many problems at the source. Master data management and data governance describe this as an organisational task, not a one-off clean-up.

## Spreadsheets and manual steps

Many controlling processes still include spreadsheets. They are flexible but prone to hidden errors. Reduce the risk with a clear input and output structure, protected formulas, version naming, a documented owner, a reconciliation to the system and a plan to move recurring manual steps into the system where the effort is justified.

## Personal data in reports

Reports that contain personal data, for example at the level of individual employees, fall under data protection rules. In the EU this includes the GDPR; in Germany, co-determination rights of the works council can also be relevant. Involve data protection and the works council early, and report at an aggregated level where possible.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. Two reports show "gross margin": sales uses revenue minus direct material, controlling uses revenue minus all variable costs. The dispute is solved by a KPI dictionary entry that defines "gross margin" for management reports and renames the sales view "material margin". The change request includes a test of the new definition against last year's figures, sign-off by both owners, and a note in the report footer with the version date.

## Key takeaways

- Governance means owner, definition, lineage, access and change control.
- A KPI dictionary prevents conflicting numbers for the same name.
- Build data quality controls into the process, not only into the clean-up.
- Treat spreadsheets as part of the control environment and handle personal data carefully.
