## Why this matters

A buyer who negotiates the price of every item equally spends time on the wrong things. Strategic sourcing means developing supply at the lowest total cost, not only the lowest purchase price, and it begins with a clear picture of what the company buys.

## Know what you spend

The first step of a sourcing process, as described by several authors, is to assess current spending: what is bought, from whom, at what prices. Then you look at the supply market (who offers what), compare total costs, and only after that develop a strategy and negotiate. Collect purchase data from accounting and the order system, merge duplicate supplier names, and map each line to a category such as steel, packaging, software or travel.

## Categories and the ABC view

A category groups similar goods or services that are bought from similar suppliers, so one team can manage them together. The ABC analysis ranks objects by their contribution and sorts them into three classes. The usual limits are not fixed; each company sets its own percentages.

- A: few categories with a high share of spend. They deserve most attention.
- B: a middle group, managed with standard methods.
- C: many categories with little spend. Keep them simple and cheap to administer.

## Total cost of ownership

Total cost of ownership (TCO) estimates all costs of an asset, not only the purchase price: energy, repair, maintenance and often hidden costs such as training or downtime. There is no single binding standard, so many models exist. Our own rule: write down which cost items you include, and use the same list for every offer.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented numbers. A company spends 4,400 thousand euro per year in eight categories: steel 1,800, packaging 700, software licences 500, marketing services 400, IT hardware 380, cleaning 300, travel 220, office supplies 100. Steel alone is 41 percent. Using our own limits (A up to about 70 percent cumulative), steel, packaging and software are class A (68 percent together), marketing, IT hardware and cleaning are B, travel and office supplies are C. Next, compare two laptops over three years. Model A costs 850 euro plus 320 for repairs and 90 for software, 1,260 in total. Model B costs 980 plus 110 and 90, 1,180 in total. B has the higher price and the lower total cost.

## Key takeaways

- Start sourcing with spend data: what, from whom, at what price.
- Group purchases into categories and sort them into A, B and C by spend; set the limits yourself.
- Put most effort into class A and keep class C simple.
- Compare offers by total cost of ownership with a fixed list of cost items, not by price alone.
