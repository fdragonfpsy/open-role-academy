## Why this matters

Personnel administration is the quiet backbone of HR: the records and routines that make sure the company knows who works for it, under which terms, and when someone is absent. Mistakes here are rarely dramatic, but they travel: a wrong entry can end up in a payroll run, a certificate or a dispute. This unit gives you the map. It deliberately contains no retention periods, leave entitlements, deadlines or section numbers, because these depend on current law, collective agreements, works agreements and the case. Check current law, your data protection officer (DPO), the works agreement or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## What a personnel file is

The German Wikipedia article on the Personalakte describes it as a collection of documents, kept on paper or electronically, that an employer holds about an employee and that contain information about the person and the employment relationship. It adds that, in Germany, neither the form nor the content of personnel files is generally regulated by statute (civil servants are a separate case). The employer decides what goes in, but the file should give a complete, truthful and careful picture of the employment. In practice, this means your job is to keep it accurate and tidy, not to collect everything you can.

## The employee lifecycle at concept level

Think of three phases, each producing documents:

- **Hire**: the contract, the person's details needed to run the employment, and proof of qualifications where the job requires it. The article lists examples such as the application documents, the employment contract with a job description and tax and social insurance documents.
- **Changes during employment**: amendments to the contract, a new role or working pattern, further training records, assessments and records of annual conversations. The article names such items among typical contents.
- **Exit**: documents that close the relationship, such as a termination agreement or notice, a reference, and a record of what was returned or handed over. Employment references are covered in the practitioner unit.

You do not need the legal detail yet. You need to know that every change should have a document behind it, filed in the right place and linked to the right person.

## Accuracy and confidentiality

The article says personnel files are to be treated as strictly confidential and secured against unauthorised access; only those who handle the documents and the employee's supervisors should have access. It also notes the employee's right to inspect their own file, and that unjustified entries can be challenged. Two practical consequences: type names and dates carefully, and never leave files open on a desk or screen. The article also says some information does not belong in the main file, for example lists of illness reasons, and that particularly sensitive material may be kept separately. Ask your DPO what applies in your company.

## Absence types as concepts

The English article on leave of absence describes leave as an authorised absence from work for a reason the workplace accepts. It distinguishes subcategories such as paid holidays, sick leave and unpaid leave, and notes that rules vary by country and are set by law, contracts or agreements. Your task is to record the correct type, because the type decides what happens next. Do not guess pay effects or entitlements from the type; those come from the rules that apply and from payroll.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. A colleague, "Employee A", moves from part-time to a different working pattern. Step 1: the change is requested and agreed in writing. Step 2: you update the master data in the HR system and file the signed amendment in the right section of the file. Step 3: you check the entry once more against the document. Step 4: you tell payroll that personnel data has changed, since payroll relies on it; how pay is calculated is a later topic. Step 5: a manager asks for the person's sick days "just to be informed". You do not answer on your own; you ask your DPO or HR lead what may be shared.

## Key takeaways

- A personnel file collects documents about a person and the employment; keep it accurate, complete and tidy.
- Hire, changes and exit each leave documents; every data change needs a document behind it.
- Personnel files are confidential; share only what is necessary and permitted.
- Record the correct absence type; do not infer pay or entitlements.
- This is orientation for Germany/EU; check current law, your DPO, the works agreement or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.
