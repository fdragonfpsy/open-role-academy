## Why this matters

Many customers start with a search or a link. To plan digital marketing you need to know where visitors come from and which of those sources you pay for.

## Where visitors come from

OpenStax lists the following traffic sources: social media, email, paid search, organic search, paid social and affiliates. To see where a visit came from, marketers attach tracking code to links. Traffic by source tells you which channels bring people to the site.

## Paid and organic search

When someone enters a search term, the search engine shows a list called the search engine results page (SERP). OpenStax explains **paid search**: companies bid on search terms in an auction against competitors and, if the bid is high enough, their ad gets a sponsored position at the top or bottom of the page. **Organic search** is the list of results that has not been paid for; algorithms choose them for relevance to the search terms. A click on an organic result costs the company nothing.

## SEO and SEM

The German Wikipedia article defines search engine optimisation (SEO) as measures that raise the visibility of a website and its content for users of a web search engine. SEO improves unpaid results and excludes direct traffic and bought advertising. It is a sub-area of search engine marketing (SEM), which the article places above SEO. In everyday use, many people use SEM for the paid side; check which meaning a colleague intends.

## How a search engine finds a page

The article describes how early search engines worked: a crawler loads the page, and an indexer reads information from it and catalogues it, for example the words used and links to other pages. A page that cannot be loaded or is not indexed cannot be listed. Search engines then rank pages by relevance to a query.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A small online shop for garden tools checks its traffic by source for a month: most visits come from email, some from organic search, none from paid search. The owner decides to test a small paid search campaign for one product and in parallel to improve the product page text so it answers what people search for. She tracks both separately, so she can compare cost and results.

## Key takeaways

- Traffic sources include social, email, paid and organic search, paid social and affiliates.
- Paid search is bought in an auction; organic results are not paid for.
- SEO targets unpaid visibility; SEM is the wider term.
- A page must be loadable and indexed to be found.
