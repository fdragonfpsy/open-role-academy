## Why this matters

At advanced level the hard part is judgement where scores and registers run out: several risks that interact, thresholds that decide who acts, decisions made with incomplete information, and issues that nobody drives. These are the situations in which a project is won or lost.

## Combined effects

Combinations of several individual risks are often what becomes threatening, which is why company-wide risk management asks for the individual risks to be aggregated into an overall picture. On a project, risks that share a cause (one supplier, one scarce expert, one shared test system) tend to occur together. Our own check: group the register by cause, ask "what if these three happen at once?" and compare the combined exposure with the contingency and the escalation thresholds.

## Appetite and thresholds (our own)

Agree these rules with the sponsor at the start, not in a crisis:

- How much cost or delay the project may absorb before it must escalate.
- A score or money value above which a risk goes to the sponsor.
- Who may release contingency, and up to which amount.
- Which checkpoints are go or no-go decisions, with their criteria.

## Deciding under uncertainty

Managers decide with incomplete information. People cannot grasp every alternative, time is short, and the outcome of an option is unknown until it is chosen. Escalation of commitment adds to this: after investing, we tend to stay with a poor decision. Two of our own rules help. Decide when further waiting costs more than the extra information is worth. Fix the stop or switch criteria in advance, so that the decision is made against a written rule and not against the pull of what has already been invested.

## Handling an issue (our own)

When a risk occurs, work through these steps:

- Move it to the issue log with date and effect.
- Name one owner and a deadline for a solution proposal.
- Escalate automatically if the deadline passes or the effect exceeds a threshold.
- After closing it, update the register: related risks, and new risks created by the fix.

## Culture

The aim of risk management is a culture of handling risk, not a document. One published criticism describes a company whose register listed a reputation risk and yet no action followed. The register was fine; the response was missing.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented project. A data centre move depends on one integrator. The register has three entries: integrator capacity shortage (score 9), loss of the integrator's key person (8) and late delivery (6). Each is below the agreed escalation threshold of 12. Grouped by cause, the exposure is estimated at 45 000 EUR of delay cost against a contingency of 20 000 EUR. The project lead escalates the group. The response is a second integrator on standby. The sponsor agrees a checkpoint criterion in advance: if the integrator has not delivered milestone M2 by 15 June, the project switches to the second one.

## Key takeaways

- Group risks by shared cause and judge their combined exposure.
- Set escalation and contingency thresholds with the sponsor before trouble starts.
- Fix go or no-go criteria in advance to resist escalation of commitment.
- Every issue gets one owner, a deadline and an escalation rule; fixes can create new risks.
