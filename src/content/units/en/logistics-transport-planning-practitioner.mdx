## Why this matters

Practitioners pick the carrier and the delivery terms, and they are asked why a shipment was late. A simple comparison and clear terms save money and arguments.

## Choosing a mode (our own criteria)

- Price per shipment, including fees and surcharges.
- Transit time and how reliable it is.
- Volume and weight of the goods.
- Type of goods and risk of damage.
- Availability at both ends, such as rail or port access.

## Shipping documents

A bill of lading is issued by a carrier and has three main functions: it is a receipt that the goods were loaded, it contains or evidences the terms of the contract of carriage, and it serves as a document of title. In international trade outside the United States, a sea waybill differs because it is not transferable and does not confer title. Which document is used depends on the mode and the contract, so ask your forwarder and Legal in unclear cases.

## Using Incoterms in practice

- Name the clause, the place and the version of the rules in the contract.
- Do not expect the clause to settle price, payment terms or the legal transfer of ownership.
- Check the current rules, because they have been revised several times.

## Delivery performance

Delivery performance is the share of deliveries on time. You can count deliveries, order lines or units, and some firms set a tolerance, for example that early deliveries count as on time. Measuring against the customer's wished date is usually worse than against the first confirmed date. When a supplier reports its own figure, find out how it was measured. If a carrier is late, check the consignment note to see when the goods were taken over, so you know whether supplier or carrier caused it.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. Carrier A charges 420 euros per shipment, carrier B 480 euros, for 50 shipments a month. A delivers 39 on time (78 percent), B 48 (96 percent). Assume each late delivery costs your plant 350 euros in expediting and idle time. A: 50 x 420 + 11 x 350 = 24,850 euros. B: 50 x 480 + 2 x 350 = 24,700 euros. The cheaper price per shipment is not the cheaper carrier once delays are counted.

## Key takeaways

- Compare modes on price, time, reliability, goods and availability.
- A bill of lading is a receipt, evidence of the contract and a document of title; a sea waybill is not transferable.
- Name clause, place and version when you refer to Incoterms.
- Compare carriers on total cost including delays, not only on price per shipment.
