## Why this matters

Every job carries some risk, from slips in an office to stress in a busy team. HR staff are often the first contact when someone reports a hazard, an accident or a health concern. This unit gives a basic map for a Germany/EU setting. It names no specific laws, duties or deadlines: they depend on current law, accident insurance rules, collective agreements and company rules. Check them or ask the responsible safety professionals or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal or medical advice.

## What occupational safety and health covers

The English Wikipedia article on occupational safety and health describes a field concerned with the safety, health and welfare of people at work. The German article on Arbeitsschutz describes measures that protect employees from work-related accidents and health damage. Practice insight: it covers more than helmets and fire exits. It includes workplace design, work organisation, working hours, equipment, training and mental strain.

## Hazard and risk

The Risk assessment article describes identifying hazards, the likelihood that they cause harm and the consequences. A **hazard** is something that can cause harm, for example a cable across a walkway, a noisy machine or constant time pressure. A **risk** combines how likely harm is with how serious it would be. A cable in a rarely used corridor and one in a busy corridor are the same hazard, but the risk differs.

## Physical and psychological hazards

Physical hazards are for example falls, noise, lifting or poorly set up screens. Psychological hazards are for example very high demands, little control over one's work or missing support. The Occupational stress article describes these as typical stress factors at work.

## Wellbeing is not the same as safety

The Workplace wellness article describes programmes that support employees' health, such as exercise or health information. Practice insight: such offers are useful, but they do not replace safe working conditions.

## What you can do

- Take every report seriously, note the facts and pass them to the responsible person.
- Do not judge alone whether a situation is dangerous.
- Treat health information as sensitive and share it only as needed.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. A colleague in the office tells you that a power strip under a desk gets warm and that people trip over a cable. You thank them, note where and what, and avoid blame. You tell the responsible person for facility or workplace safety the same day and ask what the next step is. You tell your colleague that you passed it on and that they should not use that strip until it is checked.

## Key takeaways

- Occupational safety and health protects people from accidents and health damage at work, including mental strain.
- A hazard can cause harm; a risk combines likelihood and severity.
- Wellness offers add to, but do not replace, safe working conditions.
- Report concerns quickly to the responsible person and treat health data as sensitive.
- Legal details: check current law, your collective agreement or experts.
