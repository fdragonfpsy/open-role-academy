## Why this matters

The evaluation is where an RFx is won or lost. If the criteria were fixed in advance and applied consistently, you can explain the result to the losing bidders and to your own management. If they were adjusted after the offers arrived, you cannot.

## Evaluating offers (our own method)

- Publish the criteria and weights in the request, before offers arrive.
- Score each criterion on a written scale; each evaluator scores alone first, then the team discusses differences.
- Score price with a formula known in advance. A common choice: the lowest compliant price gets 100 points, every other offer gets lowest price divided by its price, times 100.
- Check price on total cost, not unit price only: delivery, installation, training, running costs and end of life belong in the comparison. The EU directive on public procurement also names a cost-effectiveness approach such as life-cycle costing.
- Record the reasons for every score, so that the file explains the decision later.

## Clarifications and equal treatment

Answer bidders' questions in writing and send every question and answer to all bidders, without naming the asker. Do not let one bidder improve its offer while the others cannot. Where you invite revised offers, give every remaining bidder the same chance; the request for proposal can include a best-and-final-offer round for that purpose.

## Building blocks of the contract

Our own checklist: parties and scope; price and payment terms; delivery dates and acceptance; quality and warranty; liability; change requests; confidentiality and data protection; duration, notice and termination. A framework contract adds quantities or budget ceilings and the rule for individual call-off orders. Legal counsel reviews the wording; you check that it matches what the offer promised.

## Public procurement at topic level

In EU public procurement, the contract is awarded to the most economically advantageous tender. That is judged on price or cost and may include the best price-quality ratio, assessed with criteria linked to the subject matter, such as quality, delivery conditions or after-sales service. Open and restricted procedures exist as standard routes, and framework agreements are a recognised instrument. Which procedure, thresholds and national rules apply to a case is a question for legal counsel; this unit gives no numbers.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented bidders. A company evaluates three RFP offers with weights price 40, quality 35, support 25. Prices are 120,000, 132,000 and 150,000 euros, which gives price scores of 100, 90.9 and 80. Quality scores are 70, 85 and 90; support scores are 60, 80 and 90. Totals: bidder A 79.5, bidder B 86.1, bidder C 86.0. The gap between B and C is 0.1 points, which is inside the margin of judgement. The team does not declare B the winner mechanically: it re-reads the evidence behind the quality scores, asks both a clarifying question in writing, and records the reasoning before deciding.

## Key takeaways

- Fix criteria, weights and the price formula before offers arrive; document each score.
- Compare on total cost, not unit price only.
- Send questions and answers to all bidders to keep equal treatment.
- A contract checklist covers scope, price, delivery, quality, liability, changes and termination; legal counsel reviews it.
- EU public buyers award on the most economically advantageous tender; ask legal counsel which rules apply.
