## Why this matters

"Good service" is too vague to improve. Two compact models give you words to describe what customers judge and where service goes wrong.

## Five dimensions customers use

OpenStax presents the RATER framework from Zeithaml, Parasuraman and Berry. **Reliability** is delivering what was promised, accurately and on time. **Assurance** is inspiring trust, for example by communicating expertise. **Tangibles** are the visible environment: facilities, staff appearance, equipment; in support this extends to how the portal, the emails and the forms look and work. **Empathy** is attentiveness that makes customers feel valued, not only efficient task handling. **Responsiveness** is promptness and visible willingness to help, such as short waits and no maze of phone menus. Use the five words as a checklist when you review a contact.

## Five gaps between expectation and experience

The gap model locates the cause. The knowledge gap: the company does not know what customers expect. The policy gap: standards are vague, so staff cannot act on them; OpenStax's example is a rule to answer "quickly" without saying how many rings. The delivery gap: standards exist but are not met, for instance because of training or staffing. The communication gap: marketing promises more than service delivers. The customer gap: the customer's perception differs from the experience. When a complaint pattern appears, ask which gap it points to before choosing a fix.

## Customer journey and measures

A customer journey is the sequence of contacts a customer has while pursuing a goal, for example buying, installing, asking and returning. This is a common working concept; draw it as a line of touchpoints and mark where customers wait, repeat information or switch channel. Those are the risky points. Measures complement each other: customer surveys and the Net Promoter Score are named as the two main ways of gathering feedback, and retention is a common indicator of good service. Each measure is a perception or an outcome, not a cause. Combine them with the gap analysis. Our own caution: first-contact resolution only helps if you also check that the customer's problem stayed solved.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A utility's survey shows low scores for "I was understood" although replies are fast. Responsiveness is fine, empathy is not. The team reviews ten transcripts and finds agents answer the first sentence of a message and ignore the rest: a delivery gap, since the standard asks to address every question. They add a checklist step before sending and track repeat contacts on the same case.

## Key takeaways

- RATER: reliability, assurance, tangibles, empathy, responsiveness.
- Name the gap (knowledge, policy, delivery, communication, customer) before you fix.
- Draw the journey and mark waiting, repetition and channel switches.
- Measures show perception or outcome; combine them and check that solutions hold.
