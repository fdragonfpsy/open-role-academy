## Why this matters

Goods that are ordered but not delivered on time do not help anyone. Purchasing is responsible for the result, even when someone else moves the goods, so you need to know how transport is organised and what to check.

## The three core processes

- Transport: moving goods from one place to another, bridging space and time.
- Storage: bridging time only.
- Handling: transferring goods between vehicles, stores and locations.

## Decisions before a transport

- Route: by which way the goods travel.
- Means of transport: road, rail, water or air.
- Transport company: who carries the goods.
- Transport chain: how several legs are linked.

## Forwarder, carrier and freight contract

A freight forwarder (Spedition) organises the transport and often buys it from carriers (Frachtführer). Under German commercial law (HGB, section 407), a freight contract obliges the carrier to carry the goods to the destination and deliver them to the consignee, and the sender pays the agreed freight. This applies to carriage by land, inland waterway or air as part of a commercial business; for questions about liability, ask Legal. The consignment note (Frachtbrief) accompanies the goods and certifies that the carrier took over the specified goods.

## Delivery terms and delivery performance

Incoterms are voluntary standard clauses of the International Chamber of Commerce that say who arranges transport and who bears costs and risk. They do not fix the price. The rules are revised from time to time, so check the current rules and name the version in the contract. Delivery performance (Liefertreue) is the number of deliveries on time divided by all deliveries; some companies allow a tolerance, and it makes a difference whether you measure against the customer's wished date or the first confirmed date.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A supplier makes 40 deliveries in a month. 34 arrive by the first confirmed date, so delivery performance is 34 / 40 = 85 percent. Against the dates your team originally wished, only 30 arrive in time: 30 / 40 = 75 percent. Both figures are correct, they answer different questions, so agree with the supplier which one you track.

## Key takeaways

- Logistics means transport, storage and handling.
- A forwarder organises, a carrier carries; the freight contract and the consignment note document it.
- Incoterms allocate duties, costs and risk; check the current rules and do not assume they set price.
- State which date and which count you use when you report delivery performance.
