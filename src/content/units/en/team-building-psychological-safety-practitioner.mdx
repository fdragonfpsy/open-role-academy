## Why this matters

Norms and safety are shaped in daily moments: how a meeting starts, how a mistake is received, who gets to speak. This unit moves from concepts to leadership habits.

## Team norms

The German article on Gruppennorm describes group norms as social norms that can be stated explicitly or apply tacitly, like an unwritten rule. Teams always have norms; the question is whether they are chosen. Make them explicit in a short workshop: How do we make decisions? How fast do we reply to messages? How do we give each other feedback? What do we do when we disagree? Write 5 to 7 agreements in the team's own words, post them where the team works and review them every few months or when the team changes. Norms you set alone stay your rules, so involve the team.

## Behaviours that support psychological safety

The Wikipedia article describes psychological safety as a group-level phenomenon and mentions that trust matters as an antecedent. It also refers to work by Amy Edmondson, among others, on psychological safety in teams. Concrete lead behaviours (practical suggestions, not research results):

- Ask for input and for dissent, for example "What am I missing?"
- Respond to bad news calmly and with thanks first, questions second.
- Admit your own mistakes openly.
- Distinguish learning from mistakes from carelessness without public blame.
- Make sure quieter people get space, for example by asking for written input first.

## Guiding through storming

The Tuckman article describes storming as the stage where opinions clash and roles and status are negotiated. Conflict is not a failure of the team. Name the tension, return to the shared goal, clarify roles and decision rights, and let people argue about the issue without attacking the person.

## Beware of silent norming

The same article warns that in norming, members may be so focused on preventing conflict that they hold back controversial ideas. A calm team is not necessarily a safe one. Invite a devil's advocate or ask "What could go wrong?" before decisions.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. Sven's team has agreed on five working agreements. In a review meeting Sven notices that nobody objects to a plan, which is unusual. He says: "I would like to hear two reasons this might fail." After a pause, Anna names a risk nobody mentioned before. Sven thanks her and adds the risk to the plan. Later, when Murat reports a missed deadline early, Sven asks what support would help and records the lesson for the team.

## Key takeaways

- Make team norms explicit and develop them with the team.
- Safety is built by reactions: thanks for bad news, open handling of mistakes, space for quiet voices.
- Storming is normal; use it to clarify roles and decision rights.
- Silence is not agreement; invite dissent actively.
