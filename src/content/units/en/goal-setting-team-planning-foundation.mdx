## Why this matters

A team without clear goals tends to work on whatever is loudest. Goal setting turns the organisation's priorities into a few things the team can aim at and check. This unit covers the basics at concept level. It is education, not a prescribed method.

## What a goal does

The Wikipedia article on goal setting introduces the idea of setting goals to guide effort. The OpenStax chapter on goals or outcome statements distinguishes official goals (the stated aims of an organisation) from operational goals (what is actually pursued) and describes how goals work together in a hierarchy. For a lead, the practical question is: how does my team's goal support the goal of the next level above?

## SMART goals

SMART is a common checklist for formulating goals. The articles on SMART criteria describe the letters (specific, measurable, and so on); the exact words for some letters vary between sources, so check the article you rely on. A goal is more useful when you can tell whether it was reached. Compare "improve customer satisfaction" with "reduce the average first reply time to under one working day by the end of the quarter".

Check each goal: Is it clear what is meant? Can we measure or observe it? Is it realistic given our resources? Does it matter for the organisation? Is there a date?

## Fewer goals, clear priorities

Teams rarely manage many goals at once. Choose a small number and say which comes first. The German article on the Eisenhower principle describes sorting tasks by urgency and importance, a simple way to see which work is urgent and which is important. When everything is "top priority", nothing is. If a new request arrives, ask what it replaces.

## Involving the team

Goals are more likely to be followed when people understand why they matter and can influence how to reach them. Explain the reasoning and invite questions. Write the goals down where everyone can see them, and look at them regularly.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. A team of six answers customer e-mails. The department aims to improve service. The lead, Noor, proposes a team goal: first reply within one working day for 90 percent of e-mails by the end of the quarter. She shares it in the team meeting, asks what stands in the way and adds a second goal, updating the help texts, as secondary. When a request for a new report arrives, she tells the requester that this would delay the reply goal and asks for a decision on priority.

## Key takeaways

- Team goals translate organisational priorities into things you can aim at and check.
- SMART is a checklist for clear goals; check the wording of the letters in the source you use.
- Few goals with a clear order beat many equal ones.
- Explain the reasons for goals and write them down where the team can see them.
