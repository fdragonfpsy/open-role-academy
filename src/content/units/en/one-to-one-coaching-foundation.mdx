## Why this matters

A one-to-one is a conversation between you and one team member. It is the place where the person can raise topics that do not fit into team meetings. This unit covers the basics at concept level.

## Purpose and rhythm

A status meeting answers "where are we on the tasks?". A one-to-one is about the person: how they are doing, what blocks them, what they want to learn. The German Wikipedia article on the Mitarbeitergespräch describes such conversations between lead and employee as an instrument held regularly or on a specific occasion, covering topics such as goals, development and feedback. Pick a rhythm you can keep, and keep it. Cancelling often tells the person they are not a priority.

## A simple structure

1. **Open:** ask how the person is, and let them name their topics first.
2. **Their topics:** this is their time, so their agenda comes before yours.
3. **Your topics:** progress, priorities, feedback you want to give.
4. **Development:** what they want to learn or try next.
5. **Close:** summarise the agreed actions, who does what and by when.

Write down the actions and review them at the start of the next conversation.

## Listen actively

The Wikipedia article on active listening names paraphrasing (saying the message back in your own words), reflecting the feeling you notice, and asking open questions as basic techniques. It also lists minimising distractions, eye contact and asking for clarification. Put the phone away and let pauses happen. The article notes that most people overestimate their ability to listen actively, so practise on purpose.

## Ask open questions

An open question needs a longer answer than yes or no, while a closed question can be answered briefly. German sources call this Fragetechnik: using open and closed questions to guide a conversation. "How is the new process working for you?" opens more than "Is the new process OK?". Use closed questions to confirm facts, for example "Is Friday still the deadline?".

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. Mira leads a customer-support team and meets Jonas every second week for 30 minutes. She starts with "What would you like to talk about?" Jonas says he feels unsure about handling angry callers. Mira paraphrases: "So the tone of the calls is what drains you, not the volume?" Jonas nods and adds that he would like to listen in on a colleague's calls. Mira asks "What would help you most?" and they agree that Jonas shadows two calls by Friday. Mira writes the action down and opens the next conversation with it.

## Key takeaways

- A one-to-one is about the person, not just the task status.
- Keep a regular rhythm and let the other person set topics first.
- Paraphrase, reflect and ask open questions instead of rushing to advice.
- End with written actions and follow up on them next time.
