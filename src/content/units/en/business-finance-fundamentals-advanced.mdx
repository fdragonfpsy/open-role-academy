## From reading numbers to steering with them

At advanced level you own budgets, propose investments and set targets for teams. Financial understanding becomes a management tool: choosing what to measure, deciding between options and anticipating the cash effects of your decisions.

## KPIs that help

**Key performance indicators (KPIs)** make progress towards goals visible. Useful KPIs are:

- **linked to a clear goal** and few in number;
- **precisely defined** — how is it calculated, from which data, how often?
- **balanced** — a speed measure such as "tickets closed per hour" can be improved at the expense of quality, so combine it with a quality measure such as customer satisfaction or reopen rate;
- **reviewed regularly** — does the KPI still drive the right behaviour?

When a measure becomes a target, people optimise the measure. Design KPIs with that in mind.

## Business cases

A **business case** supports a decision between options. A sound one contains:

- **costs** — one-off and recurring, including internal effort;
- **benefits** — with the **assumptions** behind them made explicit;
- **risks** and what happens if assumptions turn out wrong;
- **alternatives**, including **"do nothing"**.

Challenge your own business case: which assumption, if wrong, would change the decision? Vendor figures are a starting point, not evidence.

## Decisions with contribution margins and cash

If a product line shows a loss only because fixed costs were allocated to it, dropping it can make the overall result worse: as long as those fixed costs remain, every positive contribution margin helps to cover them. Long-term decisions additionally ask whether fixed costs can be reduced.

Growth also has a cash side. If customers pay after 60 days but suppliers must be paid within 14, every additional sale ties up cash first. Even profitable, fast-growing companies can run out of money, so payment terms and liquidity planning belong in growth decisions.

## Worked example

Daniel proposes a new customer portal. His business case compares three options — build, buy, do nothing — with five-year costs, expected savings in support time and the key assumption (30 % fewer calls). A sensitivity check shows that the investment still pays off at 15 %. The management board approves the "buy" option with a review after six months.

## Key takeaways

- Few, goal-linked, precisely defined and balanced KPIs.
- Business case: costs, benefits, assumptions, risks, alternatives (incl. do nothing).
- Positive contribution margins cover fixed costs; check before cutting products.
- Plan the cash effects of growth.
