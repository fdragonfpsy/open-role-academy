## Why this matters

At an advanced level the question is no longer how to calculate a reorder point but whether the policy behind it is sensible. Every rule trades availability against money, and the figures you report can mislead if you read them in isolation.

## Service level against capital

A higher safety stock reduces the chance of a stock-out but raises the money tied up and the risk of obsolescence. ABC classes are built on value, which does not equal importance: a cheap C item can stop a production line. Our own rule: classify by value first, then mark critical items and give them their own policy, such as a higher safety stock or a second supplier.

## Reading turnover with care

- Compare like with like: the same item group, the same period, the same method of averaging.
- Turnover can be calculated for the whole warehouse, parts of it or single products; an overall figure hides slow movers.
- A high value is not automatically good, since it may come with stock-outs; a low value is not automatically bad.

## Tracing count differences

- Booking in the wrong unit or on the wrong item number.
- Goods receipts posted late or not at all.
- Issues taken from the shelf but never booked.
- Real loss or damage, which you conclude only after the process causes are excluded.

## The annual Inventur in Germany

Germany only: the HGB requires an inventory at the end of the financial year, and besides the stocktaking on a key date there are simplified methods, for example sampling or continuous inventory. Count differences are investigated and then corrected through the profit and loss account. Which method applies, and how, is a matter for Finance and the auditors; bring them clean data from your side.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. For a product group the cost of goods sold is 900,000 euros a year. Last year the average inventory was 150,000 euros, a turnover of 6 (about 61 days). This year it is 225,000 euros, a turnover of 4 (about 91 days), so 75,000 euros more capital is tied up, while the service level stayed at 98 percent. Before cutting stock, the team asks why: it finds a few slow movers from a discontinued line and a supplier minimum order. Only those are fixed; the safety stock stays.

## Key takeaways

- Safety stock is a trade-off between availability and money, set per item class.
- Value classes do not show criticality; mark critical items separately.
- Turnover is a question to investigate, not a verdict.
- Trace count differences through the process before assuming loss.
