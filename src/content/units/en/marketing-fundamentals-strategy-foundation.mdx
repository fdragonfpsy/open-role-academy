## Why this matters

Many people think marketing means advertising. In practice, advertising is only one tool. Marketing starts earlier: it asks who the customers are, what they need, and what the company should offer, at what price, where and with which message. This unit gives you the basic vocabulary you need before you plan anything.

## The marketing mix

The classic model is the marketing mix, also called the four Ps. OpenStax describes it as the tactics a company can use to promote its products or services in order to influence people to buy:

- **Product**: the good or service, including its features and quality.
- **Price**: what the customer pays in exchange.
- **Place**: where and how the product can be bought.
- **Promotion**: advertising, sales and other communication that attract customers.

The four Ps only work together. A premium product with a bargain price sends mixed signals; a great product that nobody can find gets no sales. The German-language Wikipedia article uses the terms Produkt-, Preis-, Vertriebs- und Kommunikationspolitik for the same four instruments and says the mix turns strategies and plans into concrete actions.

## From business goal to marketing goal

Marketing does not set goals in a vacuum. The company has business goals, for example more revenue from existing customers. Marketing translates this into goals it can influence, for example more repeat purchases or higher awareness in a defined group. A useful marketing goal is specific, has a number and a date, and you can say how you will measure it.

## Marketing, advertising, sales

- Marketing: understanding the market and shaping the offer and its communication.
- Advertising: paid communication, one part of promotion.
- Sales: the personal contact that closes a deal, often supported by marketing material.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A small bakery in a German town wants to grow its weekday revenue. Marketing breaks this down. Product: add a lunch sandwich range for office workers. Price: a lunch combination of sandwich and drink at one clear price. Place: a pre-order option so people can collect it quickly. Promotion: a flyer in nearby offices. The marketing goal becomes: 40 pre-orders per weekday after three months, counted in the till system. Notice how each P supports the same goal.

## Key takeaways

- Marketing is more than advertising: it covers product, price, place and promotion.
- The four Ps must fit together and fit the customer.
- Marketing goals are derived from business goals and must be measurable.
- Always say who the customer is before choosing tactics.
