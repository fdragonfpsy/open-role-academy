## Why this matters

At an advanced level, HR is rarely asked what a rule says, but how several rules fit together and when a case is too risky to handle alone. This unit covers how the layers of employment law in Germany and the EU interact, which situations should go to legal counsel, how to document decisions and how to keep up with changes. It contains no deadlines, thresholds, amounts or section numbers on purpose. HR staff do not give legal advice; your role is to spot issues, prepare the facts and involve legal counsel in time.

## How the layers interact

The German article lists the layers: EU law, statutes, collective agreements, works agreements and individual contracts, and notes that the contract is embedded in this system. Some principles from the cited articles help you reason. First, collective agreements and works agreements have binding effect on the employment relationships they cover. Second, terms in the individual contract that are more favourable for the employee take precedence over a works agreement (the favourability principle). Third, matters that a statute or collective agreement already regulates, or that are usually regulated in collective agreements, generally cannot be regulated by a works agreement unless the collective agreement explicitly allows it. Fourth, EU directives are implemented through national statutes, as with the part-time and fixed-term statute. Treat these as orientation; the precise rules and exceptions are for counsel.

## Institutions: works council and collective bargaining

The works council has legally defined rights; the Works Constitution Act is its basis, and further rights arise from other statutes, for example in connection with dismissals. In case of disagreement on certain matters, a conciliation board can decide. For workplace changes, a works council can, under legally defined circumstances, demand a social plan. Public-sector bodies have staff councils; church employers have their own representation. EU-level works councils exist for transnational companies. For HR this means: before any measure affecting several people, check who must be informed or consulted.

## When to involve legal counsel

As a rule of thumb for this course (not a legal test): involve counsel early if a case concerns termination of employment, protection against dismissal, restructuring or workplace changes affecting several employees, changes to contract terms, transfers between employers or locations, suspected discrimination, or any dispute with the works council or union. Also involve counsel whenever sources conflict, the facts are unusual or a decision is hard to reverse. Prepare a short fact sheet: parties, dates, documents, applicable agreements and what has already been said or done. Do not tell employees what their legal position is; say that the matter is being reviewed.

## Documenting HR decisions

Good documentation protects employees and the company. Record the facts, the rule or agreement you relied on (with version), who decided, which bodies were informed, and the reasons. Keep documents objective and free of speculation. This supports equal treatment, because you can show that comparable cases were handled consistently.

## Keeping up with change

Laws are amended: the German article says the Works Constitution Act has been revised several times. Use official statute texts in their current version, subscribe to updates from your employers' association or counsel, review templates and standard clauses regularly and log the date each source was last checked.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real figures. A department plans to reorganise and move tasks to another site. A manager asks you to "just send the letters". You do not decide the legal route. You collect the contracts, check applicable collective and works agreements, note which employees are affected, check whether the works council must be involved, and send counsel a fact sheet. Meanwhile you tell the manager that a legal review comes first and that nothing should be communicated to staff before then. You document each step and date.

## Key takeaways

- The layers (EU law, statute, collective agreement, works agreement, contract) interact; the favourable individual term and collective precedence principles matter.
- Works councils and unions have defined roles; check who must be involved before acting.
- Dismissal, restructuring, contract changes, transfers and disputes are typical triggers to involve counsel.
- Document facts, sources and reasons; keep them objective.
- Track law changes through official sources; HR staff do not give legal advice.
