## Why this matters

Law changes. An answer that was correct last year can be wrong today, so research is only useful when you know how current your source is. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Currency and validity

Before relying on a text, ask four questions: Is this the original publication or a later version? Has it been amended or replaced? Does it apply to our situation, region and date? Who published it? EUR-Lex offers original acts in the Official Journal as well as other versions of texts; read the site's own explanations to see how each kind of version is meant to be used. Where precise wording matters, go back to the official publication. For case law, the Wikipedia article on case law reminds us that court decisions interpret the texts, and that later decisions can refine earlier ones.

## How EU law arrives

The Commission's pages on the law-making process give an overview of how EU laws are planned, proposed and adopted. For monitoring this means that a topic is visible long before it becomes binding: proposals, discussions and adoption are separate stages. Note which stage a development is in; a proposal is not yet law.

## A simple monitoring routine

- **Scope:** list the laws and topics that matter for your organisation, per country or region.
- **Sources:** pick a few official channels (the Official Journal, official ministry or agency pages) rather than many random ones.
- **Rhythm:** check on a fixed schedule and record the date of each check.
- **Triage:** for each change write what it is, whether it applies, who must be told, and a next step.
- **Owner:** name one person per topic, so that nothing is "everyone's job".

## Research log

Record question, date, sources with links, version or publication date read, findings, open points and who reviewed the result. A log lets a colleague repeat the work and shows later why a decision was made.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An invented online shop monitors two topics: advertising rules and data protection. Every other Monday, the compliance analyst opens the agreed official pages and notes the date. One entry reads: "EU proposal on topic X: still at proposal stage, not binding; owner: Legal; check again in the next cycle." Nothing is changed in shop processes yet, but the entry exists, so the topic does not get lost.

## Key takeaways

- Always establish which version of a source you read and whether it is current.
- Proposal, adoption and application are different stages; record the stage.
- A fixed routine, named owners and triage notes turn monitoring into a process.
- A research log makes your work reproducible and reviewable.
