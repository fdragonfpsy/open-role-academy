## Why this matters

A negotiation is mostly decided before the meeting. Buyers who know what they need, what they can accept and what they do if the talks fail negotiate calmly and get better outcomes than buyers who improvise and fixate on the unit price.

## Interests and positions

A position is what someone demands, such as "a price of 3.90". An interest is the reason behind it, such as "stay within the budget" or "no stock-outs". The Harvard approach advises you to focus on interests instead of positions, and to treat the people separately from the problem. Ask yourself what each side really needs; that is where trades become possible.

## Your alternative and your limits

Before the talks, know your minimum goal. The Harvard approach suggests orienting it on your best alternative if the negotiation fails, known as BATNA, the next-best alternative. Our own way to write it down: three numbers for each important term.

- Target: what you hope to reach.
- Acceptable limit: the worst deal you would still sign.
- Walk-away point: where the alternative becomes better. Never accept a deal beyond it.

Think about the supplier's alternatives too; they shape how much room it has.

## More than price

Payment terms are a good example: they include the payment method, the due date, the payment period, and cash-discount and rebate rules, and either side may set them or they are negotiated. Further terms for the list: delivery time and reliability, quality and warranty, minimum order quantities, how long the price stays fixed, and service level.

## Four stages

A negotiation commonly runs through these stages: building the relationship in a non-task phase, exchanging information, influencing and persuading, and closing. How long each stage lasts differs between cultures. Do not skip the first two; they make the third one easier.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company and prices. A company buys 10,000 reams of paper a year. Target 3.90 euros per ream (39,000 euros), acceptable limit 4.05 (40,500). A second supplier offers 4.10 delivered, so the alternative costs 41,000; the buyer sets its limit at 4.05 because switching takes effort. The first supplier opens at 4.15. Instead of arguing about price alone, the buyer asks what the supplier needs and learns that it wants a firm annual volume. In exchange for a volume commitment the buyer asks for 4.05, a payment period of 45 days and a fixed price for 12 months. The deal closes at 40,500 euros, below the alternative.

## Key takeaways

- Prepare: interests, alternative, target, acceptable limit and walk-away point.
- Interests, not positions, open room for trades.
- Price is one term among many: payment, delivery, quality, volume and price fixing.
- Plan time for relationship and information stages before you persuade.
