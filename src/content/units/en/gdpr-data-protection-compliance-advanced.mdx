## Why this matters

As a lead you organise data protection so that it works under pressure: new projects, incidents, audits. This unit covers the management instruments of the GDPR at concept level. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Data protection impact assessment

Where processing is likely to result in a high risk to people's rights and freedoms, the regulation requires an impact assessment (DPIA) before the processing starts. At concept level it contains a description of the processing and its purposes, an assessment of necessity and proportionality, an assessment of the risks to the persons, and the measures planned to address them. Supervisory authorities publish lists of processing types for which a DPIA is required; consult your authority's current lists rather than relying on memory. Where residual risk stays high, the regulation provides for consulting the authority.

## Handling a personal data breach

A breach is a security incident leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of or access to personal data. Stages, in concept: detect and contain; assess the risk to persons; decide on notification of the supervisory authority and of the affected persons according to the regulation (check the conditions and deadlines in the text itself); document every breach, including those not reported; learn and improve. Agree beforehand who decides and who is reachable, because clocks can start before you know all the facts.

## The data protection officer

The regulation requires a data protection officer (DPO) in certain situations, and German law adds conditions of its own (see the German article on the Datenschutzbeauftragter and the BDSG article). The DPO advises, monitors compliance, cooperates with the authority and acts as contact point. The role must be able to act independently and should not be given tasks that cause a conflict of interest. The organisation stays responsible for compliance; the DPO does not take over the controller's duties.

## Supervisory authorities

Independent authorities supervise application of the GDPR. In Germany the competent authority depends on the organisation and the federal state, so identify yours before an incident. Authorities can investigate, issue orders and impose sanctions; the regulation text governs the details.

## Joint controllers and transfers

Where two parties jointly decide purposes and means, they are joint controllers and must agree how to share duties. Transfers of personal data to countries outside the EU/EEA need an additional legal mechanism; do not assume that a vendor's location or assurance is enough, and ask counsel.

## Accountability in practice

Keep evidence: the record of processing activities, DPIAs, processor agreements, breach log, training records, decisions and reasons. Review them regularly and assign owners.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company and project. A team plans a new tool that analyses employees' work patterns. You ask: is high risk likely? You do not answer alone: you start a DPIA, involve the DPO and the works council as relevant, check the authority's current lists, log the decision with reasons and set a review date.

## Key takeaways

- A DPIA is required before processing likely to result in high risk; use the authority's current lists.
- Breach handling is detect, assess, decide on notification, document and learn; check conditions and deadlines in the regulation text.
- The DPO advises and monitors independently; responsibility stays with the organisation.
- Joint controllers and third-country transfers need counsel.
