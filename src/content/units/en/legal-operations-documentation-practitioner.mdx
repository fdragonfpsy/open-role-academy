## Why this matters

When a dispute, audit or data subject request arrives, the organisation must be able to show what it did and when. Good documentation practice makes this routine instead of a scramble. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Version control

The Wikipedia articles on version control (English and German) describe tracking changes to files over time so that earlier states can be recovered and changes attributed. For legal documents the principles are the same even without special software: a single source of truth, clear version labels (draft, in review, approved, signed), a record of who changed what and why, and a rule that signed versions are never edited. When several people comment on one draft, one person merges the changes, so that variants do not multiply.

## Audit trail

The article on audit trails describes a record that shows who did what and when, which lets later reviewers reconstruct events. For a legal file, log creation, changes, approvals, access to sensitive documents and deletion. The German term "Revisionssicherheit" refers to storing documents in a way that makes later undetected changes hard; read the article to learn the idea and ask your IT and records teams what your system provides.

## Retention schedule

A retention schedule lists document types, how long each is kept, the reason and who is responsible. Periods come from different rules and from business needs; take them from the approved schedule rather than from memory (the article on Aufbewahrungsfrist explains the term). Document disposal as well: what was deleted, when, under which rule. If a dispute or investigation is foreseeable, deletion of related documents may need to be paused; escalate that question to legal counsel at once. Personal data should not be kept longer than needed, so retention and data protection must be aligned.

## Templates and playbooks

Appoint an owner for each template, version templates like any other document, record what changed and when, and retire outdated ones. A short playbook can state which clauses are standard, which need approval and who approves.

## Access and confidentiality

Grant access by role, review access lists regularly, and keep sensitive matters in restricted areas. The BSI's IT-Grundschutz is a German public source for information security practice and can serve as general orientation for confidentiality measures; the specific controls belong to your IT security function.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An invented company is asked to show how a services agreement was approved. Thanks to its practice, the legal operations lead can show: version history with labels, the approval note from the responsible manager, the signed PDF and the access log. She also shows that an old draft was deleted on schedule, with the entry in the disposal log. Nobody needs to rely on memory.

## Key takeaways

- Version labels, one source of truth and untouched signed versions keep files trustworthy.
- An audit trail records who did what and when, including deletion.
- Use the approved retention schedule; document disposal and escalate possible holds.
- Template owners, change records and role-based access are basic governance.
