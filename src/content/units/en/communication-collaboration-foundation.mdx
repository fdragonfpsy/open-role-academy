## Why this matters

Most work happens between people: handing over tasks, asking for help, agreeing on next steps. Many problems at work are not caused by missing knowledge but by misunderstandings — an unclear e-mail, a request nobody noticed, feedback that hurt instead of helped. Three basic skills prevent a lot of this: listening, writing clearly and giving useful feedback.

## Active listening

Active listening means engaging intentionally with what someone says, so that both sides really understand each other. Useful techniques:

- **Paraphrase:** repeat the core message in your own words ("So the parts arrived, but the paperwork is missing?").
- **Ask open questions:** "What happened next?" instead of "Was it the supplier?"
- **Reflect feelings** when they matter: "That sounds frustrating."
- **Minimise distractions:** no e-mails while someone is talking to you.

## Writing clear messages

Readers decide within seconds what to do with a message. Help them:

- Put the **request and the deadline first**.
- Add only the **background the reader needs**.
- Write a **subject line** that names topic and request ("Decision needed by Friday: supplier for project X").
- Use short paragraphs or bullet points.

**Choose the channel by urgency and purpose.** Urgent problems need a phone call or direct conversation; decisions and agreements should also be written down; general information fits e-mail or the team channel.

## Giving feedback

Helpful feedback is specific and about behaviour, not about the person. A simple structure: **observation → effect → request**. "In today's meeting you showed the figures without last year's comparison, so we could not judge the trend. Could you add it next time?" Vague praise ("great job") or labels ("you are chaotic") give no direction.

## Worked example

Aylin needs approval for a software purchase. Her first draft starts with three paragraphs of background. She rewrites it: subject "Approval needed by Thursday: licence for tool Y", first line with the request and price, then three bullet points of reasons. Her manager approves the same day.

## Key takeaways

- Listen to understand: paraphrase, ask open questions, avoid distractions.
- Request and deadline first, background second.
- Urgent → talk; important agreements → also in writing.
- Feedback: observation, effect, request — about behaviour, not the person.
