## Why this matters

Almost every sales conversation brings objections: "too expensive", "not now", "we already have a supplier". Beginners often hear them as a "no" and either give up or argue. Treated well, an objection shows what the customer still needs to know before deciding.

## Prevent what you can

OpenStax notes that preparation in the earlier steps gives the seller the information needed to handle objections, and that good research and an understanding of how the product helps the customer allow the seller to avoid many of them. Questions in discovery often make an objection unnecessary because the answer is already in the offer.

## Listen, clarify, respond, confirm

One common sequence (practice insight, there are other versions):

1. **Listen** until the customer has finished and show that you heard ("So the price worries you.").
2. **Clarify** with an open question: "Compared with what?", "What would you need to see?" The first words are often not the real concern.
3. **Respond** to the real concern with facts, an example or an option, not with pressure.
4. **Confirm:** "Does that answer your question?"

Do not argue and do not take it personally; an objection is about the offer and the situation, not about you.

## Typical objections

- **Price:** often means "I do not see the value yet." Return to the customer's stated needs.
- **Timing:** ask what has to happen first and agree on a next step.
- **Trust or need:** answer with proof, such as a reference or a trial, and with honest limits.
- **Competitor:** acknowledge it and ask what the customer values in it.

## First negotiation steps

Wikipedia describes negotiation as a dialogue to resolve differences or craft outcomes that satisfy interests, and notes that negotiators should know their own needs and seek to understand those of the others. A position is what someone demands ("a ten percent discount"); an interest is the reason behind it ("our budget is fixed this quarter"). Ask about the reason. Never invent a competing offer or a deadline to push the customer: this damages trust and can be unfair or even unlawful in some cases. Ask your manager or compliance contact for your company's rules.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A café owner says, "Your subscription is too expensive." You listen and reply, "Too expensive compared with what?" She says her current paper system costs nothing. You ask what the paper system costs in time. She admits about two hours each evening. You respond with a figure from her own estimate and offer a one-month trial. You confirm: "Does that address your concern?" She agrees to the trial.

## Key takeaways

- An objection is information, not rejection.
- Listen, clarify, respond, confirm.
- Find the interest behind the position.
- Stay honest: no invented offers, deadlines or promises.
- Prepare in earlier steps to avoid avoidable objections.
