## Why this matters

At practitioner level you work independently, so you are the person who performs a control, notices it failing, or explains it to an auditor. This unit gives you the vocabulary and a few working tools.

## Preventive and detective controls

The German article on the internal control system (Internes Kontrollsystem) distinguishes controls by purpose. **Preventive** controls aim to stop errors and omissions before they happen and are used especially in high-risk processes. **Detective** controls aim to uncover and correct errors after the fact; its example is checking the depreciation method during the annual close. Controls can also be **manual** or **automated** (performed by a system without manual intervention). The Internal control article lists related examples: reconciliations, review and approval authorisations, and IT application controls such as edit checks on data entry or accounting for transactions in numerical sequences. A good process usually combines both kinds.

## Process documentation and the control matrix

Controls are easier to run and to test when they are written down. A simple **control matrix** is a table with one row per control. Typical columns (a common working format, not a legal requirement): the risk, the control, who performs it, how often, whether it is manual or automated, and where the evidence is kept. The German IKS article notes that controls recur in cycles (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly), that checklists help, and that the result is recorded in a report which auditors later sample.

## Approval limits

An approval limit states who may approve what, often depending on the amount. It applies the idea of authorisation of transactions listed in the Internal control article: review of a transaction by an appropriate person. Limits are set by your organisation, not by this course; always use the current authorisation rules and never split a payment to stay under a limit.

## A retention concept

Records must be kept orderly for a defined period. The German article on retention periods explains that completed business transactions can matter again, for example when limitation or warranty periods are still running, and that commercial and tax law are the best known sources of retention rules, in addition to sector-specific ones. A retention concept therefore lists record types, the applicable rule, the owner and the storage place. **Take the periods from your country's current rules and your organisation's policy; do not copy numbers from a course.**

## Preparing for an audit request

When an auditor asks for documents: clarify the exact request and period, find the supporting vouchers and system records (the audit trail), check they are complete, log what you hand over and when, and give only what was asked. Say so openly if something is missing; do not recreate or alter records afterwards.

## Worked example

Illustration only; the process and the limits are invented company policy. A fictional company has this control matrix extract for supplier payments.

| Risk | Control | Type | Performed by | Frequency | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payment of an unapproved invoice | Approver checks invoice against order and receipt | Preventive, manual | Budget owner | Each invoice | Approval note in system |
| Payment above own limit | System blocks release above the user's limit | Preventive, automated | System | Each payment | Block log |
| Duplicate payment goes unnoticed | Weekly review of payments with same supplier and amount | Detective, manual | Accountant | Weekly | Signed checklist |

An auditor asks for evidence of the weekly review for last quarter. You export the signed checklists, note two weeks with no signature, report that openly and agree the next step with your manager.

## Key takeaways

- Preventive controls stop problems; detective controls find them. Use both.
- A control matrix records risk, control, owner, frequency and evidence.
- Approval limits are set by your organisation; never work around them.
- A retention concept names record types, the applicable rule, owner and storage; check your country's rules.
- For audit requests: clarify, collect, check, log, hand over, and never alter records.
