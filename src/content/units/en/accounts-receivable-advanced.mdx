## Why this matters

At the advanced level you manage receivables as a portfolio: whom you sell to on credit, how much risk you accept, how well collection works and how to protect the process from errors and fraud. Decisions here affect cash, profit and customer relationships.

## Credit limits and risk assessment

**Credit risk** is the risk that a customer will not pay in full or on time. A **credit limit** caps the open amount you allow per customer. In general terms, risk is assessed from information such as the customer's payment history with you, financial statements where available, external credit reports and the size and type of the business relationship. Set limits by a documented policy, review them regularly, and define who may approve exceptions. New customers often start with lower limits or prepayment. Customer data is personal or business-sensitive data: use only data you are allowed to use under your country's rules, including data protection law.

## DSO and receivables KPIs

**Days sales outstanding (DSO)** is a measure of how long, on average, it takes to collect payment after a sale. Companies define the exact formula and period themselves, so always state how a figure is calculated before comparing numbers. Other useful indicators: the share of receivables that are overdue, the aging structure, the number of disputed items and the amount written off. Use KPIs to ask questions (which customers or processes drive late payment?), not as targets that invite shortcuts.

## Bad-debt allowances (concept)

Some receivables will never be collected. Accounting reflects this by an **allowance** (German: Wertberichtigung) that reduces the carrying value of receivables to what is expected to be collectable, and by writing off receivables that are definitely lost. Which method applies, and how tax treats it, depends on the accounting framework and country; follow your company's policy and your accountants' guidance.

## Escalation and collections handoff

When reminders fail, escalate step by step: a call, a payment plan if appropriate, then handoff to a **collection agency** or legal steps. Debt collection is often regulated, and rules limit harassment and unfair practices in many countries. Before handoff, check that the claim is undisputed and documented (invoice, proof of delivery, reminder history). Consider the relationship: a long-standing customer in a temporary difficulty may deserve a payment plan, and sales should be informed before escalation.

## Segregation of duties and fraud

Separate who maintains customer master data, who issues invoices and credit notes, who posts payments and who approves write-offs. One person controlling all steps can hide errors or theft, for example by posting credit notes to cover diverted payments. A known fraud pattern is the **fake payment confirmation**: a customer sends a screenshot or email claiming a transfer was made, to get goods released. Verify against your bank statement, not against the document. Changes of customer bank details for refunds deserve a callback via a known number.

## Automation

Software can match payments by reference, send reminders on schedule and flag risk. Automation speeds up routine work, but rules need review: exceptions, disputes and unusual cases still need people, and automated reminders must not go to disputed items.

## Worked example

Illustration only; the numbers are invented. A customer has a credit limit of 5,000 and open invoices of 4,200. A new order is worth 1,500. Open plus new would be 5,700, above the limit. The order is held for review: sales and credit control either ask for a partial prepayment, request payment of overdue items first, or approve an exception with documented reasoning.

## Key takeaways

- Credit limits and documented risk assessment control exposure to non-payment.
- DSO and other KPIs need a clear definition; use them to ask questions.
- Allowances reflect expected losses; method and tax treatment depend on country and policy.
- Escalate stepwise; check the claim is undisputed and documented before collections handoff.
- Separate duties and verify payments against the bank, never against screenshots.
