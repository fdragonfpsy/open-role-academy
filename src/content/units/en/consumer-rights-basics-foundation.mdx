## Why this matters

Customers often ask: "Can I send this back?" or "Is this still under warranty?" Your answer can create wrong expectations or wrongly deny a right. This unit covers EU rules at topic level, with Germany as the national context. It is not legal advice. National law implements the EU directives, so legal or compliance staff decide the details; this unit gives no section numbers or national deadlines.

## Who is a consumer

In the EU rules, a consumer is a natural person acting outside their trade, business, craft or profession. The seller acts within its trade or business. Consumer rules therefore usually apply to private buyers, not to a company ordering for its own business.

## Withdrawal from distance contracts

For contracts concluded at a distance, such as online or by phone, the Consumer Rights Directive gives the consumer a right to withdraw without giving any reason. The basic period is 14 days. For goods it normally starts on the day the consumer (or a person they name) receives the goods; for services it starts when the contract is concluded.

- The consumer can use the model withdrawal form or any other clear statement that they withdraw. They must send it before the period ends.
- The trader reimburses the payments without undue delay, at the latest 14 days after learning of the withdrawal. It may hold back the refund until the goods are back or the consumer shows they were sent back.
- The consumer returns the goods without undue delay and bears the direct return cost, unless the trader agreed to bear it or did not inform them about it.

There are exceptions, for example goods made to the consumer's specifications, sealed hygiene goods that were unsealed, and services fully performed after the consumer's express consent. Whether an exception applies is a legal question.

## Statutory remedy versus voluntary guarantee

These are two different things.

- Statutory remedy (in German "Gewährleistung"): the seller is liable for a lack of conformity that existed at delivery. The consumer can ask for repair or replacement and, under conditions, a price reduction or termination. The seller, not the manufacturer, is the contact.
- Commercial guarantee (in German "Garantie"): an extra promise by the seller or producer. It is binding under the terms of the guarantee statement and the related advertising.

A guarantee that has expired does not mean the statutory rights have ended.

Our own four steps: ask what the customer wants, note the dates (order, delivery, first problem), note the product and the channel of purchase, then pass the case on with your company's form. Say neutrally what happens next; do not promise or refuse a right yourself.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented shop. A customer writes on day 12 after delivery: "The headphones are not what I expected. Can I send them back?" The agent notes order date, delivery date and the product, confirms that the customer's request will be treated as a withdrawal question, explains the shop's return process, and passes the dates to the returns team. She does not say "too late" or "no questions asked"; the team checks the facts.

## Key takeaways

- Consumer rules protect private buyers; the seller must follow them.
- Distance purchases give a withdrawal right of 14 days at the base, with exceptions.
- Statutory remedy and voluntary guarantee are different; an expired guarantee does not end statutory rights.
- Collect dates and facts, stay neutral and refer legal questions.
