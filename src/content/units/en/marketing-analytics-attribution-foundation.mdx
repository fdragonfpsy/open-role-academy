## Why this matters

Marketing spends money and time. Measurement tells you whether a campaign moved people towards the goal or only produced activity. At foundation level you need a small, honest set of numbers and the habit of asking "so what?" about each one.

## Metrics, KPIs and vanity metrics

OpenStax describes metrics as goals that marketers try to reach through their campaigns, typically quantitative, for example likes, shares or comments on social media content. A **KPI** (key performance indicator) is a metric you have chosen because it is tied to a business goal. A **vanity metric** is a number that looks good but does not tell you what to do next, for instance total followers or page views when the goal is sign-ups. This distinction is a common working convention, not a formal standard.

A quick test, our own working method: *If this number doubled tomorrow, would we decide anything differently or earn more?* If not, it is context, not a KPI.

## The funnel and conversions

OpenStax defines a sales funnel as a visual representation of the customer journey from product awareness to purchase; website traffic is at the top, where most visitors stop. A **conversion** happens when a company turns a visitor into a customer, according to OpenStax; the conversion can also be a sign-up or a form completion, depending on the goal. The German Wikipedia article on Konversion describes it more generally as a change of status of a target person, for example from prospect to customer.

Typical metrics along the funnel, as named by OpenStax: website traffic, traffic by source, bounce rate (visitors who view one page and leave), conversions, click-through rate (share of people who click an ad) and cost per acquisition.

## Two formulas

- **Conversion rate** = conversions / visitors. The German Wikipedia article writes it per unique visitors.
- **Cost per acquisition (CPA)** = money spent on the campaign / number of conversions (OpenStax).

Always state what counts as a conversion and which visitors you count; otherwise two people compute different rates from the same data.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented numbers. A small online shop spends 500 euros on a campaign in one month. The campaign leads to 10,000 visits and 50 purchases. Conversion rate: 50 / 10,000 = 0.5 percent. CPA: 500 / 50 = 10 euros per purchase. The campaign also got 2,000 social media likes. The likes are a vanity metric here: they do not change the decision whether the 10 euro CPA is acceptable, which depends on margin per purchase.

## Key takeaways

- Choose few KPIs, each tied to a goal; treat the rest as context.
- Define the conversion before the campaign starts.
- Conversion rate and CPA are simple ratios; write down numerator and denominator.
- High traffic or many likes alone do not show success.
