## Why this matters

In HR you handle contracts, absences, working hours and many questions from managers and colleagues. Behind each of them sits employment law. You do not need to be a lawyer, but you do need a map: where do the rules come from, what does a contract regulate, and who do you ask when it gets difficult? This unit gives you that map for Germany and the EU. It deliberately contains no deadlines, day counts, thresholds or section numbers: they change and depend on the case, so always check the current text. HR staff do not give legal advice; for individual cases, involve legal counsel.

## What employment law is

Labour (employment) law regulates the relationship between workers, employers, trade unions and the state. The English article distinguishes individual labour law, which concerns the rights of employees at work including through the contract, from collective labour law, which concerns the relationship between employee, employer and union. The German article on Arbeitsrecht draws the same line between Individualarbeitsrecht and kollektives Arbeitsrecht.

## Many sources, no single code

In Germany there is no single, unified labour code. According to the German article, the rules are scattered across several sources: EU law (mostly directives), the constitution, the civil code, many separate statutes on topics such as working time, leave, dismissal protection, part-time and fixed-term work, equal treatment and the works constitution, plus collective agreements (Tarifverträge), works agreements (Betriebsvereinbarungen) and individual employment contracts. Court decisions are not formally a source of law, but in practice they matter a lot.

## What an employment contract is

An employment contract attributes rights and responsibilities between employer and employee. In very simple terms, the employee accepts the employer's direction and the employer pays the agreed pay. The English article also explains why the dividing line to self-employment matters: some rights, such as protection against unfair dismissal, are attached to employees. Under German law the employment contract is a private-law contract; the employee owes work in personal dependence and the employer owes the agreed pay. A contract typically regulates who the parties are, which work is to be done, how it is paid, working time and how the relationship ends. According to the German article, such a contract can be concluded orally or in writing; still, write terms down and check the current statutory duties to document them.

## Why rules differ from case to case

Several layers can apply at once: statute, a collective agreement, a works agreement and the individual contract. Which collective agreement applies depends on the sector and the employer. The works council (Betriebsrat) is the elected employee representation in private-sector workplaces; public-sector bodies have staff councils instead. Terms that are more favourable for the individual can take precedence over a works agreement. This is why the answer to "what applies to this person?" is rarely a single sentence.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real figures. A new colleague asks: "The contract says one thing about leave, but my friend in another company has a different arrangement. Which is right?" A good HR reaction is not to answer from memory. Step 1: identify the layers: the individual contract, any collective agreement that applies, any works agreement, and the statute. Step 2: look up the current wording of each in the primary sources, not in an old email. Step 3: if the layers seem to conflict or the case is unusual, hand it to legal counsel with the documents. Step 4: record what you checked.

## Who to ask

For statute texts use the official portal and the current version of the law. For company-specific rules use the contract, the collective agreement and works agreements. For legal interpretation use the company's legal department or an employment lawyer, and involve the works council where its role is relevant.

## Key takeaways

- Employment law has an individual and a collective side.
- In Germany the rules are spread over many sources, none of which is a single code.
- A contract regulates the core of the relationship, but statute, collective agreements and works agreements interact with it.
- Which rules apply depends on the case; never rely on memory or on other companies.
- This content is for Germany/EU orientation only; HR staff do not give legal advice. Involve legal counsel for individual cases.
