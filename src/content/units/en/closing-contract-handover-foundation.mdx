## Why this matters

A sale is not finished when the customer says "sounds good". It is finished when both sides have agreed on the same thing, in writing, and the people who deliver know what was promised. At foundation level you learn to ask for the decision, to check the paperwork and to pass on the deal without gaps.

## Asking for the decision

The OpenStax chapter on the personal selling process lists closing as the step in which the salesperson asks for the order, and follow-up as the step after it. Many beginners avoid the ask because it feels pushy. It is not pushy when the customer has shown interest and you have answered the open questions. Typical buying signals are questions about delivery dates, price details, contract length or who will support them. A **trial close** tests readiness with a soft question, for example "Does this match what you need for the rollout?" If the answer is yes, ask directly: "Shall we start with the three-user package on the first of next month?"

If the answer is no or "not yet", ask what is missing. Do not pressure the customer or invent deadlines; a decision made under false pressure often turns into a cancellation or a complaint.

## Check before you sign

A contract is an agreement that specifies rights and obligations of two or more parties, as the general Wikipedia article puts it. Rules differ by country, so the legal details are for your legal team. What you can do is check the basics: correct customer name and address, product or service, quantity, price, term, payment terms and start date. Every promise made in the conversation that matters must appear in the document, or be removed from the conversation.

## Handover

After the signature, the customer should not have to explain everything again. A good handover note to delivery, support or customer success contains: who the contact people are, what was bought and when it starts, the customer's goal in their own words, special agreements, and open questions. Tell the customer who will contact them next and when.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. After a demo, a customer asks about the training for new users. You see this as a buying signal and ask, "Shall we include the training and start in January?" The customer agrees. Before sending the order form you check: the company name is the legal entity, three user licences, a twelve-month term, annual invoicing. The customer also wanted the training "within the first month", which you add to the form instead of leaving it to memory. You write a handover note with two contacts, the goal (faster onboarding for the new team) and the agreed training date, and you tell the customer that onboarding will call within two working days.

## Key takeaways

- Watch for buying signals and ask for the decision clearly and fairly.
- Never use invented deadlines or pressure.
- Check the order form against what was said; put promises in writing.
- Hand over goal, scope, contacts and special agreements.
- Tell the customer what happens next.
