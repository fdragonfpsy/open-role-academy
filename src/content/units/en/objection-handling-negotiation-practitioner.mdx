## Why this matters

At practitioner level you negotiate price, scope, timing and terms within limits set by your company. The aim is an agreement both sides can keep, not a win at any cost.

## Prepare

Before the meeting, write down: your interests (revenue, margin, reference value, strategic fit), what you can give (scope, term, payment terms, support) and the customer's likely interests. The Wikipedia article on the best alternative to a negotiated agreement (BATNA) describes it as the most favourable course of action a party can take if negotiations fail; it serves as a standard to evaluate offers. Know yours (for example, other prospects in your pipeline) and estimate the customer's (staying with the current supplier, a competitor, doing nothing). Set a walk-away point with your manager before you start; this is where you are better off without the deal.

## Principled negotiation

The German Wikipedia article on the Harvard-Konzept, developed by Roger Fisher and William Ury, lists central principles: treat people and the issues separately, focus on interests, not positions, develop options for mutual gain and insist on objective criteria. In sales: be firm on the problem, friendly to the person; ask why a demand is made; propose several options; refer to criteria such as list prices, comparable deals or a total-cost calculation.

## Concessions

Practice insight: never give a concession for free. Use "if... then": "If you commit to three years, I can include the training." Concede in small steps, and the later and smaller they get, the clearer the signal that you reach a limit. Package several issues together (price, term, scope, payment terms) so that you can trade where you give little and the customer gains much. Do not give discounts you cannot justify; they teach the customer that your first price was not serious.

## Real objection or stalling?

Test with a question: "If we solve this point, is there anything else that stops you?" A real objection has a concrete reason; a stall stays vague. Respond to the real one and agree on the next step for the other.

## Fairness

Pressure tactics, false deadlines, invented competing offers and misleading claims damage trust and may be unlawful in specific situations. Check with your compliance contact where you are unsure.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A customer demands a 15 percent discount. You ask what is behind it: the budget for this year is fixed. You have an approval limit of ten percent. You offer: a lower first-year fee with the full price in years two and three, in return for a three-year term and a reference. The customer accepts after asking for a quarterly payment option, which you grant in return for a signature by month end. Your BATNA (two other qualified prospects) stays unspoken but clear to you.

## Key takeaways

- Prepare interests, BATNA and walk-away point.
- Ask for the interest behind a position; use objective criteria.
- Trade concessions ("if... then"); package issues.
- Separate real objections from stalling.
- Stay fair and within your approval limits.
