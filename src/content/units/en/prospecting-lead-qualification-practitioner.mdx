## Why this matters

Practitioners choose which leads to work, in what order and how to approach them. That needs a clear picture of who buys and a repeatable way to rank leads, and it needs care with personal data. This unit is a general EU view; national rules on unsolicited advertising (in Germany the Act against Unfair Competition, UWG) also apply and are not covered here. Ask your legal or data-protection contact.

## Ideal customer profile

An ideal customer profile (ICP) describes the type of company, and the roles within it, for which your offer works best. Practice insight: build it from evidence. Look at your won and retained customers and ask what they share (size, industry, problem, trigger event) and which lost deals did not fit. Write the ICP as testable criteria so that two colleagues rate the same lead the same way.

## Scoring leads

The Lead scoring article distinguishes **explicit** data (company size, industry, job title, location) from **implicit** data (behaviour such as website visits, downloads or e-mail clicks). Fit score and interest score answer different questions: "Is this the right kind of customer?" and "Are they active now?" The German article on Lead Management places scoring between nurturing and the hand-over (Lead Routing) to sales. A simple table might give points for industry match, role match and a pricing-page visit, and set a threshold for contact. Review the thresholds against actual wins.

## Research before contact

OpenStax calls this the pre-approach: learn who decides, what the account currently uses and what recent events might trigger a need. Use public business information, and note sources. Personalised, relevant messages beat generic ones.

## Contact data and objections

Under the EU GDPR (Regulation 2016/679), where personal data are processed for direct marketing, the person has the right to object at any time (Art. 21(2)); after an objection the data may no longer be processed for that purpose (Art. 21(3)); and this right must be explicitly brought to the person's attention at the latest at the first communication (Art. 21(4)). Recital 47 states that direct marketing may be regarded as a legitimate interest, which is not a blanket permission. Practice insight: record the source and date of each contact, honour opt-outs immediately and store only what you need.

## Worked example

Your ICP: companies with 20 to 200 staff in logistics that use several sites. Scoring: industry match 30 points, role is operations manager 20, visited pricing page 15, only downloaded a general leaflet 5; contact threshold 45. Lead X scores 30+20+15 = 65 and is called today. Lead Y, a student, scores 5 and is dropped. After a quarter you compare scores of won deals and lower the threshold because many wins started at 40.

## Key takeaways

- Derive the ICP from won customers and write it as testable criteria.
- Combine fit (explicit) and interest (implicit) in lead scoring and check it against results.
- Research before you reach out.
- Under GDPR Art. 21, people can object to direct marketing at any time; log sources and honour objections.
- Rules on unsolicited advertising vary; check with legal.
