## Why this matters

Once you hold one-to-ones regularly, quality matters more than frequency. This unit covers how to adapt your approach, keep boundaries clear and follow through. It is a concept-level discussion.

## Adapt to the person

A new team member may need more telling and clearer guidance. An experienced person may need space and good questions. The Wikipedia article on coaching notes that coaching differs from mentoring by focusing on specific tasks or objectives; this helps you decide what the conversation is for right now: a concrete skill, a career question or a work problem. Ask the person what kind of support they want today: "Do you want ideas, a sounding board or a decision?" Different people prefer different formats, so offer choices (walking, video, in person) where practical.

## Structure is a tool, not a rule

The GROW article says several versions of the model exist. Experienced coaches move between stages as the conversation requires. If the person jumps to options before the goal is clear, go back one step. If the person is stuck in the problem, ask what they would like instead. Resist the urge to fill silences.

## Boundaries

- **Scope:** you are the person's lead, not their therapist. The German article separates coaching from the treatment of mental illness. Refer to HR, occupational health services or external support when issues exceed your role.
- **Dual role:** you also evaluate performance and decide on assignments. Be open about what you will and will not pass on, and keep promises about confidentiality. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep, for example where a safety or legal issue is involved.
- **Records:** keep notes short and factual, store them as your organisation's rules require, and remember that personal data of employees is subject to data protection rules in the EU. Ask HR or your data protection officer when unsure.
- **Culture and context:** norms about directness and hierarchy differ between people; check understanding instead of assuming.

## Follow-through and patterns

Trust grows when actions agreed in one-to-ones happen. Track your own commitments as carefully as theirs. Across several conversations you may notice repeating themes, such as unclear priorities. Address those at team level without naming who raised them, unless the person agrees.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. Leyla notices that three people in separate one-to-ones mention unclear priorities between two projects. She does not quote anyone. In the next team meeting she says that she wants to clarify priorities and asks the team to help rank the open items. In her next one-to-one with Mehmet, who looked tired, she asks an open question about workload; he mentions personal problems. Leyla listens, says what support is available through HR, and does not try to counsel him herself.

## Key takeaways

- Adapt the style: more telling for newcomers, more asking for experienced people.
- Use structures such as GROW flexibly.
- Be clear about scope, confidentiality and record keeping.
- Close the loop: keep your own commitments and use patterns without exposing individuals.
