## Presentation and consistency

Correct numbers can still mislead if they are presented inconsistently. Typical points to check:

- **Format and method**: the German article on the income statement explains that the format once chosen (account form or report form) generally has to be kept, and deviations must be explained in the Anhang. It also states that both the nature-of-expense method (Gesamtkostenverfahren) and the cost-of-sales method (Umsatzkostenverfahren) are in principle permitted under HGB and IFRS, and that IAS 1 prefers the cost-of-sales method.
- **Offsetting**: the English IFRS article notes that offsetting is generally forbidden under IFRS, with exceptions where specific standards require it. Do not net items to make a balance sheet look tidier.
- **Same labels, same meaning**: a line item must mean the same thing in every period, otherwise comparisons are not reliable.

## Comparing periods

The statements are usually read against the previous period. IFRS requires comparative information for the preceding period for all amounts reported, and it requires an additional (third) balance sheet when an accounting policy is applied retrospectively, items are restated retrospectively, or items are reclassified. The practical rule: before you comment on a change, find out whether the prior figure is on the same basis. If an item was reclassified, compare against the restated prior figure and say so.

## Management reports for non-finance readers

Under the HGB, the management report (Lagebericht) has to give a picture that matches the actual circumstances, and the German standards call for clarity and good structure. The English-language management discussion and analysis is described as a narrative explanation of past performance, financial condition and prospects that should be fair and balanced. Practical guidance for writing:

1. Start with the message: what happened and why it matters.
2. Explain figures in words, and tie each to its cause or state that the cause is unknown.
3. Cover risks and opportunities as well as results, as the Lagebericht structure does.
4. Define terms or avoid jargon; a reader without finance background should not need the ledger to follow it.
5. Keep it consistent with the statements: no number in the narrative that the statements contradict.

## Disclosures and the notes

The notes carry what does not fit in the statement lines. The IFRS article describes that an item that is not recognised may still need disclosure in the notes, and that the notes include a summary of significant accounting policies. In Germany, since the BilRUG reform the subsequent-events report is part of the Anhang rather than the Lagebericht, according to the Lagebericht article. For your work: keep a list of what each note must cover, and update it when policies, formats or items change.

## Limits of financial statements

Statements are a record of the past, based on rules about recognition and measurement. The Financial statement article stresses that they give objective information about the reporting period and are better protected against manipulation than forward-looking calculations, but they do not directly measure how far any reader's individual goals are met. Valuation choices affect results and equity, so two correct sets of statements under different frameworks can show different results. Name the framework and the limits when you present figures.

## Review before release

Corporations that are not small have their statements and management report audited and then disclosed, as the Jahresabschluss article states for German corporations. Before that, an internal review should be organised: a reviewer who did not prepare the statements, a checklist of the points above, a tie-out of every number to the closed ledger, and written sign-off.

## Worked example

Illustration only; the amounts are invented.

| | This year | Prior year | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 50,000 | 42,000 | +8,000 |
| Expenses | -44,000 | -39,000 | -5,000 |
| Profit | 6,000 | 3,000 | +3,000 |

Draft sentence for a non-finance reader: "Profit doubled from 3,000 to 6,000 because revenue grew by 8,000 while expenses grew by 5,000." Review questions: was any expense of 1,000 reclassified between lines this year, so that the prior column is not comparable? If so, restate the prior column and add a note. Is the reason for the revenue growth known, or only assumed? If only assumed, write "the cause is being analysed" rather than inventing one.

## Key takeaways

- Keep format, method and labels consistent; explain deviations in the notes.
- Compare periods on the same basis; restate or flag reclassified items.
- A management report should be clear, balanced and consistent with the statements.
- The notes hold disclosures and policies; maintain a list of required content.
- State the limits of the statements, and have someone other than the preparer review before release.
