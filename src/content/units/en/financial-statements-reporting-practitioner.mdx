## From closing balances to statements

Once the period is closed (reconciliations done, accruals and deferrals posted, trial balance reviewed), the account balances become the statements. Balance-type accounts (assets, liabilities, equity) feed the balance sheet, which refers to the balance sheet date. Income and expense accounts feed the income statement, which covers the period. The German article on the Jahresabschluss describes exactly this split between stock accounts (Bestandskonten) and result accounts (Erfolgskonten). Any posting error or missing accrual that survives the close therefore appears in the statements.

## Reading the statements

When you read a set of statements, check in this order:

1. Do the two sides of the balance sheet agree, and does assets minus liabilities equal equity?
2. Does the profit in the income statement explain the change in equity (allowing for owner contributions and withdrawals)?
3. How do the figures compare with the prior period? A balance sheet is often presented alongside one for an earlier date, and IFRS requires comparative information for the preceding period.
4. What do the notes say about the policies behind the figures?

## The cash flow statement

A cash flow statement shows how changes in balance sheet accounts and income affect cash and cash equivalents. It splits the cash movements into **operating, investing and financing activities**. It helps judge a company's ability to pay its bills in the short term. A company can report a profit and still be short of cash, because profit and cash move at different times. Under IFRS the standard is IAS 7, and all entities prepare a cash flow statement. Under HGB (Germany), a cash flow statement and a statement of changes in equity are required for capital-market-oriented corporations and in consolidated statements; other companies need not prepare one.

## HGB and IFRS at a high level

This section describes Germany and the EU context only as far as the cited pages do. The Wikipedia articles state that the traditional German accounting under the HGB mainly aims at creditor protection and applies the prudence principle, while IFRS statements serve an information function for users such as capital-market investors. In Germany, capital-market-oriented EU companies must prepare consolidated statements under IFRS (Section 315e HGB); other companies may do so voluntarily. Your company's accountants decide which framework applies; as a learner, know which one you are looking at.

## The basic management report

Under the HGB, medium-sized and large corporations must prepare a management report (Lagebericht) together with the annual financial statements. It is not part of the Jahresabschluss itself. It describes the course of business, the results and the situation of the company, and covers risks, opportunities and forecasts, so that readers get a picture that completes the balance sheet and income statement. English-language practice has a similar narrative, the management discussion and analysis. Separately, management accounting means giving managers financial and non-financial information for decisions.

## Worked example

Illustration only; the amounts are invented. Following on from the foundation example, cash at the start of the year was 3,000 and at year end 4,000 on the balance sheet.

| Cash flow statement | Amount |
|---|---|
| Operating activities | 9,000 |
| Investing activities (equipment bought) | -6,000 |
| Financing activities (loan repayment) | -2,000 |
| Net change in cash | 1,000 |
| Cash at start | 3,000 |
| Cash at end | 4,000 |

The closing cash of 4,000 agrees with the balance sheet, which is the basic consistency check: the cash flow statement must end at the cash figure shown there. Note that the profit of that year, 6,000, differs from the operating cash flow of 9,000; the notes or the management report would explain why.

## Key takeaways

- Closed balance-type accounts feed the balance sheet; income and expense accounts feed the income statement.
- Read statements for internal consistency and compare with the prior period.
- The cash flow statement groups cash movements into operating, investing and financing activities and ends at the cash figure of the balance sheet.
- The cited sources describe HGB as creditor-protection oriented and IFRS as information oriented; always label which framework applies.
- The management report (Lagebericht) complements, but is not part of, the Jahresabschluss.
