## Why this matters

Adding a channel is easy; running several so that the customer experiences one service is hard. At advanced level you decide structure, staffing and measurement, not only single replies.

## What omnichannel requires

Our own working definition: omnichannel service means that a customer can switch channel mid-case without repeating themselves and gets the same answer. This needs three things: one shared case record (CRM systems store phone calls, emails, live chats and service requests together), one shared source of answers, and one consistent message. Consistency of message across platforms is also a core idea in social media work, and it matters for brand and trust.

## Which issue in which channel

Our own criteria for routing:

- Complexity: many steps or attachments favour email or phone.
- Emotion: strong anger or worry favours a live voice.
- Urgency: a blocked service favours phone or chat.
- Sensitivity: personal or payment data never belongs in public channels.

Telling customers politely where the best channel is for their issue is better than forcing everything through one door.

## Staffing across channels

Call centre planning works from expected volume per period with a given service level, then plans shifts and assigns agents; staff trained for several projects ("multiskillers") can be allocated flexibly. When call volume drops, agents can take on email work (call blending). The same idea applies across channels: cross-train staff, but protect quality by limiting how many channels one person handles at once. Our own caution: chat is hard to combine with live calls, so test the limit with real data.

## Measuring without distortion

Compare channels on resolution and customer feedback, not only on speed. A chat team judged only on handling time may close cases too early; a phone team judged only on call length may rush emotional customers. Our own check: for every metric, ask what shortcut it invites and add a counter-measure such as reopen rate.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A shop sees growing chat volume and complaints that customers must repeat their order number. The lead (1) adds the order number and last case summary to the chat tool automatically, (2) writes a routing guide: refunds with disputes go to phone or email, delivery status to chat, (3) trains six phone agents for chat in low-call hours and limits them to two chats at a time, and (4) adds reopen rate next to handling time in the weekly review. After a month the "repeat yourself" complaints drop; the lead keeps the reopen rate as a standing metric.

## Key takeaways

- Omnichannel needs one case record, one source of answers and one consistent message.
- Route issues by complexity, emotion, urgency and sensitivity.
- Cross-train staff but limit parallel channels per person.
- Pair speed metrics with outcome metrics such as reopen rate.
