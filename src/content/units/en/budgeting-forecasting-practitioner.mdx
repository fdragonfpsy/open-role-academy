## Building the budget with department owners

A budget works best when the people who spend the money help to plan it. Typical steps: finance prepares a template and the planning calendar, the company's targets and general guidance are shared, each budget owner proposes figures for their cost centre, finance checks them for consistency and completeness, the proposals are discussed and adjusted, and management approves the result. Top-down means targets are set from the top and broken down; bottom-up means the smallest units plan first and the figures are added up. A mix of both is common. Your role is to coordinate, challenge politely and keep the numbers consistent, not to decide for the owners.

Two methods are worth knowing. **Incremental** budgeting starts from the previous period. **Zero-based budgeting** requires all expenses to be justified and approved in each new budget period, starting from a zero base. The Wikipedia article notes that it can take time, effort and additional staff, so some organisations apply it to only some areas each year.

## Assumptions documentation

Every budget figure rests on assumptions: volumes, prices, headcount, timing, exchange rates, inflation. Keep an **assumptions sheet** in the file, with one row per assumption: what it is, the value, who provided it, where it comes from and the date. When something changes later you can update one cell and see the effect, and when a variance appears you can check whether the assumption or the execution was wrong.

## Calculating variances

The variance is the difference between the budgeted amount and the actual amount; it applies to costs and revenues. It is favourable when the result is better than expected and adverse (unfavourable) when worse. Be careful with signs: for revenue, actual above budget is favourable; for costs, actual below budget is favourable. Teams use different sign conventions, so define yours once in the report legend and mark each line (F) or (A). Show absolute amounts and, where useful, percentages of budget.

## From budget to rolling forecast

A budget is fixed for its period. A **rolling forecast** is a general planning idea rather than a single formula: at regular intervals, for example every quarter, you update the outlook and keep a constant horizon by adding a new period at the end as one period closes. The Forecasting article stresses that forecasts allow flexibility and that data must be up to date to make a forecast as accurate as possible. Keep the budget as the agreed reference and show the latest forecast next to it.

## Spreadsheet hygiene

- Keep inputs (assumptions) separate from calculations and outputs.
- No hard-coded numbers inside formulas; refer to the assumption cell.
- One row, one meaning; consistent formulas across a row.
- Add checks: totals equal the sum of the parts; budget totals match the approved figure.
- Version the file name and date, and keep the approved budget read-only.
- Lock formula cells and note who may change what.

## Worked example

Illustration only; all amounts are invented. A marketing cost centre has a quarterly budget.

| Line | Budget | Actual | Variance (budget - actual) | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Travel | 4,000 | 4,600 | -600 | (A) |
| Software | 2,500 | 2,300 | +200 | (F) |
| **Total** | 6,500 | 6,900 | -400 | (A) |

Here the convention is variance = budget minus actual for costs, so a positive number means under budget. Travel is 600 over budget, software 200 under; in total 400 over. The cause of the travel overrun is unknown from the numbers alone, so you ask the budget owner and record the answer. Next, update the forecast for the remaining quarters: if the travel level continues, the forecast changes, and the owner can decide on actions.

## Key takeaways

- Involve budget owners; finance coordinates and checks, owners decide their figures.
- Write every assumption down with value, owner, source and date.
- Define the variance sign convention once and label each line favourable (F) or adverse (A).
- A rolling forecast keeps the outlook current by adding a new period as one closes.
- Separate inputs from calculations, avoid hard-coded numbers, add check cells and version your files.
