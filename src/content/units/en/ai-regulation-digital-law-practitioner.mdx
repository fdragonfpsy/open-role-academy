## Why this matters

Compliance work on AI is mostly structured fact-finding: what is used, for what, with which data, and who decides. The risk-based approach on the Commission's AI Act page only helps if the organisation knows its own uses. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Step 1: Inventory

Create a register of AI uses: tool or system, purpose, owner, whether it was built in-house or bought, data it processes, who is affected (customers, staff, applicants) and whether a human reviews its results. Include tools that staff adopted on their own, because these are often missed.

## Step 2: Classify

Compare each use with the four levels the Commission describes: unacceptable risk, high risk, transparency risk, and minimal or no risk. The page lists categories of high-risk use and examples for minimal risk; read them directly, and note the date and the page you used. If the use is near a boundary, do not decide alone: record the question and escalate to legal counsel. Also check which role your organisation has toward the system, for example developer or deployer, because duties differ by role, as the Commission page frames the rules for both groups.

## Step 3: Connect to other law

- **Personal data:** if the system processes personal data, the GDPR applies; involve the data protection function (see the GDPR competency). The GDPR is available from EUR-Lex in English and German.
- **Online services:** if you operate or use platform services, check the Digital Services Act information from the Commission.
- **Security and operation:** the German BSI offers material on AI for general orientation on security aspects.

## Step 4: Document

For each use record: classification, reasoning, sources with dates, open questions, controls (for example user information, human review, logging), owner and next review date. Revisit when the tool, its purpose or the rules change.

## Boundaries of your role

You prepare the facts and a first assessment. Binding legal conclusions, especially on borderline or high-risk cases, belong to qualified counsel. Do not copy summaries from encyclopedias into official decisions; use them for orientation and go to the official text.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An invented insurer finds three AI uses: a spam filter, a chat assistant for customers and a tool that ranks job applications. The register lists each with owner and data. The spam filter looks like minimal risk; the chat assistant needs a clear notice that customers talk to AI; the ranking tool touches people's opportunities, so the analyst flags it for legal review before any further use and records that she did not decide the classification herself.

## Key takeaways

- Build and maintain an inventory; classification needs facts first.
- Use the four Commission risk levels as the frame and cite the official page.
- Link AI uses to GDPR and, where relevant, platform rules.
- Document reasoning and escalate borderline or high-risk cases.
