## Why this matters

A customer writes: "The device broke after two weeks." You can defend the company, or you can use the moment to fix the problem and keep the customer. Which one you choose often decides whether the customer stays.

## Why complaints are valuable

Complaints management (German: Beschwerdemanagement) means all systematic measures a company takes when customers complain. Its aim is to keep satisfaction up despite the negative event and to learn how to avoid the error in future. Experience suggests that most dissatisfied customers leave without further contact, so a complaint is a second chance to put things right. Many companies speak of feedback management to avoid the negative ring of the word.

## Four steps (our own)

Our own method for the first contact has four steps:

- Acknowledge: say that you understand the problem and thank the customer for telling you.
- Clarify: ask short questions to get the facts, such as order number, what happened and when.
- Offer: state what you can do and by when; if you cannot decide, say who will.
- Confirm: repeat the agreement and the next step.

The four steps follow the direct complaint process described in the literature: receive, process and respond, with fixed processing dates.

## Staying calm

An upset customer is upset about the situation, not about you. Non-aggressive words and behaviour help calm things down, and negatively loaded words are best avoided. Active listening is one technique that is said to have a de-escalating effect. Avoid "That is not our fault" and prefer "Let us look at what happened". Do not argue about who is right before you understand the facts.

## Record it completely

Write down what happened, what the customer wants and what you promised, in the case record. A complaint that must be told twice feels like a second failure. Complete notes also feed the later analysis of what goes wrong most often.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer writes that a delivered chair has a crack. Reply: "Thank you for telling us, and I am sorry the chair arrived damaged. Could you send a photo of the crack and your order number? Then I can arrange a replacement. I will write to you by tomorrow noon with the delivery date." Acknowledged, clarified, offered, confirmed.

## Key takeaways

- A complaint is a second chance and a source of information.
- Acknowledge, clarify, offer, confirm.
- Calm wording and active listening lower the temperature.
- Record the case completely so nobody asks twice.
