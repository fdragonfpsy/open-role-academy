## Why this matters

Customers do not see your queue, only the wait. A team that has agreed what "good service" means, and counts it the same way every week, can notice problems early. This unit gives you the first vocabulary: service levels, response and resolution times, and satisfaction scores.

## What a service level agreement is

A service level agreement (SLA) is an agreement, usually a binding contract, between a provider and a customer. It describes aspects of the service such as quality, availability and responsibilities. The service level itself is the agreed quality of performance, for example scope, availability and reaction time. SLAs are used for outside customers, and also between departments of one company; such internal agreements are often called operational level agreements (OLAs).

Typical parts of an SLA are the type of service, the desired performance level, how it is monitored and reported, how to report problems, response and resolution time frames, and the consequences if the commitment is missed. Good SLAs follow the SMART idea: specific, measurable and realistic.

## Response time and resolution time

These two are often mixed up:

- Response time: how long until the provider starts to look into the issue (for example the first reply).
- Resolution time: how long until the issue is actually fixed.

A quick first reply does not mean a quick solution. When you read an SLA or a team target, check which of the two it talks about.

## Your first satisfaction number

The customer satisfaction score (CSAT) is a common measure. You ask customers to rate their satisfaction from 1 (very unsatisfied) to 5 (very satisfied). Count the ratings of 4 or 5, divide by all responses and multiply by 100. Behind it is a simple idea: satisfaction compares what the customer expected with what they got.

## Three questions before you react (our own)

Our own habit for any number you see in a report:

- What exactly is counted, and which cases are left out?
- Over which period and how many cases?
- Compared with what: last month, a target or another team?

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented numbers. After a chat, 50 customers answer a survey. 30 give a 5, 10 give a 4, 6 give a 3, and 4 give a 1 or 2. Satisfied answers: 30 + 10 = 40. CSAT = 40 / 50 × 100 = 80 percent. Last month it was 86 percent from 20 answers. The drop looks big, but the earlier number rests on very few answers, so the team keeps watching instead of changing the process at once.

## Key takeaways

- An SLA states agreed service quality, including response and resolution time frames.
- Response time is the start of work, resolution time is the fix.
- CSAT is the share of 4 and 5 ratings among all responses.
- Ask what is counted, over which period and compared with what.
