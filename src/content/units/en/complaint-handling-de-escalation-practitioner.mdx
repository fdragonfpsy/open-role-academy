## Why this matters

Some customers arrive already angry. If you answer anger with a defence, it grows. If you answer with attention and a clear offer, it often drops. This unit gives you techniques and a way to choose a remedy.

## Techniques that calm

De-escalation means reducing a conflict step by step or preventing it. Conversation techniques said to work in this direction include:

- Active listening: let the person finish, then restate the core in your own words.
- I-messages: say "I want to understand this correctly" instead of "You are not clear".
- Non-violent communication: separate observation, feeling, need and request.

They work together with calm, non-aggressive wording and avoidance of negatively loaded words.

## Choosing how to handle the conflict

Conflict can be handled in several styles: asserting, yielding, appeasing, avoiding, compromising, or solving the problem cooperatively. A conflict is only resolved when its core has been found and worked on. In service, cooperative, problem-oriented handling is the default; yielding on everything or avoiding the issue only moves the problem. Our own rule: first find out what the customer needs, then decide which style fits.

## Remedies within your authority

The response to a complaint can be material, for example fulfilling statutory warranty claims, or non-material, such as an apology. Know your limits: what you may offer alone, and what needs approval. Whether a customer has a legal claim in a specific case is a question for legal counsel or your legal team; you do not decide it. Poorly defined standards cause trouble too: if the policy says only "answer quickly", staff interpret it differently, so ask for concrete numbers.

## Three-part reply (our own)

Our own structure for written replies: name the issue in the customer's own words, own the part that is ours without blaming others, and state the next step with a date. Escalation to a senior colleague follows defined thresholds such as missing authority or an exceeded time limit, not the volume of the customer's voice.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer calls: "This is the second time my order is late and nobody tells me anything." Agent: "So you have waited twice and heard nothing. I would be annoyed too. Let me check the order now." After checking: "The parcel is delayed at the depot; I can send a replacement today at no cost. I will email you the tracking number within the hour." The agent listened, restated, found the core (no information) and used an authority limit of replacement orders.

## Key takeaways

- Active listening, I-messages and calm wording reduce tension.
- Find the core of the problem; prefer cooperative problem solving.
- Offer remedies within your authority; legal claims are for legal counsel.
- Name the issue, own your part, state the next step with a date.
