## Why this matters

An order commits your company to a purchase, and an invoice asks it to pay. If the two do not fit together, you either pay for things you never ordered or received, or you annoy suppliers with late payments. The link between order, delivery and invoice is a core part of the purchase-to-pay process.

## The purchase order

In business, an order is the binding request of a customer to conclude a purchase contract. In Germany it is legally an offer by the buyer; the contract comes into being when the seller accepts. An order confirmation from the seller is not legally required but is usual, and you should read it. Our own checklist for a complete order: order number, supplier, item and description, quantity, agreed price, delivery date and place, payment terms and a contact person.

## Goods receipt

When the delivery arrives, goods acceptance checks it physically against the transport papers and the delivery note. The goods receipt inspection follows and checks quality; faulty deliveries are rejected at once. Finally the receipt is booked in the system. The delivery note accompanies the goods and gives the buyer information and a means of control; it is not an invoice, which can travel separately. Quantity differences and damage are noted on the day of delivery.

## Invoice and accounts payable

An invoice is the document that bills a delivery or service. It must show which amount is claimed for which delivery so that the debtor can verify it. In accounts payable, the invoice is checked, posted, paid and filed; the department works closely with purchasing.

## The three-way match (our own description)

Compare three documents before releasing payment:

- Purchase order: what we ordered, at what price.
- Goods receipt: what actually arrived.
- Invoice: what the supplier bills.

Items, quantities and prices must agree, within a tolerance your company sets. If they do not, the invoice is blocked and the buyer and accounts payable clarify it with the supplier.

## Separate the roles

The four-eyes principle means that certain steps may only be performed with at least two persons involved, to reduce the risk of errors and misuse. In purchasing, the person who orders should not also confirm receipt and release payment alone.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented supplier and figures. A purchase order is for 100 units at 12.50 euros, or 1,250 euros. The warehouse confirms 100 units received. Invoice 1 bills 100 units at 12.50 euros, 1,250 euros: all three documents agree and payment is released. Invoice 2 from another supplier bills the same quantity at 12.80 euros, 1,280 euros. The price differs from the order, so the invoice is blocked. The buyer checks whether a price change was agreed; if not, the supplier is asked for a corrected invoice or a credit note.

## Key takeaways

- A purchase order is a binding request; keep it complete and in writing.
- Goods receipt checks quantity and quality against the delivery note and is booked in the system.
- The three-way match compares order, receipt and invoice before payment is released.
- Different people order, receive and pay; mismatches are blocked and clarified.
