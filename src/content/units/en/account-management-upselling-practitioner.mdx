## Why this matters

Existing customers are the base of recurring revenue. The OpenStax chapter on CRM states that it is typically easier and less expensive to retain a loyal customer than to acquire a new one. Account managers therefore protect the relationship first and grow it second.

## Account plan

An account plan is a short working document for one customer: the customer's goals and situation, what they use today, the people (sponsor, users, buyer, blockers), open issues, opportunities and a list of next steps with owners and dates. Update it after important contacts. It keeps the relationship from depending on one person's memory and makes handovers between colleagues possible.

## Upselling and cross-selling

The Wikipedia articles define **upselling** as inviting a customer to buy more expensive items, upgrades or add-ons, and **cross-selling** as selling an additional product or service to an existing customer. Both work when they solve a need you have actually observed, for example a team that keeps hitting the user limit. They fail when they are driven by your quota alone. Ask yourself: would the customer say thank you if they understood the offer fully? A proposal that starts with the customer's usage data, not with your price list, is usually stronger.

## Retention and churn signals

The Wikipedia article on customer retention describes retention as the ability to keep customers over a period. Warning signs include falling usage, a sponsor who leaves, repeated unresolved support issues, slow replies, a customer asking for the contract end date or comparing alternatives. Do not wait for the renewal date. Contact the customer early, listen, resolve open issues and involve support or customer success where needed.

## Renewals

Start the renewal conversation well before the term ends; the exact lead time depends on your product and contract. Review what value the customer got, check changes in their needs and propose the renewal together with sensible adjustments. Never hide an automatic renewal or price change: state them plainly, and check your company's rules and the contract with legal where you are unsure.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A customer on a team plan has a sponsor who changed roles, and two support tickets about reporting are still open. Usage of the reporting module has dropped. In your account plan you mark the account as at risk. You speak with the new sponsor, agree with support to resolve the tickets, and show the usage trend. Then you see that another department has asked about the product. You offer a pilot for that department as a cross-sell, with an adjusted renewal price. The renewal is signed after the tickets are closed.

## Key takeaways

- Retaining customers is usually cheaper than winning new ones.
- Keep a live account plan with people, issues and next steps.
- Upsell and cross-sell only on observed needs.
- Watch churn signals and act before the renewal date.
- State renewal terms openly.
