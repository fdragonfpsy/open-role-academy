## Why this matters

A discovery conversation is the part of selling where you learn what the customer actually needs before you suggest anything. Products pitched too early meet objections that are really misunderstandings. OpenStax describes the approach stage as one where the seller's role is mainly to ask questions and listen, with a soft approach working better than hard-sell tactics.

## Stated needs and real needs

Customers rarely describe what they need in a ready-made way. OpenStax distinguishes stated needs (what the customer says), real needs (what the statement actually means), unstated needs (expected but not mentioned), delight needs and secret needs. Their example: someone asks for an inexpensive hybrid car. "Inexpensive" can mean low purchase price, low running costs or low monthly payment, and only a good question tells you which. Responding only to the stated need may not satisfy the customer.

## Open and closed questions

The German article on Fragetechnik separates **open questions** (they start with words like what, how, why and invite explanation) from **closed questions** (they can be answered with yes or no, or a choice). Use open questions to explore ("How do you handle this today?") and closed questions to confirm facts ("Is the contract renewed every year?"). Avoid stacking several questions in one breath; the customer will answer the easiest one.

## Active listening

The Active listening article describes intentionally engaging with what is said and giving the speaker confirmation that the point has come across. In practice (practice insight): let the customer finish, take short notes, and repeat the key point in your own words ("So the main issue is that reports take two days, correct?"). Do not plan your next question while the other person speaks.

## Summarise

At the end, summarise the needs in two or three sentences and ask whether it is right. This catches misunderstandings early and shows respect. Write the summary into the CRM.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A small bakery asks you for "a better till system". You ask: "What is not working with the current one?" The owner says closing the day takes an hour and mistakes appear in the stock count. You ask: "How many people use the till, and where does the stock count happen?" (open). Then: "Is the stock count done by hand?" (closed). You summarise: "You want to finish the daily close in minutes and have the stock updated automatically; the budget is not fixed yet. Did I get that right?" She adds that she also wants to see sales per product. Now you know the real need is closing and stock, not just a "better till".

## Key takeaways

- Discovery means learning the customer's situation before pitching.
- Stated needs are only the starting point; probe for the real need.
- Open questions explore, closed questions confirm.
- Listen actively: let them finish, paraphrase, check understanding.
- Summarise and let the customer correct you.
