## Why this matters

People ignore most messages aimed at them. Content that is useful, and copy that is easy to understand, earns attention that a plain sales pitch does not.

## Content marketing versus advertising

The German Wikipedia article describes content marketing as a technique that addresses the target group with informing, advising and entertaining content in order to win or keep customers. In contrast to adverts, banners or spots, such content does not put a positive picture of the company first but offers useful information, knowledge or entertainment. The company shows its competence through the content instead of merely claiming it. Examples are a how-to guide, a checklist or an explainer video.

## Copy in three questions

Copywriting means writing text that helps a reader decide or act. Before you write, answer:

- **Who** reads it, and what do they already know?
- **What one goal** does this text have (learn something, sign up, ask for advice)?
- **What is the next step**, stated as one clear call to action?

A headline should say what the reader gets. Describe benefits in the reader's words rather than listing features. Short sentences, plain words and one idea per paragraph are a safe default; these are our own writing rules, not a quote from a source.

## Same message everywhere

OpenStax describes integrated marketing communications as sending the same message through different promotion methods. A blog post, an email and a social post can each use their own format, but the core message should match.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A local tax-advice office wants more enquiries. A first draft says 'We are a leading firm with many years of experience.' It is about the office, not the reader. The revised content piece is a checklist, 'Documents you need for your tax return', with a short intro in plain language. The call to action says: 'Download the checklist or book a 20-minute first call.' Audience: employees who file for the first time. Goal: first contact.

## Key takeaways

- Content marketing leads with useful information; it shows expertise instead of claiming it.
- Before writing, fix audience, one goal and one next step.
- Headlines and calls to action say what the reader gets.
- Different formats, same core message.
