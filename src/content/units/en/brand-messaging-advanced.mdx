## Why this matters

Senior brand work is about judgement: which gap limits the brand, which evidence you trust, and which claims you should not make even though they would sell.

## Keller's pyramid as a diagnostic

OpenStax summarises Kevin Lane Keller's brand equity model as four levels. First **identity**: who are you and what need do you solve? Second **meaning**: how well does the offer perform, and does it meet social and psychological needs? Third **feelings and judgements**: what do customers think and conclude? Top: **resonance**, the degree of connection, engagement and loyalty. Use the levels as a diagnosis from bottom to top. If people cannot say who you are, loyalty campaigns will not help.

## Brand equity and its limits

OpenStax defines brand equity as the additional value a brand has over a substitute; if buyers pay more for one similar product because of the brand, the difference is its equity. It also says there is no single measure of brand value, so valuation is subjective and may draw on visibility, loyalty and perception alongside financial measures. Practical consequence: report several indicators together and state their limits, instead of promising one 'brand score'.

## Standardise or adapt

A strong name should translate effectively into other languages, says OpenStax. The same applies to messages: a joke, colour or value statement may carry different meaning in another market. A reasonable rule, our own: keep the promise and proof fixed, and adapt examples, tone and channel to the market, validated with local speakers.

## Claims and EU law

Directive 2005/29/EC prohibits misleading commercial practices in business-to-consumer dealings. Under Article 7(2) a practice is also misleading when the trader fails to identify its commercial intent where this is not apparent from the context. Brand statements such as 'the most sustainable choice' are factual claims once a consumer can test them; keep evidence on file. Germany implements this in its competition law; ask legal counsel for the national detail.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A tea brand's research shows high awareness but weak resonance. Keller's levels point to the meaning level: customers cannot say how the tea differs. The team picks one verified attribute, a documented origin, as proof for a benefit message. The campaign claim 'best tea in Europe' is dropped because it cannot be substantiated.

## Key takeaways

- Diagnose bottom-up with the four levels before choosing tactics.
- Brand value cannot be reduced to one number; combine indicators and state their limits.
- Fix promise and proof, adapt expression per market.
- Keep evidence for every testable claim; do not hide commercial intent.
