## Why this matters

At practitioner level you run a search from start to finish and are the person who keeps it fair and consistent. The aim is a process in which every candidate is assessed against the same prepared criteria. This unit has no retention periods, thresholds or section numbers; for those, check current law, your data protection officer (DPO) or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## Write the ad from the profile

Start from the requirement profile and the job description. The English article says a job description is often used in the recruitment process and helps candidates judge whether they are suitable. Take over only requirements that are needed for the job and separate essential from nice to have. Describe tasks in plain language, name the practical facts the German article lists (working hours, place of work, intended pay, qualifications) and say how to apply. For inclusive wording, describe what the person will do instead of the type of person you imagine, and use wording that addresses everyone. Where a German ad needs a gender-neutral form, follow your company's current wording guidance and check it with legal counsel; do not copy phrases from other ads unseen.

## Sourcing channels and their trade-offs

The English article lists channels such as job portals, newspapers, social media, career fairs, job centres and recruitment agencies, and distinguishes internal from external recruitment. The German article adds the difference between passive recruitment (handling unsolicited applications and building the employer's reputation) and active recruitment (targeted advertising, agencies, public employment services). Each channel has a trade-off. Internal recruitment is familiar and can be quicker; external recruitment widens the pool and can bring fresh ideas. Employee referrals can improve fit, but the English article warns that relying only on them may reduce diversity. Employer branding, described there as an employer's reputation as a place to work, supports all channels, so keep what you promise in the ad consistent with reality.

## Screen against criteria

Prepare a screening sheet before reading the first application. List each essential criterion from the profile and how you will recognise it in the documents. The German article describes sorting applications by priorities and by must-have and nice-to-have criteria. Apply the same sheet to every application, note the evidence for each rating, and leave out information that has no bearing on the job. If you use an applicant tracking system or other software that filters or ranks, remember that the English article says it can filter candidates; ask what it does and involve your DPO and legal counsel before relying on it.

## Structured interviews

The English article on job interviews describes structure as the degree to which interviews are identical across applicants. Content elements it lists include basing questions on a job analysis, asking all interviewees the same questions and limiting follow-ups. Evaluation elements include rating each answer separately, using anchored rating scales, taking detailed notes, and having the same interviewers rate every applicant. It names two common question types: situational questions, which ask what someone would do in a described situation, and behavioural questions, which ask about what the person has done before. The German article notes that interviews structured around suitability criteria can improve predictive quality, but are used rarely because of the extra effort.

A simple rubric: for each question, write down what a weak, an adequate and a strong answer looks like before the interviews start. Score each answer right after it is given.

## Candidate communication

Tell candidates what to expect, who they will meet and when they will hear from you. The German article notes a written acceptance or rejection after the decision. Be honest and courteous in both cases and avoid promising what you cannot decide.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real figures. You hire for a coordination role. Step 1: turn the profile into four essential criteria. Step 2: write the ad around tasks and these criteria. Step 3: build a screening sheet with the criteria and evidence columns. Step 4: prepare three behavioural questions, one per key criterion, each with a three-level answer guide. Step 5: brief the interviewers to ask all questions in the same order and to score each answer straight away. Step 6: after the interviews compare scores per criterion, record the reasons and send every candidate a clear message.

## Key takeaways

- Write the ad from the requirement profile; include only job-relevant requirements.
- Every sourcing channel has trade-offs; relying on one can narrow the pool.
- Screen with a prepared sheet and apply it to every application.
- Structured interviews use the same questions and a scoring guide, rated per answer.
- Keep candidates informed and keep records. For legal details check current law, your DPO or legal counsel.
