## From statement to ratio

At this level you take figures from the income statement and balance sheet and calculate the ratios yourself. Write down the formula first, then the inputs, then the result with its unit. The Financial ratio article notes that statements' data depends on the accounting method and standards used, so always note which statements and which period the inputs come from.

## Profitability

- **Gross margin** = (revenue minus cost of goods sold) divided by revenue, times 100. The Financial ratio article lists gross margin as net sales minus COGS, divided by net sales.
- **Net margin** = net profit divided by revenue, times 100. The Profit margin article stresses that it is calculated with revenue as the base. A *markup* uses cost as the base instead, so a markup and a margin on the same sale are different numbers.
- **Return on equity (ROE)** = net income divided by equity. The Return on equity article describes it as a year's net income divided by total equity (after preferred dividends and excluding preferred shares), shown as a percentage. Many analysts use average equity, others use equity at the start or end of the year. State which one you used.

## Liquidity

- **Current ratio** = current assets / current liabilities.
- **Quick ratio** = (current assets minus inventory) / current liabilities. The Current ratio article says the quick ratio excludes current assets that may not be easily liquidated, like prepaid expenses and inventory; some sources also subtract other items, so write your definition down.

## Capital structure

- **Equity ratio** = equity / total assets. The Equity ratio article says that, when liabilities and equity are the only sources of financing, equity ratio and debt ratio are complementary: a higher equity ratio means a lower proportion of assets financed by debt.

## Pitfalls

1. **Definitions differ.** The Financial ratio article warns that there is no international standard for calculating the summary data in financial statements and that terminology is not always consistent between companies, industries, countries and time periods. Compare like with like.
2. **Accounting methods differ.** Ratios may not be directly comparable between companies that use different accounting methods or standards.
3. **Context matters.** The Current ratio article says acceptable values vary across industries, and that a low value can be justified if a business collects cash from customers well before it pays suppliers. Do not apply a rule of thumb without context.
4. **One ratio is not a diagnosis.** The Return on equity article notes that predicting a company's stock value from ROE alone depends on too many other factors to be useful.

## Choosing KPIs

The Key performance indicator article says that the selection of KPIs depends on an organisation's priorities and context. Start with the question: "Can we pay our bills?" points to liquidity; "Do we earn enough on sales?" points to margins; "How much is financed by debt?" points to the equity ratio. Pick two or three ratios for the question instead of a long list.

## Worked example

Illustration only; all amounts are invented. Revenue 800,000; cost of goods sold 480,000; net income 48,000; current assets 250,000 including inventory 100,000; current liabilities 200,000; total assets 1,000,000; equity at the start of the year 350,000 and at the end 450,000.

- Gross margin = (800,000 - 480,000) / 800,000 x 100 = 40%.
- Net margin = 48,000 / 800,000 x 100 = 6%.
- Current ratio = 250,000 / 200,000 = 1.25.
- Quick ratio = (250,000 - 100,000) / 200,000 = 0.75.
- Equity ratio (end of year) = 450,000 / 1,000,000 = 45%.
- ROE with average equity (350,000 + 450,000) / 2 = 400,000: 48,000 / 400,000 = 12%. With opening equity it would be about 13.7%, with closing equity about 10.7%.

The same profit gives three different ROE values depending on the definition, which is why you always state it.

## Key takeaways

- Write formula, inputs, result and unit every time.
- Gross margin and net margin use revenue as the base; a markup uses cost.
- ROE and other averages depend on which equity figure you use; say which.
- Definitions, accounting methods and industry context differ; compare like with like.
- Pick a few KPIs that answer the actual question.
