## Why this matters

At practitioner level you meet cases where the answer is not a plain yes or no: a defect after eight months, a withdrawal that arrives late, a refund that the customer says is incomplete. This unit gives you the structure behind these cases at topic level for the EU, with Germany as the national context. It is not legal advice. National law implements the directives and may differ in details, so this unit gives no section numbers or national deadlines. Ask legal counsel for those.

## Information before the contract

Before a consumer is bound by a distance contract, the trader must give clear information, for example the main characteristics of the goods, its identity and address, the total price including taxes and delivery costs, and the information about the right of withdrawal. If the trader did not give the withdrawal information, the consequences for the consumer are milder: the withdrawal period then expires 12 months after the end of the initial period, and the consumer is not liable for loss in value of the goods. So a missing or unclear notice in your shop is a reason to escalate, not to argue.

## Lack of conformity: time limits and burden of proof

The Sale of Goods Directive makes the seller liable for a lack of conformity that existed at delivery and becomes apparent within two years. For a defect that appears within one year, the law presumes that it existed at delivery, unless the seller proves otherwise or the presumption does not fit the goods or the defect. Member States may choose longer periods, for example a two-year presumption, and may require the consumer to notify the seller within a set period of at least two months after noticing the defect. This is why national details matter and why you should not state a deadline yourself.

## Order of remedies

- First, the consumer can choose between repair and replacement, unless the chosen one is impossible or disproportionate for the seller.
- Repair or replacement is free of charge, within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience.
- Price reduction or termination become possible, for example, when the seller has not completed the repair or replacement or when the defect appears despite an attempt to fix it.

The seller is the consumer's contact for these remedies. A commercial guarantee comes on top: it binds the guarantor under the guarantee statement and the advertising, and the statement must be given on a durable medium in plain language.

## Withdrawal cases that often get stuck

- Refund: the trader refunds with the same means of payment and is not required to refund extra costs if the consumer chose a delivery more expensive than the cheapest standard one.
- Condition: the consumer is liable for a loss in value only if the goods were handled beyond what is needed to establish their nature, characteristics and functioning.
- Proof: the consumer has to prove that the withdrawal was sent in time, so keep the date and channel of every message.
- Exceptions: personalised goods or opened sealed hygiene goods may be excluded; the decision belongs to legal staff.

## Triage a case (our own)

Our own triage note has four lines: contract type (distance or in shop), key dates (order, delivery, first problem, message), customer wish in their words, and facts still missing. Add the product and any guarantee document. Then say what the customer can expect, for example "our legal team will check this and reply by a named day", and refer.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented shop. On day 9 a customer reports that her blender stopped working. She asks for a refund because "the guarantee is only for repair". The agent notes the dates and the symptom, explains that a guarantee is an extra promise and that statutory rights exist besides it, and refers the case with the triage note. She does not promise a refund or deny one.

## Key takeaways

- A missing withdrawal notice extends the withdrawal period and removes liability for loss in value.
- The seller is liable for defects present at delivery; a presumption helps the consumer in the first period.
- Remedies follow an order: repair or replacement first, then price reduction or termination in listed cases.
- National deadlines differ; do not state them, refer the case with a triage note.
