## Why this matters

Legal work produces many documents: contracts, advice notes, correspondence, approvals. If they are scattered or unclear, nobody can say later what was agreed, by whom and on which basis. Legal operations is the practical side of keeping this in order. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Matter and contract files

The articles on contract management describe managing contracts from creation to execution, including documenting changes. A tidy file for a matter or contract typically holds: a unique reference, a short description, the owner, the parties, the final signed document, relevant drafts and approvals, key dates and a note of what happened. Put one reference on everything that belongs together.

## Document management and records

A document management system stores, shares and tracks electronic documents; some systems also keep a history of versions, as the Wikipedia article explains. The articles on records management (and the German "Schriftgutverwaltung") describe the systematic recording of business transactions and results. For you this means: save documents in the agreed place, not in personal folders or e-mail only.

## Naming, versions and templates

- **Naming:** agree a simple pattern (reference, short title, date, status) and use it consistently.
- **Versions:** mark drafts and final documents clearly; never overwrite the signed version.
- **Templates:** use approved templates for standard documents and report errors to the template owner instead of changing your copy silently.

## Retention periods

A retention period is the time for which documents must be kept (the German article on Aufbewahrungsfrist introduces the concept). The length depends on the type of document and on the applicable rules, which differ; do not guess. Ask your legal or records contact for the retention schedule. Retention also has an end: documents are not kept forever without reason, especially if they contain personal data.

## Confidentiality

Legal files often contain sensitive information. The general idea of confidentiality is that information is only available to those who are authorised. Share on a need-to-know basis, mark sensitive documents, and use the approved system instead of private e-mail or messaging apps.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An invented company signs a supplier contract. The legal assistant creates a file with reference "C-0042", saves the signed PDF as "C-0042_Supplier_signed", keeps earlier drafts in a "drafts" folder, and enters the end date in the contract register. Access is limited to the legal team and the contract owner. When the same type of contract comes up again, she uses the approved template rather than the old file.

## Key takeaways

- A good file has a reference, an owner, the signed version, key dates and the history.
- Consistent naming, clear versions and approved templates prevent errors.
- Retention periods depend on the document type and rules; ask for the schedule.
- Keep confidential material in the approved system and share on a need-to-know basis.
