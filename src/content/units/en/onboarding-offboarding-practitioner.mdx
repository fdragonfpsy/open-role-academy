## Why this matters

At practitioner level you no longer just tick a checklist; you coordinate people and systems so that a start or an exit runs smoothly. This unit contains no probation periods, notice periods, deadlines or section numbers: these depend on current law, collective agreements, works agreements and the case. Check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## A first-weeks plan

The German article on Einarbeitung says an onboarding plan sets out the order of tasks, time sections, criteria for when a task is mastered and the qualifications aimed at. Its scope depends on the job, and it should be worked out together by the specialist supervisor and the new employee, taking individual progress into account. The German onboarding article adds that a schedule for the first weeks is best communicated in advance. A useful plan has:

- a few **goals** per stage, written so that you can tell afterwards whether they were reached;
- named **people** to meet and **topics** to learn;
- **check-ins** between manager and newcomer, with HR available as a second contact.

## Coordinating IT, facilities and the manager

You are the conductor. Typical hand-offs: the manager defines the role's tasks and the buddy; IT creates accounts and equipment; facilities prepare the workplace and building access; payroll and personnel administration receive the data they need. Write down **who does what by when** relative to the start date, and track it in one place. The access-control article describes access control as selective restriction of access; in practice, request only the access the role needs and review it when the role changes.

## Feedback loops

The English onboarding article says newcomers seek information and feedback to learn what is expected. Give them regular opportunities: short check-ins, and a conversation at the end of the plan about what worked and what is still open. The German article describes feedback as clarifying what was achieved, what is open and what new goals follow. Ask the newcomer what was confusing, and pass patterns on to the people who own the process.

## The exit process

The offboarding article lists typical parts: transfer of knowledge, return of property such as devices, badges and keys, ending accounts and access, redirecting mail, and final administrative paperwork. A good exit process turns this into a checklist with owners:

- **Handover**: open tasks, contacts, file locations and status of ongoing work, documented for a named successor.
- **Access removal**: ask IT to end accounts and rights on the agreed date; the German offboarding article describes safe deactivation and documentation of access rights as a purpose.
- **Return of equipment**: record what was returned.
- **Reference**: the German article on Arbeitszeugnis distinguishes a simple reference (type of job and duration) from a qualified one (also performance and conduct) and names principles such as clarity, truthfulness and benevolence within truthfulness. Whether, when and how a reference must be issued is a question of current law and company rules: check them, and involve legal counsel in doubt.

## Conducting an exit interview

The exit-interview article describes it as a survey given to someone leaving, used to learn why people leave and what to change. It cautions that people may not be candid if the interviewer is part of the reason for leaving. So: offer the conversation, do not force it; explain how feedback is used; choose a suitable interviewer; listen without defending; and record themes rather than gossip. Treat what you hear as confidential and as one source among several.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. "Employee C" has resigned. You agree the last working day with the manager and confirm it in writing as your process requires. You open the exit checklist: the manager names a successor and C documents open tasks and contacts; you ask IT to end access on the final day; C returns the laptop and badge, which you record. You offer an exit interview, explain that the notes are confidential and used in aggregate, and ask for a reference request in writing; the reference text is prepared according to company rules and checked by the responsible person. You say thank you on the last day.

## Key takeaways

- A first-weeks plan has goals, people, topics and check-ins, agreed with the newcomer.
- Coordinate IT, facilities and the manager with owners and dates in one place.
- Exit: handover, access removal, return of equipment, reference; with respect.
- Exit interviews are voluntary, confidential and one input among several.
- Legal details: check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel.
