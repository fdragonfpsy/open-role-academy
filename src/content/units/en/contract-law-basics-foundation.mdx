## Why this matters

Legal & Compliance staff see contracts every day: supplier agreements, customer terms, service agreements. To review them you first need to know what a contract is and how it comes into existence. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## What a contract is

The Wikipedia article on contracts describes a contract as an agreement between parties that creates obligations the law will enforce. The German article on Vertrag adds that a contract typically arises through matching declarations of intent by two or more parties. In Germany contracts are a matter of private law, and parties are generally free to decide whether to contract and with whom, within the limits of mandatory law.

## Offer and acceptance

The usual path is simple: one party makes an **offer**, the other **accepts** it. The English article on offer and acceptance explains that an offer shows a willingness to be bound on certain terms, and acceptance must agree to those terms. If the other side answers with changed terms, that is generally treated as a new offer rather than an acceptance. Whether a message is already an offer, or only an invitation to make one (for example a catalogue or a price list), depends on the circumstances and on how the statement was meant to be understood.

## Form

Many contracts can be concluded orally, in writing or by conduct. Some types require a particular form for the contract to be valid. Which ones do is a question for the statute text and for counsel; as a rule, document what was agreed so you can prove it later.

## Typical contract types

- **Sale:** goods against payment. The German article on Kaufvertrag describes the basic exchange of ownership and price.
- **Service and work contracts:** one party performs activities or achieves a result for payment.
- **Lease and rental:** use of an item for a period against payment.
- **Employment contracts:** a special type with extra protective rules.
- **Framework agreements:** rules for future individual orders.

The type matters because statutes attach different rights and duties to it.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented companies and goods. A company sends a written quote for 100 office chairs at a stated price. The customer replies: "We accept, but delivery two weeks later." Is a contract formed? Reading the exchange, the customer changed a term; the quote has not simply been accepted. Treat the reply as a new offer that the supplier can accept or reject. Record the exchange, the dates and which terms were open. If the value is high, ask counsel.

## Key takeaways

- A contract usually arises from offer and acceptance; changed terms in an answer are generally a new offer.
- Not every statement is an offer; context decides.
- Contract types come with different statutory rights and duties.
- Mandatory law limits what parties can agree.
