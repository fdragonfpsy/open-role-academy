## Why this matters

A plan is only as good as the people and means behind it. Resource management in a project means identifying the participants and other resources, assigning them and using them as efficiently as possible. The work breakdown tells you what has to be done; resource coordination tells you who does it, with what, and when they can.

## What counts as a resource (our own wording)

- People: skills, effort and time.
- Equipment and spaces: test devices, rooms, tools.
- Licences and services: software, external providers.
- Money: budget that pays for the items above.

For each work package, write down which skills it needs, roughly how much effort, and in which period. The sum over all packages is your resource need.

## Roles and one owner per task

Everybody in a project should know what they are expected to do. A responsibility matrix lists tasks in rows and people or roles in columns and marks each cell with a short code: does the work, is answerable for the result, must be asked, or must be informed. Our own rule: every task has exactly one answerable owner. Several people may do the work, but one name carries the result. Two owners means none.

## Availability is not capacity (our own)

A colleague employed full time is not available to the project for the full time. Meetings, other projects, holidays and routine work take part of it. Ask each person for the hours they can really give per week, write that down, and plan with it. Where you cannot choose the people yourself, which happens in many organisations, say early what is missing instead of hoping.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented project. A team relaunches an intranet. Work package "migrate 120 pages" needs a content editor and about 60 hours between 1 and 15 March. The editor, Sam, is employed full time but is already assigned to routine work for two days a week and has holiday in the second week. Sam's real capacity in the period is about 20 hours, not 60. The project lead records the gap of 40 hours, names Lena as answerable owner of the migration, and asks the department head for a second editor for the first two weeks.

## Key takeaways

- Derive the resource need from the work breakdown: skills, effort and period.
- Give every task one answerable owner, even when several people do the work.
- Plan with real capacity, not with employment percentages.
- State gaps early and in numbers.
