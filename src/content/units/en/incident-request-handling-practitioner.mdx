## From single tickets to the flow of work

At practitioner level you handle complex tickets, support colleagues and notice patterns. Four habits make the difference: escalating well, bundling related tickets, sharing knowledge and spotting recurring issues.

## Escalate in time — and completely

Escalation is not a failure; late escalation is. Escalate when a ticket needs expertise or permissions you do not have, or when the **SLA target is at risk**. Hand over a complete picture: symptoms, impact, steps already tried with their results, and what you suspect. The receiving team should never have to start from zero, and the user should know who is now working on the issue.

## Mass incidents: one main ticket

When many users report the same problem within minutes — VPN down, e-mail slow — create **one main incident**, link the user tickets to it and **communicate centrally** (status page, banner, group message). The technical team focuses on one investigation; every user still gets the resolution when the main incident is closed.

## Knowledge you can reuse

If colleagues solve the same problem again and again from scratch, the team is wasting time. A short **knowledge-base article** — symptoms, cause if known, step-by-step solution, date of the last check — lets anyone solve it quickly and consistently. Good articles can also be offered to users as **self-service**. Link the article in tickets so it gets used and improved.

## Recurring incidents point to problems

If the same incident comes back every Monday and is "fixed" by a restart, the restart is a **workaround**, not a solution. Open a **problem record** and start a **root cause analysis**: collect data (when, where, what changed), test hypotheses and look for the cause behind the symptom. Keep restoring service with the workaround until the cause is removed.

## Worked example

Over two weeks, Aylin notices eleven tickets about a scanner that loses its network connection. Each was closed with "restarted scanner". She writes a knowledge-base article with the workaround and opens a problem record. The analysis shows that the scanner's IP address conflicts with a new device. After a fixed address is assigned, the tickets stop.

## Key takeaways

- Escalate early and completely; inform the user.
- Many identical reports → one main incident, central communication.
- Turn repeated solutions into knowledge-base articles.
- Recurring incidents need root-cause analysis, not endless workarounds.
