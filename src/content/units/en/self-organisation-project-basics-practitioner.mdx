## When work becomes a project

A **project** is a temporary undertaking with a defined goal, a start and an end — usually something new, involving several people. Introducing a new ticket system is a project; answering tickets every day is routine work. Projects need a bit more structure, because nobody does them every day.

## A lean project set-up

For small projects, one page is often enough:

- **Goal** (SMART) and why it matters;
- **Scope** — what is included and, just as important, what is not;
- **Milestones** with dates;
- **Roles** — who does what, who decides;
- **Risks** you can already see.

Share it with everyone involved and update it when things change.

## Clear roles with RACI

Many project problems are role problems: everyone thought someone else would do it. A **RACI matrix** lists tasks and assigns **R**esponsible (does the work), **A**ccountable (answerable for the result, signs off), **C**onsulted (gives input) and **I**nformed (kept up to date). Many approaches recommend exactly one "A" per task.

## Making work visible with Kanban

A **Kanban board** shows tasks as cards in columns such as *To do — In progress — Done*. Its two core practices are to **visualise work** and to **limit work in progress (WIP)**. If four people have fifteen tasks "in progress", little gets finished. A WIP limit — for example, at most two tasks per person — reduces multitasking and makes bottlenecks visible.

## Handling changes

Scope, time and cost are linked: more scope needs more time or money, or something else has to go (or quality suffers). When someone asks for an extra feature, do not quietly absorb it. Make the **trade-off visible** and let the accountable person decide.

## Worked example

Ahmed leads the move to a new ticket system. His one-page plan lists goal, scope (no migration of tickets older than two years), four milestones and a RACI table. The team tracks tasks on a board with a WIP limit of two per person. When sales asks for an extra report, Ahmed shows that it would delay go-live by two weeks; the sponsor decides to postpone the report to phase two.

## Key takeaways

- Project = temporary, goal, start and end.
- One-page plan: goal, scope, milestones, roles, risks.
- RACI clarifies who does, who decides, who is asked, who is told.
- Visualise work, limit WIP, make change trade-offs explicit.
