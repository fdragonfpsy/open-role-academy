## Why this matters

Many leads answer every question with a solution. That is fast, but it can leave the person dependent. A coaching approach helps people find their own solutions. This unit is a concept-level introduction.

## Coaching, feedback, directing, mentoring

The Wikipedia article on coaching describes a coach supporting a learner (the coachee) toward specific tasks or objectives, and says coaching differs from mentoring, which focuses on more general goals and overall development. The German article calls coaching a collective term for advisory methods in which the development of one's own solutions is accompanied and encouraged. Compared with that:

- **Feedback** tells someone how their behaviour or result came across. It comes from you.
- **Directing** tells someone what to do. Use it when time is short or the person is new to a task.
- **Coaching** asks questions so the person finds the answer. It fits when the person has the knowledge to get there.

Good leads switch between these on purpose.

## A structure: GROW

The Wikipedia article describes GROW as a simple method for goal setting and problem solving, developed in the United Kingdom and widely used in corporate coaching. It names stages: Goal (where do you want to be?), Reality (where are you now?), Obstacles and Options (what stands in the way, what could you do?) and Way forward (what will you do, when?). The article stresses that several versions exist. Treat GROW as scaffolding, not a script.

## Coaching questions

Use open questions and follow the answer instead of your prepared list. Examples: "What would a good result look like?", "What have you tried so far?", "What else could you do?", "What is the first step?" Avoid disguised advice such as "Don't you think you should...?". Active listening (paraphrasing, reflecting) keeps the conversation on the other person's thinking.

## Limits

Coaching is development support, not treatment. The German article distinguishes coaching from the medical treatment of mental illness. If someone describes problems that go beyond work, point to the right professional or support service, and involve HR where appropriate.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. Selin, a developer, says she struggles to estimate tasks. Her lead Tobias asks: "What would good estimates look like for you?" (Goal). "How do you estimate today?" (Reality). "What gets in the way?" (Obstacles). She says unclear requirements. "What could you try?" (Options). She proposes asking two clarifying questions before estimating. Tobias asks "When will you try it?" and she picks the next planning meeting. Tobias would have switched to directing only if a deadline made time pressure unavoidable.

## Key takeaways

- Coaching asks, feedback informs, directing instructs; choose on purpose.
- GROW gives a simple sequence: goal, reality, obstacles and options, way forward.
- Ask open questions and listen; do not hide advice in questions.
- Coaching is not therapy; know when to refer on.
