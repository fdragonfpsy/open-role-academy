## Why this matters

Once you can gather facts, the harder work starts: choosing between several acceptable answers, knowing your limits and handing over without losing the customer's time.

## Offer options

Often more than one path exists: repair, replace, refund or workaround. Describe each in one line with its consequence (time, effort, cost to the customer) and recommend one. Offering a choice lets the customer decide where their priorities differ, and recommending one saves them from having to analyse. Stay within what your policy allows; say what you cannot offer plainly.

## First-contact resolution and escalation

Our own working definition: a matter is resolved in the first contact when the customer does not need to contact you again about it. Aim for it, but not at any price. Customer support commonly defines escalation: the case goes to more senior or specialised staff when defined thresholds are met. Typical thresholds, which your team should write down, are a topic you lack permission or knowledge for, a time limit exceeded, or a customer who asks for it. Escalating early with a complete summary is better than holding a case you cannot solve.

## The handover summary

Our own template: who the customer is and how to reach them; what they want (need, not only request); facts and steps already tried; what you promised and by when; what the next person must decide or do. Write it so that the customer never has to repeat themselves. Tell the customer who will contact them and when.

## Symptom and cause

Root cause analysis looks for the underlying cause of a problem instead of treating only its visible effects. In support this means: restarting an app may remove the symptom, but if the same fault returns, record the pattern in the ticket so someone can fix the cause. Do not guess; note what is known and what is assumed.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer says the invoice portal rejects their login. The agent resets the password (symptom fixed), but the customer was locked out for the third time this month. The agent offers two options: stay with password plus mail reset, or switch on a second login step. The customer picks the latter. As the cause is a recurring lockout rule the agent cannot change, the case goes to the account team with a summary: customer, three lockouts with dates, steps tried, request to review the rule, and the promise to answer by Thursday.

## Key takeaways

- Present options with consequences and recommend one.
- Resolve in the first contact where you can; escalate when defined thresholds are met.
- A good handover summary means nobody asks the customer twice.
- Record causes and patterns, not just fixes.
