Context: EU and Germany. This unit is orientation, not legal advice; the sources are linked so you can read the primary texts. For real decisions, involve legal, the data protection contact or compliance.

## Why this matters

At practitioner level you work independently: you write offers, run outreach and negotiate. Most ethical problems at this stage are not dramatic. They are small shortcuts under time pressure: a fee mentioned only in a footnote, one more call to a contact who said no, a gift that seemed harmless.

## Misleading and aggressive practices (consumers)

The EU directive on unfair commercial practices covers business-to-consumer selling. It prohibits unfair practices and calls a practice unfair if it breaches professional diligence and materially distorts the decision of the average consumer. Two main groups follow. Misleading actions give false information or deceive through overall presentation, including about price, main characteristics or expected results. Misleading omissions leave out material information the consumer needs, or present it in an unclear or untimely way, or hide the commercial intent. Aggressive practices use harassment, coercion or undue influence. In judging this, the directive looks at timing and persistence, threatening language, exploitation of a specific misfortune, onerous barriers when a consumer wants to end a contract, and threats of action that cannot legally be taken. It also lists practices that are always unfair, such as bait advertising and falsely claiming limited availability. If you sell to business customers, these rules do not automatically apply, but national law and your company policy often set similar expectations. Ask legal which rules apply to your offer.

## Outreach and data

When you use personal data for direct marketing, the data subject may object at any time; after an objection the data must no longer be processed for that purpose, and the right must be shown clearly and separately at the latest at the first communication. Practical consequences: store the objection where every team sees it, check suppression lists before each campaign, and do not treat "a different product" as a loophole. If you plan to use a purchased or inherited list, ask legal or the data protection contact first; a vendor's claim is not an answer.

## Gifts, hospitality and corruption risk

Offering perks to buyers is common in B2B selling, which is why many companies have written limits and approval steps. German criminal law has no single corruption offence; it covers bribery in several offences, including bribery in business dealings, as the German Wikipedia article on corruption lists. You do not need to know the details. You need to know the internal rule: declare, ask before giving, and never link a gift to a decision.

## Conflicts of interest

A conflict exists when a personal interest could influence your professional judgement, for example when a relative's company could profit from your deal. Disclose it early to your manager or compliance. Disclosure is the fix; concealment is the problem.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. You send a consumer a quote with a low monthly price. The 24-month minimum term and a set-up fee appear only in a footnote on the last page. Your manager spots it and asks you to show term and fee next to the price. In the same week an opt-out reaches the CRM from a contact; a colleague on another team wants to call about a different product. You point to the flagged objection and explain that marketing contact is off for this person. Finally, your cousin runs a firm that the customer might hire as a subcontractor. You tell compliance before the selection, and someone else leads that part.

## Key takeaways

- Show material terms clearly next to the price; do not rely on footnotes.
- Persistence, pressure and hurdles to cancelling can make selling aggressive.
- A marketing objection applies across products and teams.
- Ask legal about list provenance; do not rely on vendor claims.
- Declare gifts and personal interests early.
