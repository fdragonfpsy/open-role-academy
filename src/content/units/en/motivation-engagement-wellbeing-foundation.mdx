## Why this matters

Leads cannot motivate people by pressing a button, but they strongly shape the conditions in which motivation grows or fades. They also see overload early. This unit is concept-level education, not medical or legal advice.

## Motivation in brief

The Wikipedia article on motivation describes motivational states by direction (the goal), intensity (the strength) and persistence (how long someone keeps going). It distinguishes intrinsic motivation, which comes from internal factors such as enjoyment and curiosity, from extrinsic motivation, which is driven by external factors such as rewards or avoiding punishment. In work life both exist: people may enjoy the content of a task and still need a fair salary.

## Engagement

The article on employee engagement describes an engaged employee as one who is absorbed by and enthusiastic about their work. It also notes that engagement remains a contested concept. Use it as a pointer to what to look at, such as enthusiasm and effort, not as a score to chase.

## What a lead can influence

These are practical starting points, not proven recipes:

- **Purpose:** explain why a task matters and how it fits the team's goal.
- **Autonomy:** agree on the outcome and let people choose how to reach it where possible.
- **Recognition:** acknowledge good work specifically and promptly.
- **Development:** offer tasks that stretch people and talk about their growth.
- **Fairness and clarity:** clear priorities and fair distribution of work.

## Noticing overload

The Wikipedia article on occupational stress describes it as chronic psychological stress related to one's job and notes that it can be reduced by understanding stressful conditions and taking steps to change them. Possible signals a lead may see: more errors, missed deadlines, withdrawal from the team, irritability, constant overtime. These are signals, not diagnoses. A lead does not diagnose; a lead asks, listens and acts on workload.

## Employer duties (topic level)

In Germany, the German Wikipedia article on Arbeitsschutz describes measures by the employer to prevent accidents and work-related health hazards, and the article on psychische Belastung mentions the employer's duty to determine and assess psychological strain. The details depend on the country and the organisation, so use this only as a topic: ask HR, the occupational safety function or the works council, if there is one, how it is handled in your organisation.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. Karim has delivered fast work for months but lately misses deadlines and stops joining team chats. His lead, Julia, asks in a one-to-one: "How is your workload at the moment?" Karim says he has been covering two roles. Julia does not guess at causes. She and Karim list his tasks, move one to a colleague, postpone another and agree to check in again in two weeks. She also tells him that support through HR is available.

## Key takeaways

- Motivation has direction, intensity and persistence; it can be intrinsic or extrinsic.
- Leads influence conditions: purpose, autonomy, recognition, development, fairness.
- Notice signals of overload and respond with a conversation and workload changes, not a diagnosis.
- Employer duties on health and safety exist; know whom to ask in your organisation.
