## Why this matters

Stock is money lying on a shelf. If there is too little, production or customers have to wait. If there is too much, cash, space and shelf life are wasted. Purchasing sits in the middle: every order you place changes the stock level.

## What inventory is

Inventory is the supply of goods a firm holds for use in production or for sale. Holding some is deliberate: with a large stock a firm can serve most demand and may get quantity discounts. The price is that stock ties up money, costs money to store and can become obsolete. Most companies track their major items in a perpetual inventory, a continuously updated record of stock levels, orders, sales and receipts.

## Three costs to watch

- Holding cost: money tied up, space, handling, loss of value.
- Ordering cost: every order takes work and often has fixed costs, so very frequent small orders are expensive.
- Shortage cost: lost sales, idle production and unhappy customers when stock runs out.

## The flow in a warehouse (our own simple description)

- Receive: check the delivery against the delivery note and the order, then book the goods receipt.
- Put away: store the goods in a known location so that they can be found again.
- Pick: collect the ordered items for a customer or a production order.
- Issue: book the withdrawal, so that the system stock matches the shelf.

## Counting stock

A stock count compares what is physically on the shelf with what the records say. In Germany, the Commercial Code (HGB, section 240) requires merchants to draw up an inventory at the end of each financial year; physical goods are counted, measured or weighed. If the counted stock differs from the book stock, the difference is investigated and then corrected in the books. Details belong to Finance and the auditors; as a buyer you should know that clean booking of receipts and issues makes the count easy.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A small workshop's system shows 200 boxes of screws at 4.00 euros each. The count finds 188. The difference is 12 boxes, or 48 euros. Before anyone adjusts the system, the team checks the last goods receipts and issues: one issue of 12 boxes to a repair job was never booked, so the shelf is right and the record was wrong. The team books the issue and fixes the habit of booking at the shelf.

## Key takeaways

- Inventory is held on purpose, but it ties up money and can become obsolete.
- Watch three costs: holding, ordering and shortage.
- Book every receipt and issue promptly so that the system matches the shelf.
- Investigate count differences before correcting them.
