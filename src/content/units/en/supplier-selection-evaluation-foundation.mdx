## Why this matters

A supplier chosen on gut feeling or the lowest price can cost far more later through late deliveries, defects and extra management effort. Clear criteria make the decision fair, repeatable and easy to explain to colleagues and to the suppliers who lose.

## From search to selection

In business buying the buyer's work follows these stages: search for suppliers (online, trade media, experience of colleagues), ask the qualified ones for proposals, review the proposals on capabilities, reputation, warranties and price, and select. For a large or complex purchase the team may shortlist a few suppliers and invite them to discuss their proposals. After the contract, the supplier's performance is reviewed regularly. The criteria are defined before offers are requested, for example price, technical competence, reliability from earlier experience, financial stability and availability.

## Selection criteria

German sources on supplier rating list typical criteria such as product and service quality, delivery time, reliability and service level, price and payment terms, and sometimes innovation. Some are objective and measurable (price, defect rate, adherence to quantity and date), others subjective and harder to measure (innovation, cooperation, communication). Treat subjective scores with care and write down what each score means.

## Weighted scoring (our own method)

- Knock-out criteria: requirements that must be met, such as a required certificate. A supplier that fails one is out, however high its score.
- Weights: give each remaining criterion a weight, so that all weights add up to 100 percent.
- Scores: rate each supplier on a scale such as 1 to 5, using written definitions.
- Total: multiply score by weight, add up, compare and discuss the result.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented suppliers. A food company needs packaging with food-contact approval. Weights: quality 35 percent, delivery reliability 25, price 25, service 15. Supplier 1 scores 4, 3, 5, 3, giving 3.85. Supplier 2 scores 5, 4, 3, 4, giving 4.10. Supplier 3 scores 3, 5, 4, 2, giving 3.60, but it has no food-contact approval and drops out on the knock-out criterion anyway. Supplier 2 is chosen. Supplier 1 is cheapest but scores lower on delivery, which the team had set as important before it saw any offers.

## Key takeaways

- Define criteria before requesting offers, and check the knock-out requirements first.
- Typical criteria are quality, delivery, price and terms, service and sometimes innovation.
- Objective criteria can be measured; subjective ones need written definitions of the scores.
- Weighted scoring makes the decision repeatable, but the total is a basis for discussion, not an automatic verdict.
