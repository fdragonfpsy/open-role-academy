## Why this matters

Choosing a supplier is a forecast; performance data shows whether the forecast was right. Regular evaluation makes the supplier's performance transparent, forms the basis for audits and lets you act early.

## From criteria to indicators

Indicators should relate to the buyer's goals. In practice they come from several functions: technology, logistics, quality and purchasing, and sometimes innovation. They can be quantitative or qualitative, and they are often measured per material group. Reports serve operational and strategic management, and they can be shared with the supplier so it can derive its own measures.

## Defining indicators precisely

Four common ones, with our own definitions:

- On-time delivery: share of order lines delivered by the confirmed date. Decide whether early deliveries count as on time.
- Quantity adherence: share of order lines delivered in the ordered quantity.
- Quality: defective parts per million delivered parts (ppm), counted after incoming inspection.
- Responsiveness: share of quotation requests answered within an agreed number of working days.

An indicator without a definition invites disputes. Write down the data source, the period and what is excluded, for example deliveries delayed by the buyer's own change request.

## The review routine (our own)

Collect data monthly, review quarterly, and compare with targets agreed in advance. Show the supplier its figures before the meeting, ask for its view, and agree concrete actions with dates. Include qualitative feedback from users, labelled as opinion. For the scorecard itself: keep it to a handful of indicators, and watch for small samples, since three late deliveries out of five say less than thirty out of fifty.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented supplier. In a quarter the supplier delivered 140 order lines: 118 on time (84.3 percent), 131 in full (93.6 percent). Of 24,000 parts inspected, 38 were defective: 38 divided by 24,000 times a million is about 1,583 ppm. Targets are 95 percent on-time, 90 percent in full and 1,000 ppm. Quantity adherence is met; on-time delivery and quality are not. The buyer sends the figures a week before the review, asks for a corrective plan within four weeks and schedules a follow-up check on the next quarter's data.

## Key takeaways

- Derive indicators from the buyer's goals, and define each one in writing.
- On-time delivery, quantity adherence and ppm are simple ratios; the definition decides the value.
- Share results with the supplier and agree actions with dates.
- Keep the scorecard short, and beware of small samples and subjective scores.
