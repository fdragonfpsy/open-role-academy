## Why this matters

At practitioner level you are expected to turn a business direction into a plan that others can carry out. OpenStax describes the role of marketing in strategic planning as helping to bring a customer focus into planning, to gather and analyse information about the current situation, and to identify trends in the environment and judge their impact. A marketing plan is where this ends up as concrete work.

## SWOT: a structured look at the situation

A SWOT analysis sorts findings into four boxes: strengths and weaknesses (inside the company) and opportunities and threats (outside). The value is not the grid but what you conclude. A common mistake is a long list of vague items such as 'good team'. Better: evidence-based statements, for example 'We answer enquiries within one working day; two named competitors take three days.' Then ask: which strength lets us use which opportunity, and which weakness exposes us to which threat? Those pairings become strategic options.

## Objectives and plan structure

Turn the business goal into marketing objectives with a number, a time frame and a measurement. OpenStax notes that there is little agreement on the exact structure of a marketing plan and uses a 12-element structure, starting with an executive summary. Whatever your template, a workable plan answers these questions:

- Where are we now (situation, SWOT)?
- Who do we want to reach (target groups)?
- What do we want to achieve (objectives)?
- What will we do (strategy and actions across the marketing mix)?
- Who does what by when, and with which budget?
- How will we check progress (metrics) and adjust?

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A regional software company sells scheduling software to physiotherapy practices. The business goal is more new customers in the next year. SWOT evidence: strength, customers praise the onboarding call; weakness, the website has little proof of results; opportunity, many practices still use paper calendars; threat, a larger vendor offers a free entry plan. Option derived: use the onboarding strength to run free setup webinars for paper-based practices. Objective: 60 webinar sign-ups per quarter from practices with no software, measured in the registration tool. Each action gets an owner and a date, and the plan lists a monthly check of sign-ups.

## Key takeaways

- SWOT is only useful when its points are evidence-based and combined into options.
- Objectives need a number, a date and a way of measuring.
- A plan assigns actions to owners, with budget and metrics; it is a working document, not a folder item.
- No template is mandatory; check that the questions above are answered.
