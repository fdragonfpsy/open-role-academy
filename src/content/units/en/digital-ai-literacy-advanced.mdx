## From personal skill to team capability

At advanced level you decide or advise on how a team uses digital tools and AI. Your job is less about clever prompts and more about **good decisions**: where AI adds value, where it creates risk, which rules the team needs, and how people build the skills to work with these tools responsibly.

## Assessing a use case

Before introducing an AI tool, work through a short assessment:

- **Value:** Which task gets better or faster? How will you measure it?
- **Data:** Which data will be processed, by whom and where? Is this allowed under data protection rules and company policy?
- **Quality:** How will output be checked? What error rate is acceptable for this task?
- **Failure:** What happens when the tool is wrong — how are errors detected, corrected and learned from?
- **People:** Who is affected, and are they informed?

Tasks where errors are cheap and easy to spot (first drafts, internal summaries) are good starting points. Decisions about people or legally binding statements need much stricter controls.

## Governance that works in practice

A useful team guideline answers four questions: **which tools** are approved, **which data** may be entered, **when results must be checked**, and **who is accountable**. Keep it short, explain the reasons and review it when tools or rules change.

**Human oversight** must be real. If a person has to approve every AI suggestion without time to read it, oversight exists only on paper. Define when humans review, sample outcomes regularly, keep logs, and make overriding the system easy and documented.

## Building team skills

The European Commission's **DigComp** framework, published by its Joint Research Centre, describes digital competence in five competence areas with 21 competences and several proficiency levels. It is vendor-neutral and well suited for self-assessments, development conversations and training plans — including the responsible use of AI.

## Worked example

Mira, head of a service team, wants AI-drafted customer replies. She runs a four-week pilot with internal test cases, defines that every reply is reviewed before sending, removes personal data from prompts using an approved tool, and tracks quality. After the pilot she writes a one-page guideline and plans DigComp-based training for the team.

## Key takeaways

- Assess value, data, quality, failure and people before rollout.
- Guidelines: tools, data, checks, accountability.
- Oversight needs time, monitoring and a real ability to override.
- Use public frameworks like DigComp to develop team skills.
