## Why this matters

Once the basic cycle runs, the daily work is about exceptions and choices: which path should this purchase take, what do we do when the invoice does not match, and should we make or buy at all?

## Three kinds of purchase

Business buying is not one process. A straight rebuy (reordering toner from the usual supplier) needs only a quick order. A modified rebuy (replacing outdated equipment, changing specifications) needs more steps, and a new task (a first accounting system, a new machine) runs through the full path from need description to supplier selection and later review. Match the effort to the type: a framework contract and a catalogue for the first, quotes for the second, a structured project for the third. A framework contract (Rahmenvertrag) fixes terms in advance, so the individual order only names item, quantity and delivery date.

## Invoice exceptions (our own rules of thumb)

Invoices that do not match order and receipt need a clear routine instead of ad hoc emails:

- Price differs from the order: compare with the contract price, ask the supplier for a credit note or corrected invoice.
- Quantity differs: check the goods receipt, pay only what arrived.
- No order exists: send the invoice to the person who caused the cost to clarify whether it was authorised.
- Duplicate invoice: flag it before payment.

Many companies agree a small tolerance (for example a few euros or a small percentage) under which a difference is paid automatically. Set the tolerance with finance and review it, because it is a control and a cost trade-off at once.

## Make or buy: relevant costs

When a company can produce a part itself or buy it, a comparison of full unit costs misleads. Only costs that change with the decision are relevant: avoidable costs count, unavoidable fixed costs (which remain if you buy) do not. Qualitative factors can then overrule the numbers: quality, whether the supplier can deliver the quantity, whether the price stays stable, and how dependent you become. German usage calls this Eigenfertigung oder Fremdbezug, and the criteria include transaction costs, reliability of supply and dependence.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented numbers. A company makes a part at full cost of 4.10 euro per unit: 2.40 variable cost and 1.70 allocated fixed overhead, of which only 0.30 would disappear if production stopped. A supplier offers it at 2.95 euro. Full cost suggests buying saves 1.15 euro per unit. Relevant cost of making is 2.40 + 0.30 = 2.70 euro, so for 50,000 units making costs 135,000 euro and buying 147,500 euro; making is 12,500 euro cheaper. Before deciding, add the qualitative view: the supplier has no history yet, so the team asks for a trial batch.

## Key takeaways

- Match the purchasing path to the type: straight rebuy, modified rebuy or new task.
- Handle invoice exceptions by rule, and agree tolerances with finance.
- Compare make and buy on relevant (avoidable) costs, not on allocated full cost.
- Quality, capacity, price stability and dependence can overrule a cost comparison.
