## Why this matters

A project is temporary: it has a start and a clear end. Without a deliberate closing, projects fade out. Open points are forgotten, nobody is sure whether the customer has accepted the result, people stay booked on work that no longer exists, and the experience gained is lost when the team moves on. Closing is the last project phase, and it deserves planned time and a named owner.

## What closing includes

German project-management literature describes closing as a sequence of four steps. Here is the list as our own checklist:

- Acceptance: the result passes a test that was planned in advance, ideally run by someone who did not build it.
- Closure analysis: compare the plan with what actually happened in time, cost, scope and quality, and note the reasons for differences.
- Experience capture: collect lessons learned in a form that others can find.
- Dissolution: release the people and resources, so they can move to new work.

If a project is stopped before its goals are reached, this is usually called a project termination. It should still be closed in an orderly way.

## Acceptance and handover

Acceptance means the person or body that ordered the result checks it against the agreed criteria and confirms it. Write down who handed over, who took over, on what date and which points are still open. A written record prevents later disputes about what was agreed. At handover, also agree who looks after the result afterwards, for example fixing defects or giving support.

## Lessons learned: our own five questions

A lessons-learned session is the systematic collection of experiences, mistakes and tips from a project, so that later projects can use them. It is often run as a workshop in which participants report positive and negative experiences. The content is not standardised, so choose a simple structure. Our own five questions:

- What did we plan?
- What actually happened?
- Why was there a difference?
- What do we want to keep doing?
- What will we do differently next time?

Invite the people who did the work, not only the lead, and keep the tone about the process, not about blaming individuals.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented project. A team has relaunched an intranet page. At closing, 10 of 12 acceptance criteria are met; two minor points are listed as open and assigned to the web team, who also agree to give support for the next three months. The handover is recorded in a one-page note signed by both sides. In a 60-minute session the team answers the five questions and writes down three lessons, for example "request the photo rights before the design starts". The notes are saved in the shared project folder, and the team members return to their regular work.

## Key takeaways

- Closing is a planned phase: acceptance, closure analysis, experience capture and dissolution.
- Record acceptance and handover in writing, including open points and who provides support afterwards.
- Lessons learned only help if they are collected from the people who did the work and stored where others can find them.
- Keep the review about the process, not about blame.
