## Why this matters

Every role in a company — from customer service to IT — influences whether the company earns money. Understanding a few basic terms helps you set priorities, follow discussions about budgets and savings, and explain the value of your work.

## Revenue, costs and profit

- **Revenue** (sales, turnover) is what customers pay for products and services.
- **Costs** are what the company spends to deliver them: materials, salaries, rent, software and much more.
- **Profit** is what remains: revenue minus costs. If costs are higher than revenue, the result is a **loss**.

Example: revenue of 500,000 euros and costs of 460,000 euros give a profit of 40,000 euros. High revenue alone says little — a company can sell a lot and still make a loss.

## Fixed and variable costs

- **Variable costs** change with the amount produced or sold — for a bakery, flour and packaging.
- **Fixed costs** stay the same in the short term, whatever the volume — rent, insurance, many salaries.

This distinction matters for many decisions: if the bakery sells one extra loaf, only the variable costs rise; the rent is already paid.

## Budgets

A **budget** is a financial plan for a period, for example for a department and a year. It defines how much may be spent on what. During the year, actual spending is compared with the plan (**plan versus actual**), so that deviations are noticed early. If you are responsible for a cost item — for example travel or software — knowing your budget helps you decide and avoid surprises.

## Your contribution

Every role affects revenue, costs or both. Customer service protects revenue when satisfied customers stay and buy again. IT saves costs when systems run reliably. HR affects costs and productivity through good hiring and low turnover. Being able to name this link makes your proposals more convincing.

## Worked example

Jana in customer service proposes a short FAQ page. She estimates that it could prevent about 200 calls a month. At roughly 5 minutes per call, that frees more than 16 working hours a month — time the team can spend on complex cases. Her manager approves the idea quickly because the benefit is clear.

## Key takeaways

- Profit = revenue − costs; high revenue is not the same as high profit.
- Variable costs move with volume; fixed costs do not (in the short term).
- Budgets plan spending; plan versus actual shows deviations early.
- Know how your role affects revenue and costs.
