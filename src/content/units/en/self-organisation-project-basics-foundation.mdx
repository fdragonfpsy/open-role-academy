## Why this matters

In almost every job, there is more to do than time allows. Requests arrive by e-mail, chat, phone and in passing. Without a simple system, important work gets pushed aside by whatever is loudest. Good self-organisation is not about working more — it is about working on the right things and finishing them.

## One trusted place for tasks

Write every task down in **one place** you trust — a list, a notebook or a task tool — instead of keeping it in your head or scattered across e-mails. When a request is vague ("take care of the supplier issue"), clarify two things before you start: **What is the expected result?** and **By when?** Then note the task in a concrete form: "Send the supplier the corrected order by Wednesday."

## Prioritising: important versus urgent

The **Eisenhower method** sorts tasks along two questions — is it important, is it urgent?

- **Important and urgent:** do it now.
- **Important, not urgent:** plan a fixed time for it (this is where development, planning and prevention live).
- **Urgent, not important:** delegate or handle quickly.
- **Neither:** drop it.

The second group is the most often neglected — and the one that prevents tomorrow's emergencies.

## Clear goals

Goals that are **SMART** are easier to reach and to check: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. "Be faster with e-mails" is a wish. "Reduce the average reply time to customer e-mails from two days to one day by 30 June" is a goal.

## Focus and interruptions

Every interruption costs time to get back into a task. For work that needs concentration:

- **block focus time** in your calendar and tell your team;
- **switch off notifications** for that time;
- **batch small requests** and answer them in a fixed slot.

## Worked example

Sofia starts each day by checking her list and picking the three most important tasks. She blocks 9 to 11 a.m. for the monthly report and answers chat messages at 11. The report, which used to drag on for days, is finished in two mornings.

## Key takeaways

- One trusted list; clarify result and deadline for every request.
- Plan time for important but not urgent work.
- Write goals SMART.
- Protect focus time; batch small requests.
