## Why this matters

Not every way of dividing customers gives a usable segment. At practitioner level you check segments against criteria, choose a targeting approach and turn the choice into a clear promise.

## The ADAMS criteria

OpenStax gives five tests for effective segments:

- **Accessible**: can you reach them at an affordable cost?
- **Differentiable**: are they similar inside and different from other segments?
- **Actionable**: can you do something practical and profitable for them?
- **Measurable**: can you estimate the size?
- **Substantial**: is the group big enough to justify the effort?

A segment such as 'people who feel adventurous' may fail on measurable and accessible: you cannot size it or find it. Behavioural or situational definitions usually pass more easily.

## Targeting approaches

OpenStax describes four generic strategies: undifferentiated (one mix for the whole market), differentiated (separate offers for several segments), concentrated (one segment with one mix, sometimes called niche) and a micro-level option that tailors to very small groups or individuals. Resources decide: several segments need several mixes and budgets.

## Personas

A buyer persona is a semi-fictional representation of an ideal customer. OpenStax suggests elements such as name, age, interests, media usage, finances and brand affinities. Wikipedia notes that personas come from user-experience design. The risk is invention: a persona made up in a workshop reflects the team's assumptions. Base it on interview notes, support tickets and sales data, and mark which parts are assumptions.

## Positioning statement

OpenStax describes positioning as deciding and communicating how the market should think and feel about a product, either head-to-head against competitors or by differentiation. A simple structure: for [target], who need [need], [offer] is a [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative] because [reason to believe]. The final part must be provable.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A language-learning app tests the segment 'ambitious adults'. It fails 'differentiable' and 'measurable'. Instead the team defines 'nurses in Germany preparing for a language exam for work recognition' based on twelve interviews and support questions: accessible through professional forums, measurable through exam registrations, substantial enough for a niche. Concentrated targeting. Persona 'Ana', with sourced notes on shift work and short study windows. Positioning: 'For nurses with irregular shifts, a ten-minute exam practice app, unlike general apps because every exercise uses care vocabulary.'

## Key takeaways

- Test every segment against the ADAMS criteria.
- Choose a targeting approach that your budget can support.
- Build personas from evidence and mark assumptions.
- A positioning statement names target, need, category, benefit, alternative and proof.
