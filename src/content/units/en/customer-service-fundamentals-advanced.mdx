## Why this matters

At a senior level, service quality is not only what agents say. It is the outcome of how the organisation supports them, what it promises and what it does with what customers tell it.

## The service-profit chain

OpenStax describes a model by Harvard researchers from the 1990s. Internal service quality, the support frontline staff get from the rest of the organisation, leads to employee satisfaction. Satisfaction leads to loyalty, productivity and willingness to go the extra mile, which creates value. Value drives customer satisfaction, then customer loyalty, then profit and growth. The book stresses that the links depend on each other and that profit is a result, not the goal. For you this means: slow internal tools, unclear ownership and missing product knowledge show up later as customer dissatisfaction. An employee feedback loop lets frontline staff report such obstacles.

## The services marketing triangle

Three sides must all work. **External marketing** makes promises. **Internal marketing** enables them through training, communication of goals and working conditions. **Interactive marketing** is the service encounter where promises are kept or broken. Review a service by asking for each side what you would see if it failed: overpromising advertisements, agents without answers, or an encounter that contradicts the website.

## Empowerment and its limits

OpenStax defines empowerment as giving employees some autonomy and decision freedom in their daily work and mentions a hotel chain that lets staff spend a fixed amount to solve guest problems without approval. The transferable lesson is not the amount but the logic: weigh the cost of a goodwill gesture against the value of keeping the customer. Our own method: set limits as clear numbers or categories (the policy gap shows vague rules do not work), record each use and review the records monthly. Judgement matters where defensible options conflict, for example speed against consistency for all customers.

## From contacts to improvement

Requests to customer service can be analysed to learn how the product could be improved. Tag contacts by cause, count repeats per cause and bring the top causes to the owners of product, documentation or process. This closes the loop that the knowledge gap describes.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A software vendor sees many tickets about a confusing invoice layout. Agents explain it every time, politely. Instead of praising the politeness, the lead groups the tickets, shows the billing team that one section causes most of them, and gets the layout changed. Contacts on the topic fall, and agents gain time for complex cases.

## Key takeaways

- Customer loyalty and profit follow from satisfied customers, which follow from supported employees.
- Check all three sides: promise, enablement, encounter.
- Empower with explicit limits and review them.
- Treat repeat contacts as data for fixing causes, not only as workload.
