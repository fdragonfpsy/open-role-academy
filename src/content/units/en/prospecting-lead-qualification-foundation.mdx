## Why this matters

Your time is limited. Prospecting means finding potential customers; qualification means deciding which of them are worth a real sales effort. OpenStax describes this as the first step of the selling process: build a list of leads, then reduce it to those who fit your offer and are likely to be receptive. Skipping qualification feels busy but produces long conversations with people who will never buy.

## Where leads come from

The Lead generation article names several sources: the internet, personal referrals, telephone calls, advertisements and events. OpenStax adds trade shows and purchased lists. A lead is, in essence, contact information of someone who may be interested. In German, finding new customers is called Akquise (Akquisition). Contact with someone you have no prior relationship with is called Kaltakquise (cold approach); contact with existing customers or people who asked for information is Warmakquise. The rules for approaching people differ by channel and country, so ask your company before you contact anyone by phone or e-mail. This unit does not give legal advice.

## Qualifying with simple criteria

A common checklist, mentioned in the Sales process engineering article, is BANT: **B**udget, **A**uthority, **N**eed, **T**imeline. In plain questions:

- Need: Is there a problem our offer solves?
- Authority: Is this person involved in the decision, and who else is?
- Budget: Can they plausibly pay for a solution?
- Timeline: Is there a reason to decide in a reasonable period?

You rarely get all four on the first call. Treat the criteria as things to find out, and note which ones are still unknown. A lead that clearly has no need is **disqualified**, politely and quickly, which is a good result because it frees time.

## Keep records

Write down who you spoke to, what you learned and the next step in the CRM. Record only information you actually need for the sale. Practice insight: a lead with missing basics (no contact name, no source) is hard for a colleague to follow up.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A café owner fills in a contact form on your website about card payment terminals. You call. Need: the current terminal is old and often fails (yes). Authority: she is the owner (yes). Budget: she has not looked at prices (unknown). Timeline: the current contract ends in two months (yes). You mark the lead as qualified with budget unknown and book a follow-up where you will ask what a monthly fee would be acceptable. A second contact, from a list, says they only take cash and never will. You note "no need" and close the lead.

## Key takeaways

- Prospecting finds leads; qualification decides where effort goes.
- Leads come from many channels: referrals, events, web, calls and advertising.
- BANT (budget, authority, need, timeline) is a simple checklist; mark unknowns instead of guessing.
- Disqualifying early is a good outcome.
- Ask your company which contact methods are allowed before you reach out.
