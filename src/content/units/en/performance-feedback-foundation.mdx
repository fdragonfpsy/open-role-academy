## Why this matters

People want to know what is expected of them and how they are doing. Performance management is the set of routines that answers these two questions, and HR designs and supports them. This unit gives the basic map. It deliberately contains no legal specifics: rules on appraisals, works councils and employee data depend on your country, collective agreements and company rules. Check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## What a performance appraisal is

The English Wikipedia article describes a performance appraisal (also called performance review or evaluation) as a periodic process in which an employee's job performance is documented and evaluated, most often by the immediate manager. It says the appraisal is part of a performance management system and aims to align individual work with the organisation's goals. The same article notes criticism: annual reviews can give feedback too infrequently to be useful, and many companies are moving to shorter cycles.

## Goals versus tasks

A **task** is a piece of work to do ("send the monthly report"). A **goal** is a desired future state that the work aims at ("managers receive reliable figures by the fifth working day"). The Goal setting article describes goals as more deliberate than wishes and as guiding a person towards a desired future state. It also reports that, according to goal-setting theory, specific and ambitious goals lead to more performance improvement than easy or general goals, provided the person accepts the goal and has the ability to reach it. Practice insight: a goal list made only of tasks hides what really matters; ask "what should be different when this is done?"

## What useful feedback is

Practice insight, in line with the rules listed in the German Wikipedia article on feedback as a group-dynamics method (which names requested, descriptive, concrete, helpful and timely):

- **Specific**: describe a concrete situation, not a general verdict.
- **Timely**: speak soon after the event; waiting for weeks wastes the chance to learn.
- **Behaviour-focused**: describe what you saw or heard, not the person's character or motives.

The German article also says feedback is not the same as "telling someone your opinion", and it suggests that the receiver listens, asks clarifying questions and says thank you instead of defending.

## Regular check-ins

Short, regular one-to-one conversations (practice insight) mean that nothing in a formal appraisal comes as a surprise. The Wikipedia article says transparent appraisal systems reduce the risk of surprise negative evaluations. Keep check-ins simple: progress on goals, obstacles, support needed.

## Fairness basics

The German article on Leistungsbeurteilung lists the basics: criteria that are uniform and transparent, a clearly defined scale, an agreed assessment period, and standards that are the same for everyone. It also calls appraisal a sensitive area, because it can raise motivation but can also demotivate. Whether and how a works council must be involved is a question for current law and your works agreement: check the rules and ask the works council.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. Manager A tells you: "Employee B is not a team player." You ask for a specific situation. A explains that in last week's meeting B did not share the status of a task until asked. You help A rephrase: "In last week's meeting you shared the status only when asked. Please tell the team early when a task is delayed." The feedback now names a situation and a behaviour, is given soon after, and says what is wanted. You also suggest a short weekly check-in so that questions come up early.

## Key takeaways

- Performance management documents and discusses performance and aligns work with goals.
- A goal describes a desired result; a task is a piece of work.
- Useful feedback is specific, timely and about behaviour, not character.
- Regular check-ins prevent surprises; fairness needs clear, shared criteria.
- Legal details: check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel.
