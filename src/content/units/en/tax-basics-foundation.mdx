## Why this matters

Almost every invoice you handle in finance carries value-added tax (VAT). If you understand the basic logic, you will see why invoices must be complete, why tax is booked separately from the net amount, and why the company itself is not the one who ultimately bears this tax. This unit covers concepts for Germany and the EU. It deliberately contains no rates, thresholds, deadlines or legal section numbers: these change, so always check the current rules at the tax authority or with your tax advisor.

## What VAT is

VAT is an indirect tax on the value added at each stage of production and distribution. Suppliers collect it from customers at the point of sale and pass it on to the government; the person who is meant to bear it is the final consumer, because the tax is built into the price. In Germany it is called Umsatzsteuer (also Mehrwertsteuer). The German article describes the system as an all-stage net system with input tax deduction.

## The chain: output tax and input tax

Each business that sells something charges tax on its sales: the **output tax** (Umsatzsteuer). It shows this tax on the invoice. A business that buys goods or services from another business receives an invoice with tax on it: for the buyer this is **input tax** (Vorsteuer). The buyer may deduct the input tax from the output tax it owes. The difference is paid to the tax authority (in Germany: the Finanzamt), or refunded if the difference is negative. Because of this deduction, every business is taxed only on the value it adds.

## Why invoices matter

The invoice documents the tax that was charged, which is what makes the deduction possible. The English article stresses that invoices are central to VAT, and that every transaction leaves a trail that helps with compliance and audits. The German article on the VAT law notes that a proper invoice is required, among other things, for input tax deduction. Which details a proper invoice must contain is set by law: look them up in the current rules instead of relying on memory.

## Who is a business?

Under the German VAT law, a business (Unternehmer) is someone who independently carries out a commercial or professional activity. What counts is a sustained activity intended to generate income; unlike in income tax law, it does not matter whether a profit is intended. The tax arises when a business supplies goods or services for payment within Germany. For borderline cases, ask the tax advisor.

## Worked example

Illustration only; all amounts are invented and no real rate is used. A manufacturer sells to a wholesaler, who sells to a shop, which sells to a consumer.

| Step | Net price | Output tax charged | Input tax deducted | Paid to tax authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer sells to wholesaler | 100 | 20 | 0 | 20 |
| Wholesaler sells to shop | 150 | 30 | 20 | 10 |
| Shop sells to consumer | 200 | 40 | 30 | 10 |

The authority receives 20 + 10 + 10 = 40 in total, which equals the tax on the final price of 200. The consumer carries the 40; the businesses only pass it on. Each business paid the difference between its output tax and its input tax.

## Key takeaways

- VAT is collected by businesses along the chain, but is meant to be borne by the final consumer.
- Output tax is charged on sales; input tax is tax on purchases that a business can deduct.
- The amount paid to the authority is output tax minus input tax.
- Invoices document the tax and are the basis of the deduction; check the current legal requirements.
- A business is someone who independently carries out a sustained commercial or professional activity.
- For rates, thresholds and deadlines, use current official sources or a tax advisor.
