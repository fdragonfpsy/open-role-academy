## Why this matters

'Everyone' is not a target group. Customers differ in what they need, so one message for all tends to fit nobody well. OpenStax defines market segmentation as dividing a market into smaller, more precisely defined groups of consumers or organisations with common needs who are expected to respond similarly to a marketing action. The three steps together are known as STP: segmenting, targeting and positioning.

## Four ways to segment

OpenStax focuses on four types for consumer markets:

- **Geographic**: where people live (region, climate, language, urban or rural).
- **Demographic**: who they are (for example age or household situation).
- **Behavioural**: how they act (usage, purchase habits, loyalty).
- **Psychographic**: why they choose (values, attitudes, lifestyle).

The German Wikipedia article on Marktsegmentierung lists similar criteria, for example demographic, socio-economic and psychographic ones, and purchasing behaviour.

## The three steps

1. **Segmenting**: divide the market into groups with similar needs.
2. **Targeting**: choose which group or groups you will serve. OpenStax calls this group your target market and notes that it is not always the same as the target audience, for example when children use a toy but parents buy it.
3. **Positioning**: decide how you want the target group to see your offer compared with alternatives.

## Describing a target group

Use concrete words: who they are, what job they want done, where you can reach them, what they currently use. 'Women 25 to 45' is a start but says little about needs.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A small café wants to attract more customers on weekday afternoons. Segmenting by behaviour: (a) remote workers who stay two hours with a laptop, (b) parents after school pick-up, (c) tourists. The café targets group (a) first because they come on quiet afternoons and are reachable through the coworking space nearby. Positioning sentence: 'The quiet café with reliable Wi-Fi and fair prices for long work sessions.' Parents and tourists are still welcome, but the café does not design its offer around them.

## Key takeaways

- Segmentation divides a market by needs; targeting picks; positioning defines the place in customers' minds.
- Four common bases: geographic, demographic, behavioural, psychographic.
- Target market and target audience can differ.
- Describe target groups concretely, not as everyone.
