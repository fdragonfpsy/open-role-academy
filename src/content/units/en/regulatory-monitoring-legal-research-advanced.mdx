## Why this matters

Routine monitoring reports what has been published. Leading teams also look ahead, so that the business can prepare before a rule applies. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## Horizon scanning

The Wikipedia article on horizon scanning describes it as the early detection and assessment of emerging developments, mainly for policy makers. In a legal department the same idea means looking for weak signals of regulatory change: announced plans, consultations, evaluations of existing rules, pending court cases and supervisory priorities. Scanning is selective and judgement-based; it does not predict outcomes, it ranks topics by likely relevance and by how much time you have.

## The EU lifecycle as a map

The Commission's better-regulation page lists evaluations, fitness checks and impact assessments, and stakeholder consultation through its "Have your say" portal. These are visible stages in the life of a rule. An evaluation of an existing act can signal later amendment; a public consultation shows which options are discussed; an impact assessment accompanies a proposal. Using these stages, a team can map each tracked topic to a stage (idea, consultation, proposal, adoption, application, evaluation) and decide how closely to follow it. Taking part in a consultation, directly or through an association, is one way to influence a rule early.

## From signal to decision

1. **Capture** the signal with source, date and stage.
2. **Assess** relevance: which business processes, contracts, products or markets could be affected?
3. **Prioritise** by likelihood, impact and lead time; avoid treating everything as urgent.
4. **Assign** an owner and a review date.
5. **Decide and document**: act now, prepare, or watch; record the reasoning.
6. **Communicate** to affected functions in plain language and keep a register of open topics.

## Quality of the research

Court decisions interpret texts, so include relevant case law when it matters, and note jurisdiction. Distinguish facts (what an official page says) from your assessment (what it means for us). Keep both in the log and label the assessment as such. Plan periodic reviews of the register itself: retire closed topics, and check that the official sources you rely on are still the right ones.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An invented logistics firm tracks "EU rules on topic Y". The register shows: evaluation of the current act published, consultation open, no proposal yet. The head of legal ranks the topic medium, because adoption is not near but customer contracts would need review if the rule changed. Decision: "watch, review in the next quarterly cycle, owner: Contracts team", with a note that the assessment is internal and not legal advice.

## Key takeaways

- Horizon scanning looks for early signals and ranks them; it does not predict.
- Map each topic to a stage of the EU rule lifecycle and note which stage it is in.
- A documented chain from signal to decision makes the work reviewable.
- Separate official facts from your own assessment in every entry.
