## Why this matters

Controlling is not only analysis; it is also a process that runs again and again. Management expects the same numbers on a fixed day, built the same way. If you understand the cycle and the calendar, you know when your work is due, who depends on it and where errors enter.

## The planning, control and reporting cycle

A common way to describe the cycle (German: Controlling-Regelkreis) has these steps:

1. **Plan:** set targets and a budget for the period.
2. **Record:** book actual costs and revenues in the accounting system.
3. **Compare and analyse:** compare actual with plan, find and explain the variances.
4. **Report:** communicate results and key figures to the people who decide.
5. **Act and adjust:** agree measures and, if needed, update the forecast or the plan. The next cycle then starts.

Controlling supports the process: it provides the method, the calendar and the numbers, while managers remain responsible for decisions and results.

## The monthly calendar

Reporting follows a **calendar** that fixes who delivers what by which working day. Typical blocks are: bookings and accruals are completed, the figures are checked and reconciled, reports are produced, budget owners comment on variances, and a management meeting discusses the results. Companies set their own working days and steps. Missing a deadline early in the chain delays everything after it.

## Cost centres and master data

A **cost centre** (German: Kostenstelle) is a unit to which costs are assigned, such as a department. Reports by cost centre only make sense if the cost centre structure, the responsible persons and the account assignments are maintained consistently. These descriptive data are called **master data** (Stammdaten). A wrongly assigned cost centre produces a wrong variance report even if every booking is correct.

## Basic checks before release

Before sending a report, check at least: do the totals agree with the accounting system, are signs and units consistent, is the period correct, do the numbers look plausible compared with last month and are open questions marked. A report that is fast but wrong costs trust.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented calendar. Working day 3: bookings and accruals closed. Working day 4: controlling reconciles the report totals with the ledger. Working day 5: reports sent to budget owners. Working day 7: commentary due. Working day 8: management meeting. If bookings are late on day 3, the reconciliation on day 4 is at risk, and commentary on day 7 may be based on figures that later change. The analyst should flag the risk early instead of fixing it silently.

## Key takeaways

- The cycle: plan, record, compare and analyse, report, act and adjust.
- A reporting calendar is a chain; early delays affect every later step.
- Cost centre structures and master data must be consistent.
- Check totals, signs, period and plausibility before release.
