## Why this matters

A group of people reporting to you is not automatically a team. Teams grow through attention to goals, roles and the way people treat each other. This unit gives concept-level basics.

## What makes a team

The German Wikipedia article on Teamentwicklung describes small groups that work in direct contact toward a goal with divided responsibilities, and says teamwork should not be left to chance. Practical starting points: a shared goal everyone can state, clear roles (who does what, who decides what), and a few working agreements, such as how you communicate and how you handle disagreement. The English article on team building describes it as interventions that improve team effectiveness and clarify roles; it separates this from simple social activities.

## Stages of team development

The Wikipedia article on Tuckman's stages describes a model Bruce Tuckman proposed in 1965 with four stages:

- **Forming:** people get oriented, are polite and look to the lead.
- **Storming:** opinions clash, roles and status are negotiated.
- **Norming:** disagreements are resolved, cooperation and shared rules develop.
- **Performing:** with norms and roles in place, the team focuses on its goals.

The German article adds a fifth stage, adjourning, added later for teams that end. Treat the model as a rough map: the article notes that teams can get stuck or return to earlier stages when new challenges arise. As a lead, give more direction in forming, expect and moderate friction in storming, and help the team write down its norms.

## Psychological safety

The Wikipedia article defines psychological safety as the belief that one will not be punished or humiliated for speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns or mistakes. In teams it means people can take risks without being shamed by others. It does not mean that everyone agrees or that standards are low. As a lead you shape it through reactions: thank people who raise problems, and avoid blaming in public.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. Petra takes over a team of six that was just formed from two departments. In the first week people are polite and say little. She asks everyone to name the team's goal in a sentence and finds three different answers. She works with the team on one shared goal and a short list of agreements: questions are welcome in meetings, mistakes are reported early, and decisions are written down. When a colleague admits a mistake in the next meeting, Petra says thanks and asks what the team can learn.

## Key takeaways

- A team needs a shared goal, clear roles and working agreements.
- Tuckman's model (forming, storming, norming, performing) is a rough map, not a law.
- Psychological safety is the belief that speaking up carries no punishment or humiliation.
- Your reaction to the first mistake or concern sets the tone.
