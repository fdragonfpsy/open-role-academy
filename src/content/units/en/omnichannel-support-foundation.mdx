## Why this matters

Customers pick the channel that suits them, not the one that suits you. A reply that works on the phone can fail in writing, and a public comment is read by many people. You need a basic feel for each channel.

## The channels

Customer support uses various channels: telephone, email, live chat, self-service knowledge bases and social media. A classic inbound call centre takes calls for orders, information, fault reports and complaints. A customer service centre also serves email and social media channels and uses special software for them. Our own summary of strengths: phone is quick for complex or emotional matters, email suits detailed cases and attachments, chat suits short questions in real time, and social media is public and fast-moving.

## Writing without tone of voice

Text lacks the nonverbal signals of speech, so rudeness or ambiguity makes written communication harder. Common netiquette advice:

- Give emails a meaningful subject that matches the content.
- Do not mix several topics in one email; start a new one for a new topic.
- Avoid writing in capital letters, which is usually read as shouting.
- Keep spelling and sentences correct and quote only what is needed.

Netiquette has usually no legal force; the operator of a channel sets the rules.

## Social media is public

On social media, communication goes in several directions, and comments cannot be controlled: others can read negative experiences. It also needs people who respond to comments regularly. Reply politely, briefly and by name, and move details to a private channel. Never ask for personal data in a public comment.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer posts publicly: "Still no refund after 10 days!" Reply: "Hello Mira, I am sorry for the wait. I will look into this today. Please send us your order number by direct message so I can check it." The reply is short, polite, does not discuss details in public and moves the case to a private channel.

## Key takeaways

- Each channel has strengths: phone for complexity, email for detail, chat for speed, social for publicity.
- Written text has no tone of voice; use clear subjects, one topic per email, no capitals.
- Social replies are public; keep them short and move details to private.
- Never request personal data in public.
