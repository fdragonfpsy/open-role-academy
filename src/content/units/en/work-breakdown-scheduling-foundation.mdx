## Why this matters

A plan that says "website: March to June" cannot be tracked, because nobody knows what has to happen in between. Breaking the work down is how you find the tasks you forgot, and a schedule built on those tasks gives people dates they can believe.

## Breaking work down

A work breakdown structure (in German, Projektstrukturplan) divides a project into manageable parts. Starting from the project itself, you split it into sub-tasks and then into work packages, which are the lowest level and are not divided further. The structure is the basis for scheduling, resource planning and cost planning, and its findings feed into risk management. For this reason it is sometimes called the "plan of plans".

Two design principles matter from the start. Each level should use one consistent way of splitting: by deliverables, by phases or by function. And the structure should include all the work in the project scope, and nothing outside it. Our own rule of thumb for size: a work package should be small enough that one person can say "done" within about one to two weeks.

## Work package description (our own)

For each work package write:

- Name: a noun for the result, such as "Visitor form tested".
- Owner: one person.
- Result: what exists when it is done.
- Effort guess: in days.

## Schedules and milestones

A bar chart (Gantt chart) shows activities as horizontal bars on a time axis; the longer the bar, the longer the activity. It works best for projects with a small to medium number of activities. Choose the level of detail with care: too few activities give no control, while listing every single task weakens the picture. A milestone is an event of special importance that divides the project into stages with checkable interim goals. Typical milestones are decisions, the existence of deliverables, and acceptances. Fix the check criteria in advance, otherwise the check becomes arbitrary.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A team moves to a new floor. Deliverables: "Workstations ready", "IT connected", "Old floor cleared". Work packages under "IT connected": "Network sockets checked" (owner Ben, 1 day), "Phones configured" (owner Chloe, 2 days), "Printers installed" (owner Ben, 1 day). On the bar chart the work packages appear as bars, and a milestone "IT ready for test" follows them with the criterion "every desk can print and call". The first plan had only "move in May"; breaking it down uncovered the forgotten printer installation.

## Key takeaways

- Split the project into sub-tasks and work packages; the lowest level is the work package.
- Split each level the same way and cover all the scope, nothing more.
- The work breakdown is the basis for the schedule, resources and cost.
- Milestones need check criteria agreed in advance.
