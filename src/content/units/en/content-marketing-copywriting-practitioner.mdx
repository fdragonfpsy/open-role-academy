## Why this matters

Content without a plan produces many posts and little effect. At practitioner level you decide what to create, for whom, at which point of the customer journey, and how you will know it worked.

## Start from questions

The German Wikipedia article describes the strategic planning of content creation as content strategy, usually the responsibility of marketing. Our own working rule: collect real questions first, from support tickets, sales conversations and search terms, and turn each recurring question into a topic. Internal wishes ('we want to announce our new office') rarely match what the audience searches for.

## Map content to the funnel

OpenStax describes the sales funnel as a visual representation of the customer journey from product awareness to product purchase, with most people dropping off before the end. A simple mapping, our own simplification:

- **Awareness**: guides and explainers that answer a general problem.
- **Consideration**: comparisons, checklists, case studies.
- **Decision**: product detail pages, pricing information, trial or contact offers.

Each piece needs one next step that fits its stage; a hard sales call after a beginner article feels pushy.

## Structure of a text

Headline (what the reader gets), lead (two sentences confirming the problem and the promise), body (short sections with subheadings, one idea each), call to action. Put the most important point first, because many readers scan.

## Measure per piece

OpenStax lists website traffic, traffic by source, conversions and bounce rate as online metrics. Pair each piece with one primary metric: awareness content with traffic by source, decision content with conversions. A high bounce rate on a page can point to issues with the page, OpenStax notes loading time as one, but it can also mean the reader found the answer; read it together with the page's purpose.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A software company for small clinics collects fifteen support questions. The most frequent is how to document patient appointments in a privacy-friendly way. The team writes a guide for awareness, a checklist for consideration and a demo offer for decision. Each has one call to action. After a month they compare conversions per piece and retire a topic nobody reads.

## Key takeaways

- Take topics from real audience questions.
- Match each piece and its call to action to a funnel stage.
- Structure: headline, lead, scannable body, one call to action.
- Pick one primary metric per piece and read it with the page's purpose in mind.
