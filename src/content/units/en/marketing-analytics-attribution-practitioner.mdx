## Why this matters

At practitioner level you compare channels and decide where the next euro goes. That needs clean source data and an honest view of what a channel report can and cannot say.

## Tagging links (UTM at concept level)

OpenStax notes that marketers can track traffic by source by attaching a piece of tracking code to links on social media pages and other sources, and lists traffic sources such as email, paid search, organic search, paid social and affiliates. In practice this code is often a set of parameters on the link address, commonly called UTM parameters: they name the source (for example a newsletter), the medium (email) and the campaign.

Rules that keep data usable, our own working method: agree on one lower-case naming scheme, keep a shared list of campaign names, tag only links you control (not internal links on your own website) and never put personal data in a link.

## Attribution models

A customer rarely converts after one contact. **Attribution** assigns credit for a conversion to the contacts before it.

- **First touch:** all credit to the first contact. Useful to see what creates awareness; it ignores everything that happened later.
- **Last touch:** all credit to the final contact before conversion. Simple and common; it favours channels near the purchase, such as search for the brand name, and undervalues earlier ones.
- **Linear:** equal credit to every contact. It is fair on paper, but a minor reminder gets as much credit as the decisive one.

No model shows what would have happened without the marketing. Each is a rule for splitting credit, not a measurement of cause.

## Limits to state in every report

- Contacts you cannot see (offline, word of mouth, people who decline tracking) are missing.
- The same person on several devices can look like several people.
- Different tools use different rules, so totals rarely match.
- Cost per acquisition per channel (OpenStax: spend divided by conversions) depends on the model chosen.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented numbers. A customer first sees a social post, later opens a newsletter and finally buys after clicking a search ad. First touch credits social, last touch credits search, linear gives each a third. The team writes in its report: "Search looks best under last touch, but social started the journey; we do not conclude we should cut social."

## Measurement plan template

Our own method: goal and KPI; definition of conversion; tagged links per channel; who owns the data; which model is used for the headline and which for comparison; what consent setup applies (ask legal counsel; see the EDPB guidance on consent for the principle that consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous).

## Key takeaways

- Tagged links tell you the source; naming discipline makes them usable.
- Attribution models split credit by rule; they do not prove cause.
- Show at least two models and state the blind spots.
- Plan measurement before the campaign, including consent questions.
