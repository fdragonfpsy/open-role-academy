## Why this matters

Almost every HR role touches hiring at some point. A good hire saves a lot of trouble later; a poor process wastes time and can treat candidates unfairly. This unit gives you the map of a hiring process for Germany and the EU context. It deliberately contains no deadlines, retention periods or section numbers: they change and depend on the case, so check current law, your data protection officer (DPO) or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## The hiring process at a glance

The English Wikipedia article describes recruitment as the overall process of identifying, sourcing, screening, shortlisting, interviewing and hiring candidates. It stresses that the process varies widely with the employer, the seniority of the role and the sector. Typical stages include a job analysis for new or changed jobs, a kick-off conversation between the recruiter and the hiring manager to understand the need, sourcing, screening and selection, and one or more interviews. The German article on Personalbeschaffung starts earlier: before anyone is hired, the staffing need is determined, for example by how many people are needed, with which qualifications, when and where.

## Three documents that are easy to mix up

- **Job description**: according to the English article, a written narrative of the tasks and responsibilities of a position. It may also name the reporting line and the qualifications needed, and it is usually based on a job analysis.
- **Requirement profile** (Anforderungsprofil): the German article defines it as a systematic collection of the prerequisites and characteristics needed for a task. It is the result of a requirements analysis, it focuses on the characteristics of the person, and the requirements can be weighted by relevance. The job description, by contrast, focuses on tasks and organisational placement.
- **Job ad**: the public text that invites applications. The German article lists what applicants should be told, such as the job title, a short description of the work, the department, working hours, required qualifications, the intended pay and the place of work.

The logic runs in one direction: analyse the job, define what a person needs to succeed, and only then write the ad.

## What is fair to ask, at concept level

A fair process judges candidates against criteria that are relevant for the job. The English article on job interviews notes that characteristics such as race or gender are typically not relevant to whether a person can do the job. The German Personalbeschaffung article gives an example of the same logic: questions about current illness are discussed there only where the illness matters for the work to be done. Which questions are allowed in detail depends on current law and the case, so treat this as orientation and ask legal counsel or your DPO before you design questions about private matters.

## Document as you go

Write down what you did and why: the requirement profile, where the job was advertised, how applications were assessed, and the reason for each decision. The German article mentions an evaluation sheet that is filled in after each selection interview and used to decide, followed by a written acceptance or rejection. Notes help you stay consistent and help colleagues who take over.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real figures. A department asks you to "find someone like Sam", who just left. Step 1: do not start with the ad. Ask what tasks the role covers and which characteristics are really necessary, and write them into a requirement profile. Step 2: mark each requirement as essential or nice to have. Step 3: draft the ad from the profile, with title, tasks, requirements, working hours and place of work. Step 4: keep a short record of these decisions. Step 5: if the manager wants a requirement that looks like it is about a personal characteristic rather than the job, raise it with legal counsel instead of deciding alone.

## Key takeaways

- Recruitment runs from need and job analysis through sourcing and screening to interviews and a decision.
- Job description, requirement profile and job ad are different documents with different jobs.
- Criteria and questions should relate to the job; private details need legal advice.
- Document each step and the reason for each decision.
- This is orientation for Germany/EU; check current law, your DPO or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.
