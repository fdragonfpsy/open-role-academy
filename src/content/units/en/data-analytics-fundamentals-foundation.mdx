## Why this matters

Every team has data, but few teams have answers. Data analysis is the work that turns a question into something a person can decide on. Knowing the steps helps you avoid the most common mistake: starting with a spreadsheet instead of a question.

## What data analysis is

Data analysis is the process of inspecting, cleansing, transforming and modelling data to find useful information and to support decisions. The phases are iterative: what you learn late in the work can send you back to an earlier step.

## The lifecycle (our own simple description)

- Question: which decision depends on this, and who will use the answer?
- Data: which data could answer it, and where does it come from?
- Prepare: check the data and clean it.
- Analyse: summarise, compare, look for patterns.
- Communicate: show the result together with its limits.
- Decide and review: act, then check whether it worked.

## Four types of analysis

These are common teaching labels; the wording here is our own.

- Descriptive: what happened? Totals, averages, trends.
- Diagnostic: why did it happen? Comparing groups, looking for causes.
- Predictive: what is likely to happen? Forecasts and models.
- Prescriptive: what should we do? Recommendations.

Most analytics work starts descriptive. A prediction built on a poorly understood past is rarely better than the past.

## Kinds of data

Numerical data are numbers you can calculate with, such as an order value. Categorical data are labels, such as a region or a product group. Data in tables with fixed columns are called structured; free text, images and audio are unstructured.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A support lead asks: "Why are customers unhappy?" That is too vague to analyse. The analyst asks what decision depends on it and sharpens the question: "Did the share of tickets answered within 24 hours change last quarter?" The data is a ticket export with created and answered timestamps. The descriptive result: 82 percent in the first quarter and 71 percent in the second. That is a finding the lead can act on, and it prompts a diagnostic follow-up: which ticket categories got slower?

## Key takeaways

- Start with the question and the decision, not with the data.
- The lifecycle is iterative; expect to loop back.
- Descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive analysis answer different questions.
- Know whether a column is numerical or categorical before you calculate.
