## Why this matters

Not every category deserves the same strategy. A tough price negotiation fits a market with many suppliers; for a part that only one firm can make, the same tactic can endanger supply. A simple segmentation tells you which approach to try first.

## The sourcing process

Descriptions of strategic sourcing agree on the order: analyse spend, research the market, find suitable suppliers, develop the strategy, negotiate, put the new supply structure in place and keep tracking results. One later description folds the middle steps into market research and an RFx process (a request for quotation or proposal) and splits negotiation from contracting. Use whichever naming your company uses, but keep the sequence: strategy comes before negotiation.

## Segmenting with the Kraljič matrix

Peter Kraljič introduced a matrix in 1983 that sorts purchases into four classes along two axes: the importance of the purchase (for example its share of total purchasing value) and the complexity of the supply market (monopoly situations, entry barriers, technological innovation). The original matrix classifies purchases, not suppliers, though it can also be used for supplier types.

- Non-critical: low impact, plentiful supply. Simplify and delegate administration.
- Leverage: important, but the market is low risk with many offers. Use your buying power and negotiate often.
- Bottleneck: low economic impact, but continuity of supply is at risk. Secure supply through medium-term relationships; cost matters less.
- Strategic: important and in a complex or risky market. Think medium to long term, watch the market, assess make or buy, build alternatives and stable, close cooperation.

## Single or multiple sourcing

Buying from several suppliers on purpose avoids dependence and other disadvantages; single sourcing concentrates volume and effort but increases dependence. Our own rule: decide per category, and write down the reason.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. Copy paper is non-critical: one catalogue supplier, one annual contract. Standard steel sheet is leverage: the team invites quotes from five suppliers and negotiates a volume agreement. A special seal available from one manufacturer is a bottleneck: the value is small, but a missing seal stops a production line, so the team agrees a longer contract, a safety stock and starts looking for a second source. A custom control unit from two specialists is strategic: the team reviews make or buy, holds regular reviews with both suppliers and plans joint development. Each decision goes into a one-page category plan (our own format): category, segment, strategy, reason and next step.

## Key takeaways

- Strategy comes before negotiation; keep the sequence of the sourcing process.
- The Kraljič matrix sorts purchases by importance and supply-market complexity into four fields.
- Leverage means pressure on price; bottleneck means securing supply; strategic means partnership and alternatives.
- Single versus multiple sourcing is a per-category decision with a written reason.
