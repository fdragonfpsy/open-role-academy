## Why this matters

Quality is not something you add at the end. If nobody has said what "good" means for a deliverable, every person judges by their own measure, and arguments arrive at acceptance. Quality management in the sense of the German-language literature is the systematic planning and steering of processes with regard to quality. For a project, it starts with a few clear agreements.

## Two views of quality

A customer judges quality by how well a product serves its purpose. A producer judges it by how far the product conforms to predetermined standards. A project needs both: the standard must reflect the purpose, and the deliverable must meet the standard.

## Acceptance criteria (our own method)

For each deliverable, write two to five criteria that anyone can check with a yes or no.

- Weak: "The report is easy to read."
- Better: "The report has a one-page summary, every chart has a title and source, and two reviewers from the target group find the main message without help."

Agree the criteria with the person who accepts the deliverable before work starts, and note who will check them and how.

## Assurance and control

Quality assurance means systematic effort to make sure that agreed requirements will be met. Its focus is preventing mistakes and defects, which is why it is sometimes described as moving quality effort earlier, a "shift left". Quality control checks the result for conformance, that is, it detects defects. A common view in the quality literature is that quality needs more than inspection at the end: building it into every step is cheaper than finding faults late.

- Prevention (examples): templates, checklists, clear requirements, a second pair of eyes on a draft.
- Detection (examples): reviews of finished parts, tests, inspections against the criteria.

## Plan the checks

Put reviews into the schedule as tasks with an owner and with time for rework. A review that is not planned is the first thing dropped when time is short.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented project. A team produces a training handbook for 80 staff. Acceptance criteria agreed with the head of training: each chapter has learning goals, every procedure has been tried by one person who did not write it, and the handbook passes a spell-check and a layout check. Prevention: a chapter template and a checklist. Detection: a peer review in week 3 with two days for rework in the plan. The review finds two procedures with a missing step; both are fixed before printing.

## Key takeaways

- Quality means fitting the purpose and meeting the agreed standard.
- Acceptance criteria are checkable with yes or no and agreed before work starts.
- Assurance prevents defects; control detects them; use both.
- Plan reviews as scheduled tasks with time for rework.
