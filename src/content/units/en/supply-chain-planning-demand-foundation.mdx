## Why this matters

Purchasing does not start with an order but with a question: how much will we need, and when? A rough forecast and a simple plan are better than ordering on gut feeling.

## The supply chain

A supply chain is the system through which an organisation acquires raw material, produces products and delivers them to customers. Supply chain management plans, steers and improves this flow. It coordinates, among other things:

## What it coordinates

- Sourcing and procurement of materials.
- Production scheduling and order processing.
- Inventory, transport and storage.
- Customer service and the flow of information.

## From forecast to purchase

- Forecast the quantity of finished goods expected to be needed.
- Use the bill of materials, which lists each item and how many are required per product, to derive the parts needed.
- Compare with stock on hand and open orders, then order the difference in time for the lead time (our own step).

## A simple forecast (our own method)

A moving average takes the last few periods, for example four weeks, and uses their average as the forecast for the next one. It is quick to do in a spreadsheet, but it ignores trends and seasons, and every forecast is wrong to some degree, so plan with a safety margin. The bullwhip effect shows why accuracy matters: orders to suppliers swing more than the sales to end customers, and the swings grow further up the chain.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A small manufacturer sold 120, 130, 110 and 140 units in the last four weeks. Forecast for next week = (120 + 130 + 110 + 140) / 4 = 125 units. Each unit needs 2 motors, so 250 motors are required. 180 are in stock, so 70 must be ordered, in time for the supplier's lead time.

## Key takeaways

- A supply chain runs from raw material to the customer; supply chain management coordinates it.
- Planning starts with a forecast, then the bill of materials, then stock and orders.
- A moving average is a simple forecast with clear limits.
- The bullwhip effect makes order swings grow upstream, so plan carefully.
