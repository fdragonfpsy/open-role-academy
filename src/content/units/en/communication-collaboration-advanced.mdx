## Collaboration as something you shape

At advanced level you lead teams, projects or cross-functional work. You no longer only take part in collaboration — you **shape the conditions** for it: what people dare to say, how meetings include everyone, and how groups with different goals work together.

## Psychological safety

Psychological safety describes the shared belief in a team that people will **not be punished or humiliated for speaking up** with ideas, questions, concerns or mistakes. The team-level concept is associated with the researcher Amy Edmondson. It does not mean that everyone is always nice or that standards drop. In psychologically safe teams, disagreement and bad news surface early — which is exactly what makes them valuable.

Leaders influence this strongly through everyday behaviour:

- **Model fallibility:** name your own mistakes and open questions.
- **Invite input actively**, especially from quieter or newer team members.
- **Respond to bad news with curiosity** ("What happened? What do we need?") instead of blame.
- **Thank people** who raise problems, even when the timing is inconvenient.

## Hybrid collaboration

When some people are in the room and others join remotely, those in the room easily dominate. Deliberate facilitation helps: structure turns (for example, remote participants first), collect input in a shared chat or document in parallel, ensure everyone has good audio and video, and **make decisions only in the meeting**, not afterwards in the hallway.

## Working across teams

Friction between departments often comes from **different goals and unclear interfaces**, not from bad intentions. Instead of deciding who is "right", bring both sides together to map the shared process, agree on facts, define common goals and clarify hand-over rules. Where conflicts remain, agree on an escalation path in advance.

## Worked example

After a failed release, Sara notices that her developers had seen the risk but nobody had raised it. In the retrospective she starts with her own misjudgement, asks every person for one observation, and introduces a standing agenda item "risks and concerns" in the weekly meeting. Three weeks later, a junior colleague flags a problem early — and it is fixed before release.

## Key takeaways

- Psychological safety = speaking up without fear; it enables learning, not comfort.
- Leaders build it through everyday reactions to mistakes and bad news.
- Hybrid meetings need deliberate facilitation for fairness.
- Solve cross-team friction through shared goals and clear interfaces.
