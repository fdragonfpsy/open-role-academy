## Why this matters

A presentation or demo is the moment the customer sees how your product could solve their problem. Many sellers use it to show everything the product can do. Customers, however, mainly want to know whether it solves what they told you in discovery. This unit gives you a simple structure for that.

## Build on discovery

OpenStax describes the presentation step as the point where the seller highlights the product aspects of most benefit to the prospect, based on the needs uncovered earlier. If you skipped discovery, you can only guess. Before you present, write down the two or three needs the customer named, in their own words.

## From feature to benefit

A common practice tool (one of several in use) separates three levels:

- **Feature:** what the product has ("automatic reminders").
- **Advantage:** what it does ("sends a message before a payment is due").
- **Benefit:** what it means for this customer ("your team no longer chases late payments by phone, which was your main complaint").

Only the benefit answers the customer's question. Say the benefit in the customer's own words and leave out features that match no stated need.

## Structure

1. **Open:** thank the customer, state the agenda and the time, and confirm the goals you heard.
2. **Show:** one need at a time, with the matching feature and benefit. Pause and ask whether it fits.
3. **Check:** ask what questions the customer has.
4. **Close with a next step:** agree on who does what by when.

## A demo is evidence, not a tour

The Wikipedia article on product demonstration describes a demo as visual support for the sales presentation and an effective way to address a prospect's specific product concerns. So demonstrate the customer's use case with realistic data, not the full menu. Prepare: test the equipment and the connection, keep a stable demo setup, and know how you will carry on if something breaks.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A small bakery chain said in discovery that closing the tills in the evening takes too long and errors appear. You open with this goal, then show only the closing report: one screen that totals each till and flags differences. You say, "The difference column shows where to look first, so you do not have to count again." You skip the loyalty module, which nobody asked for, and mention that it exists if they are interested later. You end by agreeing that the owner tests the closing report in one shop next week.

## Key takeaways

- Present after discovery and build on the needs the customer named.
- Translate features into benefits for this customer.
- Use a clear structure: open, show, check, next step.
- A demo shows the customer's use case, not every function.
- Prepare the setup and have a fallback.
