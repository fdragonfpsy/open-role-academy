## Why this matters

At practitioner level the demo is planned like a small project. You agree beforehand what the customer wants to see and how they will judge it, so that the result can lead to a decision instead of "interesting, we will think about it".

## Agree the agenda and success criteria in advance

Send a short agenda before the session: the use cases you will show, who should attend and the time. Ask: "What would you need to see to feel confident this works for you?" Note these as success criteria and refer to them in the demo. Ask in advance who attends and in which role (user, budget holder, technical reviewer, sponsor). OpenStax stresses that the presentation should rest on needs found earlier; the criteria are the sharpened form of those needs.

## Fit the room

Different people listen for different things. A user wants to see everyday handling, a manager wants the effect on results, technical staff want integration and security. Plan short blocks for each, keep depth to what each person needs and say so ("I will show the technical details at the end for those interested"). Address people by name and check back with the decision maker, not only with the friendliest participant.

## Show the flow, not the screens

Tell a story along the customer's process: start with the situation ("Monday morning, three new orders arrive"), show the steps, end with the outcome. Use their terms and, if possible, their sample data (with permission, and without personal data in shared recordings). Keep to the critical path; mark clearly what is a prepared setup and what is the product in standard use.

## Honest handling of gaps and questions

When asked about a function you do not have or are unsure about, say so, note it and promise an answer by a date. Do not present plans or prototypes as available features. This is both a matter of trust and of fair dealing: claims made in a presentation can later be held against the seller. Ask your legal or compliance contact how product statements should be handled.

## Proof and next step

The Wikipedia article notes that a demo helps address specific concerns. Back claims with proof that fits: a customer story, a pilot with defined measures or a trial. Close by checking each success criterion ("Did we show enough on the closing report?") and agree a dated next step, such as a trial with named users.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A regional training provider wants to replace manual booking. Before the demo, you agree three criteria: bookings in under two minutes, automatic confirmations, a sample export for accounting. Present are the manager, two coordinators and an accountant. You show the booking flow first (coordinators), then the dashboard (manager), then the export (accountant). A coordinator asks about waiting lists; your product has none today, so you say so, describe the current workaround and promise a written answer by Thursday. At the end you check the three criteria and agree a two-week trial with the two coordinators.

## Key takeaways

- Agree agenda and success criteria in advance and check them at the end.
- Plan the demo for the people in the room and their roles.
- Tell the customer's process as a story with their data.
- State gaps openly; never present plans as existing features.
- End with a dated, measurable next step.
