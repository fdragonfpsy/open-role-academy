## Why this matters
At advanced level you design how data protection works in HR: new processes, HR software, incident handling, documentation. This unit stays at concept level and contains no deadlines, retention periods, article or section numbers, thresholds or fine amounts, because they depend on current law and the case. Check current law, your data protection officer (DPO) or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice; this is not a substitute for company policy. Context: EU and Germany.

## Privacy by design and by default

The English article on privacy by design describes it as an approach in which privacy is taken into account throughout the whole engineering process, and lists seven foundational principles, among them proactive rather than reactive measures and privacy as the default setting. The GDPR article states that the regulation incorporates data protection by design and by default, that measures should be designed into business processes, and gives pseudonymising data as soon as possible as one example; it also says the controller remains responsible and must be able to demonstrate compliance even when a processor does the work.

Applied to HR: when a new process or system is planned (a new applicant portal, a new onboarding tool), involve the DPO at the design stage, not at go-live. Define purpose, minimal data set, access, and how long data stays.

## Processor relationships (HR software)
When an HR software vendor processes personal data on your company's behalf, the company is the controller and the vendor the processor, as the GDPR article's terms describe; the controller stays accountable. Involve the DPO and procurement before anything is signed, understand what data goes to the vendor and what happens to it at contract end, and check that tool settings match what was agreed. You do not draft legal terms; you make sure the right people review them.

## Breach handling process

The English article on data breaches defines a breach as unauthorised exposure, disclosure or loss of personal information, with causes such as insider mistakes, lost devices and phishing. It says prevention cannot eliminate risk and that post-breach efforts commonly include containment, investigation of scope and cause, and notification where required by law. Your role in HR is internal: recognise a possible breach and escalate immediately to the DPO and IT security according to your company process. Do not wait to be sure, fix it quietly or contact outsiders yourself. Whether and when anyone outside must be informed is a decision for the DPO and management based on current law. Document facts: what you saw, when, what you did.

## Employee monitoring and time tracking
The German article on employee data protection says the GDPR lets national rules and collective agreements cover processing in the employment context, including monitoring systems at the workplace, and that national law applies in Germany. So any proposal to monitor employees (activity tracking, video, location data, evaluating emails or logs, time tracking beyond what is established) is a high-caution topic. Do not implement it on a manager's request. Check with the DPO and, where one exists, the works council or works agreement. Document the purpose, why less intrusive means are insufficient, who sees the data and for how long.

## Training and documenting measures
Data protection is a continuing practice. The DPO article lists raising awareness among employees and ensuring the organisation is trained as typical DPO tasks. Support this: brief new HR staff at onboarding, record each HR process (purpose, data, access, retention rule) and keep evidence of training.

## Keeping up with law and guidance
Rules and authority guidance change, so do not rely on memory or on this unit. Use your DPO as the first contact, follow your supervisory authority's published guidance, and update your documentation when something changes.

## Worked example
Illustration only; no real data. Your company wants a tool that tracks how long employees are active on their computers, to "improve productivity". Step 1: do not set it up. Step 2: write down the purpose and data involved. Step 3: bring in the DPO and, where applicable, the works council before any decision. Step 4: ask whether less intrusive means reach the goal. Step 5: if it proceeds, record the decision, access and deletion rule, and brief employees as agreed.

## Key takeaways

- Build data protection into HR processes at design stage; choose protective defaults.
- With HR software vendors, the company stays accountable; involve DPO and procurement.
- Breach: recognise, escalate immediately to the DPO and IT security, document facts; the DPO decides on external steps.
- Monitoring and time tracking proposals need DPO review and works council or works agreement checks before implementation.
- Train, document and keep up with law and guidance; the DPO is your first contact.
- Check current law, your DPO or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.
