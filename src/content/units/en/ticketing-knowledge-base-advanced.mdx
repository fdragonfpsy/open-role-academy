## Why this matters

A ticket system fills quickly, and a knowledge base ages quickly. Advanced work means keeping both useful: removing outdated content, choosing categories that answer real questions and using the data to prevent contacts.

## Knowledge lifecycle (our own)

Our own lifecycle has four stages:

- Create: write an article after a repeated ticket, not in advance for everything.
- Review: every article has an owner and a review date; unreviewed articles are flagged.
- Improve: use search terms with no hits and "was this helpful" signals.
- Retire: remove or merge outdated articles.

Our own view: a knowledge base needs a clear structure, consistent formatting and a good search. If staff and customers see different answers, customers lose trust, so one source should serve all channels.

## Categories that answer questions

Design categories from the questions you want to answer later, for example "why do customers contact us?" and "where do cases get stuck?". Keep the list short, avoid overlaps, give each category a definition and allow "other" only with a free-text reason that is reviewed monthly. Change categories rarely, because every change breaks comparison over time.

## From data to fewer contacts

Analytical CRM software collects and analyses customer data to show trends and needs. In the same way, count tickets by category and cause every month. A category with many repeated tickets calls for a fix in product, process or article, and a knowledge article is the cheap first step. Report the top three reasons and the action taken, so the effect is visible.

## Gaming and distortion

A target such as "answer quickly" is read differently by everyone unless it is defined. Targets also invite shortcuts: a first reply that says only "we are looking into it" meets a response-time target but does not help. Closing a ticket to stop the clock meets a resolution target but may reopen it. Our own counter-measures: measure reopen rate and customer feedback next to time, and define what counts as a real first reply.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A team notices that "Where is my parcel?" is the top category with 300 of 900 tickets per month. The lead writes an article with the tracking steps, adds the link to the order confirmation email and checks after a month: the category falls to 180 tickets. Meanwhile the team had met its first-reply target by sending "we are checking" messages; after the lead redefines a first reply as "contains the next step or an answer", the target gets harder but customers wait less.

## Key takeaways

- Run articles through create, review, improve and retire.
- Design few, defined categories from the questions you want answered.
- Count by category and cause; fix causes and write articles.
- Define targets precisely and pair time with outcome metrics.
