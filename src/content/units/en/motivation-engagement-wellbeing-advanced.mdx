## Why this matters

Individual conversations help, but recurring overload usually has causes in the system: priorities, staffing, unclear scope. Advanced leaders work on these causes. This unit is a concept-level discussion and not legal or medical advice.

## Look at the system

The OpenStax chapter on organisational influences on stress and the chapter on coping with work-related stress treat work stress as something shaped by the organisation, not only by individuals. Ask: Which demands are recurring? Where do we lack control or clarity? Which meetings, interruptions or approval steps add load without value? Gather input from the team, for example in a retrospective, and choose one or two changes per cycle. Surface structural problems upward: if capacity does not match commitments, tell your own manager early with specifics.

## Working time and health and safety (Germany and EU, topic level)

The German Wikipedia article on the Arbeitszeitgesetz describes it as public-law occupational safety that limits working time, sets minimum breaks and rest periods, and contains rules on Sundays, public holidays and night work. The article on Arbeitsschutz describes employer measures to prevent accidents and work-related health hazards, and the article on psychische Belastung mentions the employer's duty to determine and assess psychological strain. For a lead this means: working time and risk assessment are not only personal preferences; ask HR and the safety function how rules apply to your team, what the organisation documents, and whether a works council has a role. Do not interpret the law yourself and do not agree on arrangements that ignore these frameworks.

## Sustainable engagement

The article on employee engagement says the concept remains contested. Avoid treating engagement as a number to push upward. High engagement achieved through constant overtime is not sustainable. Plan recovery after peaks, make sure holidays are taken and cover is arranged, and role-model your own boundaries; teams copy what leads do.

## Boundaries of the leader role

You can adjust tasks, priorities and deadlines, and you can listen and point to support. You cannot diagnose health issues, ask for medical details, or promise outcomes that depend on others. Respect privacy: share only what is needed and agreed. Sick or returning colleagues are subject to rules your organisation and HR know; involve them early.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. In a quarterly review Dana sees that overtime peaks every month-end because three reports are requested at the same time. She brings this to the team, which agrees to stagger two reports and drop one that nobody uses. Dana confirms the changes with her own manager, tells the team about the change in plain terms and checks the overtime pattern in the next quarter. When one colleague mentions persistent exhaustion, she suggests contact with HR and the occupational health service instead of discussing symptoms.

## Key takeaways

- Treat recurring overload as a system problem and involve the team in fixing it.
- Working time and health and safety are employer duties at topic level; ask HR rather than interpreting rules.
- Engagement sustained by overtime is not sustainable; plan recovery and model boundaries.
- Know the limits: you adjust work and listen, you do not diagnose.
