## Why this matters

Every role works with digital information: e-mails, documents, intranet pages, search results — and increasingly AI tools that write, summarise or translate. Being digitally literate does not mean knowing every feature of every program. It means finding what you need, judging whether it is reliable, and using tools in a way that is safe for you, your colleagues and your customers.

## Checking information

Before you rely on information, ask four quick questions:

- **Who** published it, and what is their interest (inform, sell, persuade)?
- **When** was it published or updated?
- **Where** does it come from — is the original source named?
- **Does anyone else** independent confirm it?

A professional-looking page is no proof of accuracy. For important facts, go to the original source.

## How AI chatbots work — in plain words

Tools such as AI chatbots are usually based on **large language models (LLMs)**. They are trained on very large amounts of text and generate answers by producing words that are likely to come next. That is why the answers sound fluent and confident. But a likely-sounding sentence is not automatically a true one. AI tools can **"hallucinate"**: they present false or invented information — sometimes even invented sources — as fact.

## Using AI tools safely

- **Never enter personal or confidential data** (customer names, contract numbers, internal figures) into a tool your company has not approved for that purpose. What you type may be sent to and stored by the provider.
- **Check the output** before you use it, especially facts, numbers, names and references.
- **Follow your company's rules** on which AI tools are allowed. If there are none, ask.

## Worked example

Lena in customer service wants a quick answer to a question about a product warranty. The chatbot replies confidently and mentions a "standard 5-year warranty". Lena checks the company's product page: the warranty is two years. She uses the official information and tells her team lead that the chatbot answer was wrong.

## Key takeaways

- Who, when, where, who else: four questions before you trust information.
- AI chatbots predict likely text — fluent is not the same as correct.
- Keep personal and confidential data out of unapproved AI tools.
- You remain responsible for what you use and send.
