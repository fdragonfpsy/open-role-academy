## Why this matters

As an HR professional you rarely give the feedback yourself; you help managers and employees do it well. This unit covers goal setting, the appraisal process, documentation and coaching. It contains no legal specifics: check current law, your works agreement or legal counsel, and check the rules and the works council before introducing or changing appraisal forms.

## Supporting goal setting

The SMART article describes SMART as a mnemonic for goal criteria (specific, measurable, assignable, realistic, time-bound) and notes that variants use words such as attainable, relevant and timely. It says the term was proposed by George T. Doran in 1981. The Goal setting article also quotes a critical view that equating goals with SMART action plans may stifle a fuller use of goal theory. Practice insight: treat SMART as a checklist, not a guarantee. Ask: what will we observe when this is reached, by when, who is responsible, and does the person accept the goal? The German article on Zielvereinbarung adds that a goal should be accepted, realistic with effort and motivating, and that agreeing goals differs from a one-sided target set by the manager.

## The appraisal process

Based on the Performance appraisal article and the German article on Zielvereinbarung, a typical cycle is: prepare and define goals, agree them, work towards them, compare target with actual (Soll-Ist-Vergleich), then start again. Practical steps for HR:

1. Provide the form, criteria and timetable, agreed with the people responsible.
2. Remind managers to collect evidence over the whole period, not only recent weeks.
3. Hold the conversation, then record agreed goals and development steps.
4. Store the record only where it should be kept (see the advanced unit on data protection).

The Wikipedia article says proper documentation records performance transparently and protects all parties in case of disputes. Practice insight: record facts and agreements, not guesses about personality.

## Coaching managers in giving feedback

One common approach (practice insight, not from the cited pages) is **situation, behaviour, impact**: name the situation, describe the observable behaviour, explain its effect, then ask for the other person's view and agree on next steps. The German feedback article describes a similar scheme: what I observed, what I thought or felt, how I reacted, what I wish for. Coach with real examples: ask the manager to draft, then remove judgments such as "careless" and replace them with what happened.

## Difficult feedback conversations

Practice insight: prepare facts and examples, state the purpose at the start, give the person space to respond, and listen actively. The German article advises receivers not to justify themselves; for managers, the equivalent is not to argue back either, but to ask clarifying questions. End with a clear, written next step and a follow-up date. If emotions run high, pause and continue later. If a conversation touches on conflict, health or discrimination, involve the right people and follow your company rules.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. Manager A wants to give "Employee B" feedback about repeatedly late reports. You coach A: Situation: "In the last three monthly reports"; behaviour: "the report reached finance after the agreed date"; impact: "finance could not close on time." A asks B for the reasons and learns that B waits for data from another team. Together they agree on a new data deadline and a check-in in two weeks. A writes a short note of what was agreed. The goal is rewritten: "Report delivered by the agreed date in the next three cycles."

## Key takeaways

- SMART is a helpful checklist; accepted, realistic goals matter more than the acronym.
- An appraisal cycle: define goals, agree, work, compare target and actual, repeat.
- Document facts and agreements, and collect evidence across the whole period.
- Situation, behaviour, impact is one common model; practise with real examples.
- Prepare difficult conversations, listen, agree next steps. Legal details: check current law and your works agreement.
