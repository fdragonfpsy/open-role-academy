## Why this matters

At advanced level you shape how the organisation prevents harm, not only how it reacts. This unit stays general for a Germany/EU setting. It names no specific laws, duties or deadlines: legal obligations, co-determination rights and health data rules depend on current law, collective agreements and company rules. Involve legal counsel, occupational safety experts and occupational physicians for specific cases. HR staff do not give legal or medical advice.

## From single measures to a system

The Occupational safety and health article describes a broad field, from prevention to workers' welfare. Practice insight: a mature approach links four strands: (1) regular risk assessment, including psychological stress, (2) safe work design, (3) support offers and (4) a structured response to long absence and return. Clear roles prevent gaps: who owns assessments, who follows up measures, who talks with representatives, and who decides on escalations.

## Work design before programmes

The Occupational stress article notes that demands, control and support matter. The Hierarchy of hazard controls article shows that changing the work beats relying on individual behaviour. Practice insight: a yoga class does not offset permanent understaffing. The Workplace wellness article describes programmes as part of a wider approach. Decide where limited effort has the most effect, and say so openly to leadership.

## Using indicators carefully

Combine sources such as aggregated absence rates, accident and near-miss reports, survey results and assessment findings. Practice insight:

- Report on groups large enough to prevent identifying individuals.
- Treat numbers as questions to explore, not proof of cause: absence can rise for many reasons.
- Never use health data to judge individual performance.
- Share only what is necessary and who needs to know.

## Sensitive cases

Return-to-work support, as described in the article on Betriebliches Eingliederungsmanagement, is a structured, participatory process. Practice insight: stay neutral, explain the process and what will be recorded, keep medical details with the employee, involve representatives where applicable, and follow up. For signs of burnout or severe distress, point to professional support and avoid diagnosing.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. Over two quarters, a logistics site shows more near-miss reports and more short absences in one shift. You do not blame the shift. With safety experts and the team you review the assessment: a new schedule shortened breaks between tasks. You propose a pilot with changed rotation (work design), keep the stress survey as group-level only, and offer confidential counselling as an addition. You set a review after one cycle and report back to leadership and employee representatives, with no personal data.

## Key takeaways

- Combine assessment, work design, support and return-to-work into one prevention approach.
- Change the work before relying on individual programmes.
- Use aggregated indicators as questions; protect privacy and avoid health judgments.
- In sensitive cases stay neutral, explain the process and follow up.
- Legal details: involve counsel and experts; check current law and agreements.
