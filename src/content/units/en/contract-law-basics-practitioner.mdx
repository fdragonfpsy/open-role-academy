## Why this matters

Most contracts you review are built on templates or on the other side's standard terms. You need to spot what is negotiated, what is pre-formulated and where problems arise when performance goes wrong. This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## General terms and conditions

The German article on Allgemeine Geschäftsbedingungen (AGB) describes them as pre-formulated contract terms that one party (the user) presents to the other for many contracts. Because the other party usually cannot negotiate them, German law subjects such terms to a specific content control: terms that unfairly disadvantage the other party can be ineffective. How strict the control is differs between business-to-business and business-to-consumer contracts. For consumers, EU law also sets information duties and rights in distance and off-premises contracts; the Consumer Rights Directive on EUR-Lex is the primary source. Whether a given clause is valid is a legal question for counsel.

## Performance problems

Things can go wrong after signing. At concept level, statutes know typical situations:

- **Delay:** performance not made on time.
- **Defects:** goods or work do not conform to the agreement.
- **Impossibility:** performance cannot be made at all.
- **Breach of other duties:** for example, failing to protect the other party's property or data.

Each situation can lead to rights such as demanding performance, price reduction, withdrawal or damages, and the details depend on contract type and on the contract itself. Liability clauses try to allocate risk, but mandatory law limits what they can exclude.

## A clause checklist

For each clause, ask:

1. **Who** owes **what**, **when** and **how** (clear and complete)?
2. Does it fit the contract type and any applicable mandatory rule?
3. Is it balanced, or does it shift risk to one side without a reason?
4. Is wording unambiguous? Are terms defined once and used consistently?
5. Are consequences of non-performance and the way to end the contract stated?
6. Which law and place of jurisdiction apply, and is that acceptable?
7. Is a clause from standard terms, and does it conflict with an individually negotiated term?

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented clause. A supplier's terms say: "The supplier is liable for nothing under any circumstances." Check: does this look balanced? Does it conflict with mandatory law? You do not decide validity yourself: mark it as high-risk, note that it is in standard terms, add your questions and send it to counsel with the surrounding clauses.

## Key takeaways

- AGB are pre-formulated terms and are subject to special fairness control; consumer contracts have extra EU protection.
- Typical performance problems are delay, defects and impossibility; consequences depend on type and contract.
- Liability clauses cannot exclude everything.
- Use a checklist and mark unclear or high-risk clauses for counsel.
