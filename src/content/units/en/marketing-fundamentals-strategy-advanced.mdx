## Why this matters

At advanced level you do not only write plans, you decide what the plan leaves out. A strategy is a set of choices about where to compete and how to win there; tactics are the actions that carry it out. Many plans fail not because a tactic is bad but because the tactics do not add up to a choice.

## Testing coherence

Take every planned action and ask: which strategic choice does this support? Actions that serve no choice are candidates for removal. Next, check the marketing mix for contradictions: a strategy of being the trusted premium option does not match constant discounting, and a niche strategy does not match mass-media buying. The German Wikipedia article on Marketingstrategie defines a marketing strategy as a long-term, planned approach to reaching the marketing goals within a marketing plan. In practice this means the mix implements the strategy, not the other way round.

## Trade-offs under a limited budget

Every budget forces choices. A practical method: list the options, estimate for each the expected contribution to the objective, the effort and the uncertainty, and say openly what you will not do. Document the assumption behind the choice, so that you can tell later whether the plan or the assumption was wrong.

## Steering with metrics

OpenStax has a section on tracking marketing plan progress with metrics. The core idea for advanced practice: define in advance which result would make you change course, for example 'if cost per qualified lead stays above the agreed limit for two months, we stop the channel'. A metric without a decision rule is only decoration. Also separate leading indicators (sign-ups, enquiries) from outcome measures (revenue), and do not confuse correlation with the effect of your campaign.

## Several businesses, several strategies

OpenStax points out that a company with several strategic business units usually plans each separately, because the markets differ. The same applies to segments inside one business: one overall slogan rarely serves a student customer and a corporate buyer equally.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An independent online insurance broker has seven planned actions. When the marketing lead maps them to two strategic choices, 'be the easiest comparison for freelancers' and 'grow through recommendations', three actions fit neither (a general image ad, a trade-fair booth and a prize draw). She proposes to drop them, shift the budget to a freelancer guide and a referral offer, and sets the rule: if the referral offer produces fewer than the agreed number of new contracts after one quarter, it is reviewed. The reasons and assumptions are written in the plan.

## Key takeaways

- Strategy is a choice; tactics serve it. Remove actions that serve no choice.
- State trade-offs and assumptions openly; say what you will not do.
- Metrics need decision rules, and leading indicators differ from outcomes.
- Different businesses or segments may need different strategies.
