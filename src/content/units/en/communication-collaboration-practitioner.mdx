## From talking to working together

At practitioner level you coordinate with several people, often across teams and locations. The basics — listening, clear messages, feedback — remain essential. On top come three skills: making meetings worth their time, working well when people are not online at the same time, and turning disagreement into better decisions.

## Meetings that are worth it

A meeting costs the time of everyone in it. Make it count:

- **Goal and agenda in advance**, so people can prepare.
- **Only the people needed** for the decisions or input; others get the summary.
- **Facilitation:** keep time, make sure everyone is heard, park side topics.
- **Written outcome:** decisions, action items with owners and dates — shared the same day.

If a meeting has no decision to make and nothing to discuss, an update in writing may be enough.

## Working asynchronously

Teams in different locations and time zones cannot solve everything in calls. Asynchronous collaboration works when there is **one shared document** instead of parallel copies, **comments** instead of reply-all chains, a **deadline for input**, and a **named person who decides**. Write messages so they can be understood without a follow-up call: context, question, options, deadline.

## Disagreement and feedback

Disagreement about the task is not a problem — it often improves decisions. What matters is how it is handled. When someone objects, ask what concerns are behind the objection, summarise them, and look for **criteria both sides accept** (cost, risk, time). Avoid both extremes: pushing your view louder, or giving up immediately to keep the peace.

The same applies when you **receive critical feedback**: listen, ask clarifying questions, thank the person — and decide afterwards what you will change. Defending yourself at once makes people less willing to give you feedback next time.

## Worked example

Jonas coordinates a process change with colleagues in two offices. Instead of a weekly one-hour call for twelve people, he shares a document with the proposal, asks for comments within three days and names the department head as decision-maker. A 30-minute call is held only for the two points that remain disputed. The decision is documented in the same document.

## Key takeaways

- Goal, right people, facilitation, written outcome — or no meeting.
- Async: one document, comments, deadlines, a decision-maker.
- Explore the concerns behind objections; agree on criteria.
- Receive feedback by understanding it first.
