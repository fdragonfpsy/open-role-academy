## Why this matters

Work changes: new tools, new rules, new tasks. Learning and development (L&D) is how a company helps people keep up and grow. HR plans and supports it. This unit gives the basic map for a Germany/EU setting. It contains no legal specifics: rules on paid training time, funding, works council participation and data depend on current law, collective agreements and company rules. Check them or ask legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## What L&D is

The English Wikipedia article on training and development describes it as activities that help employees acquire knowledge and skills for their current job and prepare for later roles. The German article on Personalentwicklung describes the same idea as measures that develop the qualifications and potential of employees in line with company goals. In short: L&D links what people can do today with what the organisation and the person need tomorrow.

## Common learning formats

- **On the job**: learning while doing real work, for example by shadowing a colleague or taking on a new task with support.
- **Courses and workshops**: in person or online, with a trainer and a fixed schedule.
- **E-learning**: self-paced digital content, often delivered through a learning management system (LMS), a software platform that offers and tracks learning content.
- **Mentoring**: an experienced person supports a less experienced one over time. The Mentorship article describes it as a relationship of guidance and support.

Practice insight: no format is best in general. The right choice depends on the goal, the learners and the budget.

## Recognising a training need

A training need is a gap between what a task requires and what a person or team can currently do. The Needs assessment article describes needs assessment as a systematic process of identifying such gaps before choosing a solution. Sources of evidence are, for example, new tasks or tools, recurring errors, questions to the support team, feedback from managers and the wishes of employees. Practice insight: not every problem is a training problem. Unclear processes or missing tools cannot be fixed with a course.

## Check afterwards

Practice insight: after a measure, ask simple questions. Did people like it? Did they learn something? Do they now do something differently at work? Did it help the team? The German Bildungscontrolling article treats checking the quality and benefit of education measures as part of managing them.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. The finance team introduces a new expense tool. A team lead tells you that several people ask the same questions. You talk to three colleagues and find that nobody knows how to attach receipts. The gap is specific and small. You choose a 30-minute screen-sharing session plus a one-page guide, not a full-day course. Two weeks later you ask the team whether the questions have stopped and whether receipts are attached correctly. The need was clear, the format fit it and the check was simple.

## Key takeaways

- L&D connects what people can do today with what work requires tomorrow.
- Typical formats: on the job, courses, e-learning (often via an LMS) and mentoring.
- A training need is a specific gap; not every problem needs training.
- Check afterwards whether people learned and whether work improved.
- Legal details: check current law, your collective agreement or legal counsel.
