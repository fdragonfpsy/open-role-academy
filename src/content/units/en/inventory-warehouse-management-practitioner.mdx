## Why this matters

Practitioners decide when to order and how much, not only whether. A clear rule per item replaces guessing and makes stock-outs and overstock both less likely.

## The reorder-point system

In a reorder-point system an order is triggered as soon as stock falls to a defined reorder level (Meldebestand). The check happens after every withdrawal, so order dates are not fixed in advance. The level depends on the typical use until the goods arrive plus a safety stock for unusual delivery times or higher use. There are two common policies: order a fixed quantity when the level is reached, or refill up to a target stock level.

## A simple calculation (our own method)

- Average use per day, taken from recent history.
- Lead time in days from order to goods receipt.
- Reorder point = average use x lead time + safety stock.

## How much to order

The classic economic order quantity balances ordering cost against holding cost. It assumes steady demand and full-batch deliveries, so treat it as a starting point: transport rates and quantity discounts can move the best quantity in real life.

## ABC classes and review rules

- A items: the few items with the largest contribution; review often and count more often.
- B items: the middle group; regular review with standard rules.
- C items: many items with little value; simple rules such as a fixed order cycle.

## Stock turnover

Stock turnover is the cost of goods sold divided by the average inventory; 365 divided by the turnover gives the average days stock is held. A low value may point to overstocking or obsolete goods, though occasionally it is intended, for example before an expected shortage.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. An item is used at 25 units per day, the supplier needs 8 days, and the team wants 60 units of safety stock. Reorder point = 25 x 8 + 60 = 260 units. For the whole warehouse, the cost of goods sold is 480,000 euros and the average inventory is 120,000 euros. Turnover = 480,000 / 120,000 = 4 times a year, or about 91 days of stock (365 / 4).

## Key takeaways

- The reorder point covers use during the lead time plus a safety stock.
- Safety stock protects against higher use and late deliveries, not against everything.
- ABC classes let you spend control effort where it counts.
- Read turnover with its reasons: a low value is a question, not a verdict.
