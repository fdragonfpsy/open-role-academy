## Why this matters

Accounts receivable (AR) is money that customers owe your business for goods or services already delivered on credit; it appears as an asset on the balance sheet. Selling is only half the job: until the money arrives, the sale has not helped your cash. The AR team makes sure that invoices are correct, payments are recorded promptly and nothing stays open without anyone noticing.

## The order-to-cash flow

A typical sale runs like this: the customer orders, the goods or services are delivered, an invoice is issued, the customer pays, and the payment is posted and matched to the invoice. This is often called **order-to-cash**. In an ERP or accounting system each customer has a **customer master record** (German: Debitorenstammsatz) with name, address, billing details and payment terms. Wrong master data leads to wrong invoices, so keep it accurate.

## Issuing a correct invoice

An invoice is a commercial document from a seller to a buyer. It lists the goods or services, usually with quantities, prices and the terms of sale. A correct invoice is the best way to get paid on time: if the customer finds an error, payment is typically delayed until it is fixed. Before sending, check that customer, address, description, amounts and payment terms match the order or contract. Many countries require specific details on invoices, for example for VAT; these differ by country, so check your country's rules and your company's guidelines.

## Payment terms and open items

**Payment terms** state when and how the customer should pay, for example a number of days after the invoice date. The resulting **due date** drives everything that follows. An **open item** (German: offener Posten) is an invoice that has not yet been paid in full. The **open items list** shows all open items by customer with invoice number, amount and due date. Reviewing it regularly is the core routine of AR.

## Posting incoming payments

When money arrives, you post it to the bank account and clear the matching receivable, so the item disappears from the open items list. Use the invoice number or payment reference to find the right item. If you cannot identify the payment, do not guess; park it for clarification.

## Worked example

Illustration only; the amounts are invented. A company invoices a customer for a service: net 1,000, VAT 190 (a rate chosen for the example; use the real rate for your case), gross 1,190. Recording the invoice:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts receivable (customer) | 1,190 | |
| Service revenue | | 1,000 |
| Output VAT (payable to tax authority) | | 190 |

When the customer pays into the bank account:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Bank | 1,190 | |
| Accounts receivable (customer) | | 1,190 |

The receivable is cleared and the open item closes. VAT handling depends on the country and the company's tax status.

## Key takeaways

- Accounts receivable is money customers owe you; it is an asset on the balance sheet.
- The flow: order, delivery, invoice, payment, posting and matching.
- A correct invoice with clear payment terms is the best basis for timely payment.
- The open items list shows what is unpaid and when it is due; review it regularly.
- Post payments against the right invoice; unclear payments are clarified, not guessed.
