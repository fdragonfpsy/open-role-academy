## From using tools to using them well

At practitioner level you use digital tools daily and others ask you for help. The difference between "using" and "using well" lies in three habits: giving tools good input, checking their output, and organising information so the whole team can rely on it.

## Good prompts

AI assistants can only work with what you give them. A useful prompt usually contains:

- **context** — what the situation is and what the assistant needs to know;
- **task** — what exactly it should do (draft, summarise, restructure, translate);
- **audience and tone** — who will read it;
- **format** — length, structure, bullet points, language.

Provide the facts yourself instead of asking the tool to "know" them. Then treat the result as a **first draft**, not a finished product.

## Verifying output

Summaries can drop important points, shift emphasis or invent details. A simple routine helps: compare key numbers, names and dates with the source; check whether anything important is missing; and read the result once as your reader would. If your team or company requires it, state that AI was used. You stay responsible for what you send.

## Bias and fairness

LLMs learn from large amounts of existing text, which can contain stereotypes and historical bias. This matters most when AI touches decisions about people — for example in recruiting, credit or performance topics. Such uses need clear rules, human oversight and compliance with data protection law; raise the topic early.

## One reliable version

Files like "budget_final_v3_NEW.xlsx" in e-mail threads create conflicting copies. Better: one shared file in an agreed location, with access rights and **version history**. That makes changes traceable and protects data.

## Worked example

Tom prepares a steering-committee update. He gives the assistant the facts (delay, new date, decision needed), the audience and a 150-word limit. The draft is good but states the wrong date in one place — Tom corrects it, adds a note that the draft was AI-assisted, and stores the final text in the project's shared folder.

## Key takeaways

- Context, task, audience, format: the four parts of a useful prompt.
- Always check facts and completeness against the source.
- Be careful with AI in decisions about people.
- One shared file with version history beats ten attachments.
