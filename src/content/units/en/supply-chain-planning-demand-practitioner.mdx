## Why this matters

Demand changes, suppliers have lead times and capacity is limited. Practitioners need a routine that brings sales, operations and purchasing to one plan before problems arrive at the dock.

## Sales and operations planning

S&OP is a management process that integrates demand forecasting with supply planning, to balance demand with the available resources so that different departments work from the same assumptions. It is usually run monthly and looks ahead up to 18 months at product-family level. Typical steps:

## The steps

- Data gathering.
- Demand planning.
- Supply planning.
- Pre-meeting to resolve gaps.
- Executive meeting for the final decision.

## MRP and the purchasing role

Material requirements planning uses a master schedule, based on demand forecasts, together with the materials already on hand to ensure materials are in the right places, in the right amounts at the right times. Consumption-driven reorder points and needs-driven planning are different ways to trigger orders. Purchasing's own contribution to S&OP (our own view) is supplier lead times, capacity limits and known risks; raise them in the pre-meeting, not after the plan is approved.

## The bullwhip effect and countermeasures

- Demand signals: each stage forecasts from the orders it sees; countermeasure (our own): share real end-customer sales data.
- Order batching: bundled orders create peaks; countermeasure: smaller, more frequent orders.
- Shortage gaming: fear of shortage leads to over-ordering; countermeasure: transparent allocation rules.
- Price fluctuations: expected price rises trigger forward buying; countermeasure: stable price agreements.

## Finding bottlenecks with data

Look at the forecast error per item, for example the mean absolute difference between forecast and actual, the variation in supplier lead times, the delivery performance by supplier and the weeks of demand covered by stock. Items where several of these look bad are your candidates for attention. These indicators are our own suggestion; choose the ones you can measure reliably.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. An item was forecast at 100 units per month. Actual demand was 90, 120, 80 and 130. The absolute errors are 10, 20, 20 and 30, so the mean absolute error is 20 units. Average demand was 105, so the forecast was on average 5 units low and about 19 percent off per month. With a supplier lead time of 6 weeks that varies by 2 weeks, the team raises the safety stock for this item and asks the supplier for a capacity commitment.

## Key takeaways

- S&OP brings demand and supply to one plan in a regular cycle.
- MRP derives timed material needs from the schedule and stock on hand.
- The bullwhip effect has four typical causes, each with a countermeasure.
- Combine forecast error, lead times and delivery performance to find bottlenecks.
