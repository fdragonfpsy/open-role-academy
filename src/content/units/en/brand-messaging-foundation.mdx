## Why this matters

Customers meet your organisation in many places: an advert, a website, a delivery note, a support call. If these moments feel unrelated, people cannot tell what you stand for. Brand work gives all of them one direction.

## What a brand is

Wikipedia defines a brand as a name, term, design, symbol or other feature that distinguishes one seller's goods or services from those of others. OpenStax goes further: a brand is an intangible asset with tangible value, a feeling that results from the organisation's promotion efforts together with what customers make of it. The consequence is important. You do not fully control your brand, because customers help define it. What you control is what you say, show and do.

## Parts of a brand

OpenStax names assets such as the brand name, the brand mark (logo) and the brand positioning. Name and mark make the brand recognisable. Positioning is the idea you want people to link with it, for example 'the quick option' or 'the careful option'.

## Brand message and consistency

A brand message is the short statement of what you offer, for whom, and why it matters. OpenStax describes integrated marketing communications as using different promotion methods to send the same message to the target audience. Customers differ, so you use several channels, but the core message stays the same. Without this, the messages become disjointed and customers are confused about what to do.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A small bakery in Hamburg uses a friendly tone on Instagram, a formal tone on its flyers and the slogan 'cheapest rolls in town' on a poster, while its website says 'premium craft baking'. Visitors cannot tell whether it is a discounter or a craft bakery. The owner decides on one message: 'Fresh craft bread, baked every morning in your neighbourhood.' Every channel now repeats this idea in its own format, while the tone stays warm and plain.

## Key takeaways

- A brand is a name, a mark and a feeling that customers form; you influence it but do not own it alone.
- Positioning is the idea you want linked with the brand.
- One core message, repeated in different formats, beats several unrelated ones.
- Write the message in one plain sentence: what, for whom, why it matters.
