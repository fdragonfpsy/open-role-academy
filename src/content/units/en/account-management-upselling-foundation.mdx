## Why this matters

Winning a customer is only the start. The OpenStax chapter on the selling process describes follow-up as a major step in creating lifetime customer value. The CRM chapter adds that retaining a loyal customer is typically easier and less expensive than acquiring a new one.

## What account management is

Account management means looking after existing customers so that they get value, stay and, where it fits, buy more. It includes onboarding, regular contact, solving problems, preparing renewals and noticing new needs.

## Useful contact

Contact should have a reason: check how the first weeks went, share a tip that fits their use, ask about goals for the next quarter, or inform them about a change that affects them. Contact that only asks "do you want to buy more?" teaches customers to ignore you. Note each conversation in the CRM so that colleagues know the status.

## Upselling and cross-selling

**Upselling** means inviting a customer to buy a more expensive item, upgrade or add-on. **Cross-selling** means selling an additional product or service to an existing customer. At foundation level the rule is simple: offer something extra only when it solves a problem the customer has told you about or that clearly shows in how they work. Otherwise you risk harming trust.

## Problems are a chance

A customer who reports a problem still cares enough to tell you. Reply quickly, take ownership, involve the right colleague and report back. A well-solved problem often strengthens the relationship.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented. A small customer bought a plan for five users. During a call four weeks after start, they say the sixth colleague is waiting for access. You do not push a larger plan. You ask how many people will use it in the end, hear "about eight", and offer the plan for ten users with a clear price comparison. You write the conversation into the CRM and plan a call in two months to ask how the new team members are doing.

## Key takeaways

- Existing customers are cheaper to keep than new ones are to win.
- Every contact needs a useful reason.
- Offer extras only for needs you have actually heard or seen.
- Problems handled well build trust.
- Write down what you learn in the CRM.
