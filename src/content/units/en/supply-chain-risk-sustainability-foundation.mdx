## Why this matters

When a supplier fails, you feel it first: a line stops, a customer waits, a deadline slips. Purchasing sits closest to suppliers, so you are often the first person who can notice a problem, and the person who is asked what happened.

## What supply chain risk is

A supply chain risk is something that can hurt your organisation but arises at a supplier or somewhere else in the chain for your goods and services. The most common way to size a risk is to combine two questions: how likely is it, and how large is the effect if it happens. A simple grid with likelihood on one axis and impact on the other, often coloured like traffic lights, helps to compare risks and to decide which ones to deal with first.

## The four steps

- Identify: find out what could go wrong, for example a supplier insolvency or a blocked transport route.
- Assess: estimate likelihood and impact for each risk.
- Treat: decide what to do, for example reduce, accept or share the risk.
- Monitor: keep checking, because suppliers and markets change.

## Sustainability is part of the picture

Suppliers can also create environmental and social risks, for example harmful working conditions or pollution at a production site. Such a risk can hurt your reputation, your customers' trust and your ability to buy from that supplier. In our own words: treat it like any other risk, with likelihood, impact and an owner.

## Due diligence laws, at topic level

Germany has a law on due diligence in supply chains (Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz, LkSG) that obliges companies above a certain size to deal with human rights and environmental risks in their supply chains. At EU level there is a directive on corporate sustainability due diligence (2024/1760). Scope and national rules have been under discussion and may change, so this unit says nothing about thresholds or duties. Whether your company is affected is a question for legal counsel, so ask Legal or Compliance rather than guessing.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company and figures. A buyer for packaging lists three risks for a carton supplier: insolvency (unlikely, high impact), a late delivery (likely, low impact) and a report of unsafe working conditions at its plant (unlikely, high impact for the brand). On a 1 to 3 scale for each axis the scores are 3, 3 and 3 (likelihood times impact: 1 x 3, 3 x 1, 1 x 3). All three rank equal, which shows the limits of a simple score: the buyer adds a short note on who acts, and passes the working-conditions report to Compliance the same day.

## Key takeaways

- A supply chain risk is sized by likelihood and impact.
- The four steps are identify, assess, treat and monitor.
- Environmental and social issues at suppliers are risks too.
- For due diligence laws, know that they exist and ask legal counsel for what applies to your company.
