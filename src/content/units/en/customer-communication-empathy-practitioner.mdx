## Why this matters

Customers write "This is the third time!" and you can answer the fact, the feeling or the demand. Which one you answer decides whether the contact calms down or escalates.

## The four sides of a message

Friedemann Schulz von Thun's model describes every message with four aspects: the matter itself, what the speaker reveals about themselves, what the message says about the relationship, and the appeal, what the speaker wants the receiver to do. The model also speaks of four ears: a receiver can listen on any of the four sides. Disturbances arise when sender and receiver interpret and weight the sides differently. "This is the third time!" carries a fact (three contacts), a self-revelation (I am annoyed), a relationship signal (you let me down) and an appeal (fix it now).

## Empathy in wording

OpenStax describes empathy in service as focusing attentively on the customer so that they receive caring service, connecting during delivery and making them feel valued; it is not only what is said but how. In writing, this means naming the situation ("I see you have already contacted us twice") before the solution. Do not claim to know how they feel and do not promise results you do not control. Our own rule: acknowledge, then act, then state the next step.

## Tone and positive wording

Adjust formality, length and speed to the customer and the situation: a short, practical reply for someone in a hurry, more explanation for someone unsure. Say what you can do instead of what you cannot: "I can send the replacement tomorrow" rather than "We cannot ship today". Keep the same respect for everyone, whatever the tone of the customer. Positive wording does not hide facts. If the answer is no, give the reason and an alternative.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer writes: "Third mail about my refund. Nobody cares." Draft reply: "As stated in our terms, refunds take several working days." Reviewed on four sides: the draft answers only the fact and signals the relationship "you are wrong". Revised: "Thank you for your patience, and I am sorry you had to write three times. Your refund was approved on 12 November; the bank transfer usually takes a few more days. I will check on Friday and write to you if it has not arrived." Fact, feeling, appeal and next step are covered.

## Key takeaways

- Every message has four sides; answer more than the facts.
- Empathy means acknowledging the situation, not guessing feelings or overpromising.
- Adapt tone to person and situation; keep respect constant.
- Offer what you can do, with reasons and alternatives when the answer is no.
