## Why this matters

Asking several suppliers for offers is the most reliable way to learn what a fair price and a good solution look like. A vague request produces offers that cannot be compared, and a request that quietly favours one supplier damages trust and, for public buyers, can put the whole award at risk.

## The family of requests

- RFI (request for information): you do not yet know what the market offers. You collect information on products and supplier capabilities; an RFQ or RFP usually follows.
- RFQ (request for quotation): the requirement is fixed. Suppliers quote price and terms for exactly that specification.
- RFP (request for proposal): you describe the need and suppliers propose their own solution. This leaves them more room than a tender, so the evaluation can weigh technology and innovation as well as price.
- Invitation to tender: a formal, structured procedure in which the contract goes to the best tender submitted, often decided mainly on price.

"RFx" is the collective term for all of these.

## Who may bid

An open tender is open to all suppliers who can guarantee performance. A restricted tender invites only pre-qualified suppliers, often chosen with a questionnaire that asks every candidate the same questions. A sole-source request goes to a single supplier and fits only where there is essentially one suitable supplier. Allow enough time to prepare an offer; a very short deadline can reduce the number of bids and so the competition.

## What a good request contains (our own checklist)

- Background and goal in a few sentences.
- Requirements, split into must-have and nice-to-have.
- Evaluation criteria and weights, stated before offers arrive.
- Timetable: question deadline, submission deadline, decision date.
- A price table that every bidder fills in the same way.
- How long the offer must stay valid.
- One contact person; answers to questions go to all bidders.

## Contracts in brief

In a purchase contract the seller transfers ownership of the goods and the buyer pays. In Germany it is in principle formfree, so it can be concluded in writing, orally or by conduct; you still document it. A framework contract fixes the terms for many later individual orders: delivery and payment terms, quality requirements, duration, notice period and prices. The wording of contracts is a job for legal counsel; your job is to know what it says.

## Public buyers (topic level)

Public contracting authorities in the EU must follow formal procurement rules. The EU directive on public procurement requires them to treat bidders equally and without discrimination and to act in a transparent and proportionate way. If you work for a public body, ask your procurement office or legal counsel before you send any request. A private company designs its own procedure, but fairness is good practice there too.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company and prices. A company needs 25 identical standing desks to a fixed specification, so it sends an RFQ to three suppliers with a question deadline, a submission deadline and a required validity of 60 days. The quotes are 410, 395 and 430 euros per desk, which is 10,250, 9,875 and 10,750 euros in total. The cheapest quote is valid for only 30 days, but the internal approval takes 45. The buyer asks the supplier to extend the validity in writing, sends the question and answer to all three bidders, and only then compares.

## Key takeaways

- RFI collects information, RFQ compares prices for a fixed need, RFP invites solutions, a tender awards on the best submitted offer.
- A clear request states requirements, criteria, timetable, price format and validity.
- A purchase contract regulates goods and payment; a framework contract fixes terms for later orders.
- Public buyers follow formal rules for equal treatment and transparency; ask legal counsel.
