## Why this matters

Almost every team processes personal data: customers, applicants, staff, suppliers' contacts. In Legal & Compliance you are often the first stop for questions about it. This unit gives you the vocabulary and the core ideas of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.

## What the GDPR is

According to the English and German Wikipedia articles, the GDPR (in German DSGVO) is an EU regulation on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data. As an EU regulation it applies directly in all member states (see the unit on sources of law). Germany adds its own federal and state data protection statutes that fill in parts that the regulation leaves open. The primary source is the regulation on EUR-Lex, available in both languages.

## Key terms

- **Personal data:** any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. A name, an email address or an online identifier can qualify; the German article on personenbezogene Daten explains the term.
- **Processing:** any operation on personal data, such as collecting, storing, using, sharing or deleting.
- **Data subject:** the person the data relates to.
- **Controller:** the party that decides on the purposes and means of processing.
- **Processor:** a party that processes data on behalf of a controller and follows its instructions.

## Core principles

At concept level, the regulation expects that personal data is:

- processed lawfully, fairly and transparently;
- collected for specified purposes and not used in incompatible ways (purpose limitation);
- limited to what is necessary (data minimisation);
- accurate and kept up to date;
- stored no longer than needed (storage limitation);
- protected by appropriate security (integrity and confidentiality).

The controller must also be able to demonstrate compliance (accountability). You can read the exact wording of these principles in the regulation text.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A sales team wants to import a list of contacts from a trade show and use it for a newsletter. Questions to ask: What data is on the list? Who is the controller? For which purpose was the data given? Is the new purpose compatible? Do we need all the fields? How long will we keep it? Who else sees the data? You do not decide alone: you collect the facts and involve the data protection contact or counsel.

## Key takeaways

- The GDPR is an EU regulation on processing personal data; it applies directly, with German statutes adding detail.
- Know the roles: data subject, controller and processor.
- Core principles: lawfulness, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, security and accountability.
- Collect the facts and involve the data protection contact for decisions.
- This unit is education, not legal advice. It describes concepts for Germany and the EU only, with no section numbers, deadlines or amounts; check the current official texts and involve legal counsel for individual cases.
