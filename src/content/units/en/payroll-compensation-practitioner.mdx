## Why this matters

Once you work independently in HR, you are the link between people events and the payslip. The payroll accountants calculate, but they depend on what you deliver. This unit describes the process concept for Germany/EU. It contains no rates, ceilings, allowances or deadlines; the cut-off dates it mentions are always the ones your company or payroll provider sets.

## The data flow

The English article defines payroll as the list of employees entitled to compensation together with the amounts, and as the records of past payments. Four kinds of people events feed it:

- **Hires:** complete master data, start date, pay agreement, tax and insurance attributes as required by payroll.
- **Changes:** pay changes, hours, cost centre, and contract changes, with the effective date.
- **Absences:** leave and sickness records that affect pay, from the time and absence system.
- **Leavers:** end date, outstanding leave, final documents.

The German article on HR information systems describes the value of one shared data basis for master, time and payroll data. If HR is the source, payroll should not retype data; if it must, agree who owns which field.

## Cut-off discipline

Payroll runs in a fixed period, usually monthly. The **cut-off date** is the last day on which changes still count for the current run. It is set by your company or provider, so find it, put it in the team calendar, and treat it as firm. When a change misses the cut-off, do not improvise: tell payroll at once, record the reason, and agree whether it is handled in the next run or as a correction. The German article notes that retroactive pay changes, for example from collective agreements, can lead to a corrective recalculation of past periods; late data is a normal reason for corrections, but a costly one.

## Common errors and simple checks

Typical error sources are late or missing hires and leavers, wrong effective dates, unrecorded absences, duplicated entries, and typing errors in bank or tax data. Checks that help:

- A **checklist per event** (hire, change, absence, leaver) with required fields.
- A **plausibility review** before the cut-off: new entries, changed pay, unusually large or empty values.
- A **comparison with the previous period**: who is new, who is gone, whose pay changed, and do the changes match the HR records?
- A **four-eyes check** on pay-relevant changes.

## Variable pay and benefits

**Fixed pay** is agreed and recurs. **Variable pay** (for example bonuses or additions) depends on conditions. **Benefits**, described in the English article as non-wage compensation, range from insurance to company cars; the article notes that in most countries many benefits are taxable at least to some degree, and that mandated and discretionary benefits are perceived differently. For you, the conceptual rule is: every pay element needs a clear basis (contract, works agreement or collective agreement), a clear start and end, and an owner who tells payroll. How it is taxed or insured is for the payroll accountants or the tax advisor to decide.

## Corrections

When an error is found, the process is: (1) log the case with the person, period and what is wrong; (2) find the cause at the source (data, timing or calculation); (3) let payroll make the correction in the system, never as an off-system promise; (4) inform the employee clearly and confidentially; (5) record the fix and, if it recurs, add a check. Do not decide yourself how tax or contributions are corrected: ask the payroll accountants or tax advisor.

## Worked example

All figures are invented. A leaver, Jan, left at the end of a month, but the end date reached payroll after the cut-off. The next payslip therefore still shows a normal pay of 3000 units.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | The mismatch is found in the comparison with the HR leaver list. |
| 2 | HR logs the case: end date was entered late; cause: missing leaver checklist step. |
| 3 | Payroll corrects the case; the overpayment of 3000 units is handled by the process the company and payroll accountants define. |
| 4 | HR adds a leaver check two days before every cut-off. |

HR does not decide how to recover the amount: that involves payroll and, where needed, legal advice.

## Key takeaways

- HR delivers hires, changes, absences and leavers; payroll can only be as right as that data.
- The cut-off date comes from your company or provider; missing it creates corrections.
- Checklists, plausibility review, comparison with the previous period and four-eyes catch most errors.
- Every pay element needs a clear basis, dates and an owner.
- Corrections are logged, fixed at the source, executed in the system and communicated confidentially.
- Tax and contribution questions go to payroll accountants or tax advisors.
