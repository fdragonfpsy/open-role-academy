## Why this matters

At practitioner level you run the routines: a change arrives, you process it, system and file agree, and payroll receives clean data. This unit contains no deadlines, leave entitlements, retention periods or section numbers; they depend on current law, agreements and the case. Check current law, your data protection officer (DPO), the works agreement or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.

## Processing a contract change

Use the same steps every time: (1) receive the request from the right person, (2) check the change is agreed and documented in writing, (3) update the master data, (4) file the document, (5) confirm the entry against the document, (6) inform those who depend on the data, such as payroll. Whether a change needs the works council or other formalities is not for you to decide; flag it to the HR lead or legal counsel first. Never overwrite history: add the new document, keep the old one.

## Leave and absence workflows

The English article on leave of absence shows that there are many kinds of leave and that the rules differ by country and are set by law, contracts or agreements. A workflow therefore needs the type to be chosen first: request, approval by the responsible manager, record in the system, and a visible calendar entry for the team. Keep the reason out of shared views: the Personalakte article says lists of illness reasons do not belong in the file, and a team calendar only needs "absent". Entitlements come from the rules that apply; ask your HR lead rather than calculating from memory.

## Time recording: purpose and fair use

The English article on time and attendance systems says they track when employees start and stop work and can help reduce overpayment and transcription errors and support proof of attendance. The German article on Zeiterfassung adds that in Germany systems recording presence are usual, that going further, such as combining time calculation with productivity checks, is described as not permissible without explicit consent, and that a 2019 ruling of the European Court of Justice requires member states to oblige employers to record working time accurately, objectively and reliably. It also says employees should be able to view and comment on their recorded data, that deleting recorded data damages authenticity, and that corrections should leave a change log. Rules: record only what the purpose needs, show people their own data, correct through logged changes, and ask your DPO about methods such as biometrics.

## Employment references

According to the German article, the Arbeitszeugnis is a document the employer issues in writing when employment ends, either simple (type and duration of the work) or qualified (also performance and conduct). The article names four principles: clarity, truthfulness, completeness and goodwill. The English article notes that coded phrases exist in German references. You run the process: record the request, route the draft to the manager, check names, dates and titles, and file the final version. Wording of the assessment and disputed points belong with the manager and legal counsel; the article also notes that standardised assessment methods can trigger works council co-determination.

## HR system data hygiene

The German article on personnel information systems describes them as systems for recording, storing, processing and analysing personnel administration data, with components such as master data, payroll and time data, reporting and planning. The English article on data cleansing explains that errors come from entry mistakes, transmission or storage problems or differing definitions, and distinguishes validation at entry from later cleansing. For you: use mandatory fields and drop-down values, one format for names and dates, regular consistency checks, and a note of what changed and why.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. "Employee B" asks for a role change and a leave request in the same week. You process the change by the six steps. You notice the HR system lists two spellings of a department name: you correct to the agreed value, note the change and tell the HR lead. For the leave request you choose the type, get approval and enter only "absent" in the team calendar. A colleague asks you to "quickly check" the hours Employee B logged on a project; you clarify the purpose with your DPO first.

## Key takeaways

- Use one repeatable routine for contract changes; add and keep, never overwrite.
- Choose the absence type first; keep reasons out of shared views.
- Time recording needs a purpose, transparency and logged corrections.
- Reference requests: you run the process; wording disputes go to the manager and legal counsel.
- Keep HR data consistent with validation and logged corrections.
- This is orientation for Germany/EU; check current law, your DPO, the works agreement or legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal advice.
