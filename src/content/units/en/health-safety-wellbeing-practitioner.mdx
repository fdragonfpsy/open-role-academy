## Why this matters

At practitioner level you support assessments and follow-up instead of only passing on reports. This unit stays general for a Germany/EU setting. It names no specific laws, duties or deadlines: obligations, documentation requirements and the roles of safety experts, occupational physicians and works councils depend on current law and company rules. Involve the responsible experts and legal counsel. HR staff do not give legal or medical advice.

## Steps of a risk assessment

The Risk assessment article describes identifying hazards, estimating likelihood and consequences and deciding on actions to reduce risk. The German article on Gefährdungsbeurteilung presents a similar logic for work. A practical sequence (practice insight):

1. Define the area, job or activity.
2. Identify hazards by observing, asking employees and reviewing incidents.
3. Rate the risk: how likely, how severe?
4. Decide on measures and assign owners and dates.
5. Carry out the measures and check whether they work.
6. Record the result and review it when work changes.

## Choosing measures: the hierarchy of controls

The Hierarchy of hazard controls article ranks measures from most to least effective: eliminate the hazard, substitute it, use technical controls, use organisational measures, and last, personal protective equipment. Practice insight: asking people to "be more careful" is the weakest option. Prefer changing the work itself.

## Psychological stress factors

The Occupational stress article names factors such as high demands, low control and low support. In an assessment, you look at the work (tasks, time pressure, interruptions, shift patterns, leadership and cooperation), not at individual personalities. Practice insight: group-level results protect privacy better than individual diagnoses. Results should lead to changes in work design.

## Absence and return to work

The article on Betriebliches Eingliederungsmanagement describes a structured process to support employees returning after longer illness. Practice insight: it is voluntary for the employee, health details stay with the employee, and the aim is to find workable adjustments. Check current law and involve the responsible representatives.

## Worked example

Illustration only; no real data. In a service centre, several people report headaches and stress. You gather facts with the safety professional: loud open space, phone peaks at lunchtime and little say over breaks. Measures by hierarchy: add acoustic panels (technical), stagger breaks and add a second queue at peak times (organisational), and offer a short briefing on stress management as a supporting step, not the main one. You set owners and a review date after eight weeks, and report only aggregated results to the team.

## Key takeaways

- Assess: identify hazards, rate risk, decide measures, act, check, document.
- Prefer eliminating or reducing hazards over relying on behaviour or protective equipment.
- Assess psychological stress factors in the work, not in individuals.
- Return-to-work support is structured, voluntary for the employee and privacy-sensitive.
- Legal details: check current law, collective agreements and ask experts.
