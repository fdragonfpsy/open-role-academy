## Why this matters

Advanced practice is about decisions under uncertainty: you cannot control search engines, your measurements are imperfect, and some tempting tactics cause damage later.

## Splitting paid and organic

OpenStax describes paid search as bidding in an auction for sponsored positions, while organic results are not paid for. They differ in speed and control. Paid search can start quickly and stops when the budget stops; organic visibility builds through relevant content and takes time, but you do not pay per click. A decision rule, our own: use paid search to test demand and messages for a topic quickly, and invest in lasting content where the tests show interest. Always compare cost per conversion for both, and record which assumptions you made.

## SEO promises and rules

The German Wikipedia article states that methods which push irrelevant pages into top positions are called search-engine spamming and violate the rules search engines set to protect their results from manipulation. It also lists techniques that exist to influence results, and notes that dominant search engines differ from market to market. Consequences for judgement: be sceptical of anyone who guarantees a ranking, because the algorithm belongs to the search engine and not to the agency; ask for the method and for how success is measured; and choose methods that make the page more useful for readers, because those do not depend on loopholes.

## Interpreting metrics

OpenStax defines a conversion as turning a visitor into a customer, and bounce rate as the share of visitors who view one page and leave. Neither is complete. A bounce on a contact page may be a success if the visitor found the phone number. Sources overlap: a person may see a paid ad, later search organically and convert. Report which source got the credit by your rule, and state that other rules would split it differently.

## Privacy in measurement

Measurement that identifies people or uses cookies is subject to the GDPR. The principles in Article 5, such as lawfulness, purpose limitation and data minimisation, apply. In practice: define the question first, collect only what it needs, and ask the data protection contact before introducing a new tracker. Do not use trackers on the basis of habit.

## Reducing dependency

A channel can change rules, prices or reach without notice. Balance owned channels such as email lists and the website with rented channels such as search and social platforms.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An agency promises a niche software firm 'first place on Google in three months'. The head of marketing declines: no one outside the search engine can guarantee this. She asks for a content plan answering customer questions, a technical check of crawlability, and a reporting setup with conversions per source. In parallel, a small paid test checks which messages attract demo requests.

## Key takeaways

- Use paid search for fast tests, organic for lasting visibility, and compare cost per conversion.
- Rankings cannot be guaranteed; manipulation breaks search-engine rules.
- Read bounce rate and conversions in context; attribution rules are conventions.
- Measure with a clear question, minimal data and the privacy contact on board.
