## A dashboard is a set of decisions

A KPI dashboard is only useful if each number helps someone decide or act. The Key performance indicator article says KPIs provide a focus for improvement, support evidence-based decisions and that their effectiveness depends on clear definition, reliable data and appropriate interpretation; poorly designed indicators may create unintended incentives. So keep a dashboard small: a handful of KPIs tied to questions the audience actually has.

## Mix of indicator types

The article distinguishes lagging indicators (outcome-oriented, e.g. annual revenue; easy to measure but hard to influence directly) from leading indicators (drivers that predict future outcomes, e.g. number of client proposals; more actionable but harder to correlate) and input indicators (resources consumed). It warns that an overemphasis on lagging quantitative indicators can lead to short-termism and "gaming" of metrics, and that balanced scorecard thinking, with financial, customer, internal process and learning perspectives, prevents over-reliance on financial metrics alone.

## A KPI definition card

For every KPI on the dashboard, write down:

1. **Name and purpose:** which question it answers.
2. **Formula:** numerator, denominator, unit, period.
3. **Data source:** which system or statement, and any adjustments.
4. **Owner:** who explains changes and acts on them.
5. **Refresh rhythm:** how often it is updated and when.
6. **Comparison basis:** earlier periods, plan or another agreed reference.

Without the card, two colleagues can calculate "margin" differently and argue about numbers instead of causes.

## Honest charts

The Misleading graph article describes ways charts can mislead; it has a section on truncated graphs, which compares a truncated axis with a full-scale one using the same data. As a rule: show the baseline, label axes and units, and choose a chart type that fits the question. If you must truncate an axis, say so in the chart.

## Benchmarking cautions

The Financial ratio article says ratios are generally not useful unless benchmarked against past performance or another company, but also that firms in different industries face different risks, capital requirements and competition, so their ratios are usually hard to compare, and that different accounting methods can make ratios not directly comparable. Check definition, period, accounting standard and business model before comparing.

## Communicating to non-finance colleagues

Lead with the so-what: what changed, why it matters and what you recommend. Then show one or two supporting numbers. Avoid jargon or explain it in one line, and state the definition and the main limit of the figure.

## Limits of ratios

Ratios compress information, so they hide detail. They look at the past, depend on accounting choices and give no cause by themselves. Use them to find questions, then investigate.

## Worked example

Illustration only; all amounts are invented. Revenue grew from 1,000,000 to 1,200,000 (+20%). Net profit moved from 100,000 to 96,000. Net margin fell from 100,000 / 1,000,000 = 10% to 96,000 / 1,200,000 = 8%.

- Number dump: "Revenue +20%, net profit -4%, margin 8%."
- So-what version for the sales manager: "Revenue grew by 200,000, but we kept 4,000 less profit than last year, because costs grew faster than revenue. Before we push more volume, we should check discounts and delivery costs. Margin = net profit / revenue; the figures are unaudited."

Chart check: two bars show 98 and 102. On an axis starting at 96 their heights are 2 and 6, so the second looks three times as large, while the true increase is 4 / 98, about 4.1%. Start the axis at zero or label the cut.

## Key takeaways

- A dashboard should have few KPIs, each with a definition card: formula, source, owner, refresh, comparison.
- Balance lagging and leading indicators; beware gaming and short-termism.
- Avoid misleading chart design: baseline, axes, units.
- Benchmark only after checking definition, period, accounting method and business model.
- Lead with the so-what; state definition and limits.
