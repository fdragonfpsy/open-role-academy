## Why this matters

At advanced level the segments are rarely obvious. You have several candidates, limited budget and competitors who have already claimed some positions. The work is choosing, defending the choice and checking it.

## Selecting segments

OpenStax notes that some segments may not be attractive and others may not align with the company's strengths, and that choosing one segment with one mix is a legitimate strategy. A practical filter: score each candidate on market size and growth, intensity of need, competitor strength, fit with your capabilities and cost of reaching it. Do not average away a veto: a large segment that you cannot reach should fail, whatever its other scores. State the assumption behind each score so that others can challenge it.

## Head-to-head or differentiation

OpenStax describes head-to-head positioning as competing directly on similar attributes and differentiation as emphasising unique qualities, possibly seeking new markets. Head-to-head can work when you can truly beat a competitor on an attribute customers care about and can prove it. Differentiation works when a distinct need exists that others do not serve. Ask which claim customers can verify and a competitor cannot easily copy. The German Wikipedia article describes positioning as clearly setting an offer apart in the target group's perception; that is a perception, so it has to be tested, not decided in a meeting room.

## Testing a positioning

Before a launch, show two or three positioning statements to representatives of the target group. Ask what they understand, what they would expect to pay and which alternative they would compare it with. Look for statements that are understood without explanation. If the market perceives the offer differently from your intention, the market's view counts.

## Fair targeting

Segments and personas simplify people, and simplification can turn into stereotyping. Keep persona details tied to needs and behaviour, avoid assumptions about protected characteristics, and check that your targeting does not exclude or exploit people in vulnerable situations. In the EU, consumer protection and data protection rules also limit how you may use personal data for targeting; involve your legal or data protection contact when targeting is based on personal data.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. An independent e-bike maker considers three segments: commuters, delivery riders and older leisure riders. Scores and assumptions are written in a table. Delivery riders are attractive but fail on reach; the commuter segment is crowded by large brands. The team chooses leisure riders over 60 with a differentiation positioning built on easy mounting and service by local dealers. It tests two statements with 12 riders in dealer shops; the version emphasising 'easy to step on' is understood immediately, the 'smart' version is not. The persona avoids age-related assumptions about technology and rests on interview quotes.

## Key takeaways

- Select segments with explicit criteria and keep vetoes such as 'cannot reach'.
- Positioning is a perception; test it with the target group.
- Claim only what customers can verify and competitors cannot easily copy.
- Guard against stereotypes and unfair or unlawful use of personal data.
