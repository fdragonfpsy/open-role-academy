## Why this matters

Most companies spend a large part of their money on suppliers. If buying is chaotic, money leaks away through duplicate orders, unchecked invoices and goods nobody asked for. A clear purchase-to-pay cycle (P2P) gives every purchase a path, an owner and a paper trail.

## Procurement, purchasing and sourcing

The words are used loosely, so check how your company uses them. A common reading: procurement is the whole commercial process of acquiring goods, services or works from outside and agreeing the terms, while purchasing is the narrower part that orders and pays. German usage is similar: Einkauf (the buying activities such as collecting quotes, negotiating, ordering) is one part of Beschaffung, which also includes planning and control. Sourcing is the more strategic layer: choosing where and from whom to buy. Procurement also splits into direct spend (items that end up in the product, such as raw materials) and indirect spend (everything else, from office supplies to consulting).

## The purchase-to-pay steps (our own simplified view)

P2P covers requesting, purchasing, receiving, paying and accounting. Real companies name the steps differently; we use seven:

- Need: someone requests an item or service (a requisition).
- Approval: the budget owner agrees.
- Supplier and price: pick an existing contract or ask for quotes.
- Order: the buyer sends a purchase order with item, quantity, price, delivery date and place.
- Receipt: the goods or the service are checked against the order.
- Invoice check: the supplier invoice is compared with order and receipt.
- Payment and booking: the invoice is paid on time and recorded in the accounts.

## Documents and roles

The documents follow the steps: requisition, purchase order, delivery note or receipt confirmation, invoice. Typical roles are requester, approver, buyer, goods receiver and accounts payable. Our own rule of thumb is that no single person should request, order, receive and pay the same purchase. Splitting the roles (the four-eyes principle) makes errors and fraud easier to catch.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. Mia in the sales team needs 20 new headsets. She files a requisition, her team lead approves the 1,400 euro budget, and the buyer Jonas orders from the supplier named in an existing framework contract. The goods arrive; the warehouse confirms 20 of 20 received. The supplier invoice reads 1,400 euro for 20 headsets, which matches order and receipt, so accounts payable books and pays it on the due date. If the invoice had said 22 headsets, the mismatch would have stopped payment until it was clarified.

## Key takeaways

- Procurement is the whole acquisition process; purchasing (Einkauf) is its ordering and paying part; sourcing is the strategic layer.
- The cycle runs need, approval, supplier and price, order, receipt, invoice check, payment and booking.
- Each step has a document and a role; invoice, order and receipt must agree before payment.
- Separate the roles so that nobody controls a purchase from request to payment alone.
