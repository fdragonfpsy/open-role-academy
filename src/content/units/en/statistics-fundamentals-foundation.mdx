## Why this matters

Almost every analysis starts by describing data: what is typical, how much does it vary, what does the shape look like? Wrong summaries lead to wrong decisions long before any advanced method is used.

## Centre

- Mean: the sum of all values divided by their number.
- Median: the middle value of the ordered data (for an even count, the average of the two middle values).
- Mode: the most frequent value.

The mean uses every value, so a few extreme values pull it. The median hardly reacts to them. For skewed data such as salaries or delivery times, the median is often the better description of a typical case.

## Spread

- Range: largest minus smallest value; very sensitive to extremes.
- Interquartile range (IQR): the spread of the middle half of the data, between the first and third quartile.
- Standard deviation: a typical distance of values from the mean; it is the usual companion of the mean.

Two groups can have the same mean and very different spread, so always report centre and spread together.

## Population and sample

The population is the whole group you care about; a sample is the part you actually observe. Summaries of a sample describe the sample and estimate the population. How the sample was chosen decides whether the estimate can be trusted.

## Shape

A histogram groups values into ranges and shows how many fall in each. Look for one or several peaks, symmetry, and a long tail on one side (skew). In right-skewed data the mean is usually larger than the median. Isolated values far from the rest are outliers; investigate them before removing them.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented data. Five delivery times in days: 2, 3, 4, 5, 36. The mean is 10, the median is 4, the range is 34. The 36 is probably a parcel that was lost and found again. Reporting only "10 days on average" would mislead; the analyst reports "median 4 days, one extreme case of 36 days", and checks the 36 with logistics.

## Key takeaways

- Report centre and spread together.
- Use the median when data are skewed or contain extreme values.
- A sample is only as good as the way it was chosen.
- Plot the data before you trust any single number.
