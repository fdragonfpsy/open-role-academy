## Why this matters

Email reaches people directly, but it also lands in a private space. In the EU, sending marketing emails to people who did not agree to receive them is restricted. A good email programme therefore starts with permission, not with a list of addresses.

## What email marketing is

Email marketing means sending commercial messages by email, for example a newsletter, an offer or a reminder. OpenStax lists email as one of the traffic sources that can bring visitors to a website, next to paid search, organic search, paid social and affiliates. Because email can be tracked, you can compare sources and see which one leads to conversions, for example a purchase or a sign-up.

## Permission comes first

This section is Germany and EU context at topic level and not legal advice. The EU ePrivacy Directive, Article 13, says electronic mail for direct marketing may only be allowed for subscribers who have given their prior consent. The German Wikipedia article on opt-in explains the same idea: opt-in is an explicit consent procedure, its opposite opt-out, where people receive advertising until they object, is in many cases not permitted. In Germany the UWG, section 7, is the relevant national law. National law adds details, so ask legal counsel about them.

## Two more basic rules

The same Article 13 forbids sending marketing emails that hide the sender's identity or that have no valid address to which the recipient can send a request to stop. In practice every marketing email needs a clearly identifiable sender and an easy way to unsubscribe. Consent can also be withdrawn: the GDPR says it must be as easy to withdraw consent as to give it.

## Building a list the right way

Use a sign-up form with a clear statement of what people will receive and how often. Make agreeing an active step taken by the person, and never add people just because you have their business card or found their address online. A small list of interested people beats a large list of people who never asked.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A Hamburg yoga studio wants to start a monthly newsletter. It puts a form on its website: "Monthly class news and offers, one email per month, unsubscribe at any time." It does not import addresses from its old contact list because those people never agreed to marketing emails. Every email shows the studio's name and address and has an unsubscribe link in the footer.

## Key takeaways

- Marketing email in the EU needs the recipient's prior consent in general (ePrivacy Article 13); details in Germany are in the UWG, so ask legal counsel.
- Opt-in means explicit agreement; opt-out is in many cases not permitted.
- Always show a real sender and an easy way to unsubscribe.
- Build the list from sign-ups, not from found or bought addresses.
