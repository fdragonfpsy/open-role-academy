## Why this matters

Not every ticket can be handled at once. Priority, clear notes and good articles decide whether the right case is handled first and whether the next colleague can pick it up.

## Priority and service levels

A service level agreement (SLA) describes the agreed quality of a service, including scope, availability and reaction time. Support requests are often governed by SLAs that define target response and resolution times; tickets that are unresolved or high priority are escalated to senior or specialised staff when defined thresholds are met. Our own priority rule: ask how many people are affected (impact) and how soon harm occurs (urgency). High impact and high urgency come first; a single customer asking a general question comes later.

## Case notes (our own)

Our own note format has four lines:

- Facts: what is known, with numbers and dates.
- Done: what you tried and the result.
- Promised: what the customer was told and by when.
- Next: who does what next.

Avoid feelings and opinions about the customer; stick to what happened. Notes may be read by the customer under data protection rules, so write them professionally; ask your data protection officer how access requests are handled.

## Writing a knowledge article (our own)

Articles only help if people can find them, so write for search. Our own template for one article:

- Title: the question as the customer would ask it.
- Short answer: one or two sentences first.
- Steps: numbered, one action each.
- Limits: when this article does not apply and whom to ask.
- Owner and review date.

One problem per article; link related articles. A search that finds nothing is a signal to write one.

## Tidy records

Merge duplicate tickets, correct wrong categories and close what is done. Record only data needed for the case, and keep sensitive details out of free-text fields unless your policy allows them.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. Two tickets arrive within an hour: one from a customer whose payment page fails (many customers might be affected, purchases are blocked) and one asking for the invoice address format. The agent sets the first to high priority and escalates it by the agreed threshold, the second to normal. After solving the second, she writes a three-step article "How to change the invoice address" with owner and review date.

## Key takeaways

- Priority comes from impact and urgency.
- SLAs define targets; escalation follows defined thresholds.
- Notes state facts, done, promised and next.
- Articles answer one question, with steps, limits, owner and review date.
