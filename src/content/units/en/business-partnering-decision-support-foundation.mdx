## Why this matters

Management accounting is described as providing financial and non-financial information so that managers can make decisions. A controller who only delivers tables leaves the last step to the reader. A business partner helps with that last step: what do these numbers mean, and what could we do about it?

## From reporting to partnering

Reporting answers "what happened?". Partnering also asks "why?", "what if?" and "what do you recommend?". The German term Controlling stresses this support function: controllers prepare information and a framework so that managers can steer, while the decisions stay with the managers.

In practice this means:

- Understand the manager's goals and worries before you open the spreadsheet.
- Use the manager's language: orders, customers, capacity, rather than account numbers.
- Keep the message short and the details available on request.

## Translating a number into a message

A useful pattern has three parts:

1. **Fact**: what the number says, with the comparison (plan, last year).
2. **So what**: why it matters for the goal.
3. **Next step**: one or two options, or a question the manager has to decide.

Weak: "Cost centre 4711 is 12% over budget." Better: "Cost centre 4711 is over budget because of overtime. If this continues, the department misses its annual target. Options: hire temporary staff or move the deadline."

## Independence and integrity

Advice is only useful if it is honest. A controller works close to the manager, but should report the numbers as they are, including unwelcome ones. A **conflict of interest** arises when a personal or other interest could work against a duty owed to someone else, for example when your own bonus depends on a figure you report. Typical first steps: say openly that a conflict exists, let someone else check the figure, and write down what was done. If you are unsure, ask your superior or compliance contact.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented figures. A sales manager asks whether a discount of 10% for a large customer is "worth it". The controller does not just answer yes or no. She shows the margin before and after the discount, states the assumption about extra volume, and names the volume at which the deal breaks even. The manager then decides, knowing the condition. When the manager asks to leave out the weak assumption from the slide, the controller keeps it on the slide and explains why.

## Key takeaways

- Partnering means supporting decisions, not only reporting results.
- Structure messages as fact, so what, next step.
- Show assumptions openly; do not hide unwelcome numbers.
- Disclose conflicts of interest and ask when unsure.
