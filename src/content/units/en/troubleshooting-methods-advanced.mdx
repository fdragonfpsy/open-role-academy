## Diagnosis as a team discipline

At advanced level you lead the diagnosis of complex incidents — often under time pressure, with several teams and incomplete information. Your job is to keep the investigation structured, safe and honest about what is known.

## Evidence over assumptions

Recent changes deserve critical attention: if a service slows down right after a firewall update, the update is a strong lead. But **correlation does not imply causation** — two things happening at the same time does not prove one caused the other. Before acting on a hypothesis, look for evidence that would confirm or refute it: logs, affected traffic, whether unchanged systems show the same symptom. If the impact is high and a rollback is safe, rolling back can still be the right step to restore service — just do not mistake it for proof of the cause.

## Multiple and interacting faults

Complex systems can have **several faults at once**, whose symptoms overlap. If a fix removes some symptoms but crashes continue, do not assume the fix failed — there may be a second, independent cause. Be wary of confidence when a fix only seems to work for a while. Verify every fix against the **original symptoms**, under the original conditions.

## Safe diagnosis in production

Trial-and-error in production can turn one incident into several. Reproduce and test in a non-production environment where possible. When you must change production during diagnosis, use the **(emergency) change process**: a short risk assessment, a back-out plan, documentation. This keeps the timeline reconstructable for the later review.

## Making troubleshooting repeatable

Expertise that sits in one person's head is a risk. Build:

- **runbooks** with diagnostic steps and decision points for recurring incident types;
- **observability** — meaningful logs, metrics and synchronised clocks;
- **reviews** after significant incidents that update runbooks and monitoring.

## Worked example

After a storage upgrade, two applications crash at night. The team suspects the upgrade, but Elif asks for evidence: only one application uses the upgraded storage. Logs show the second crash is caused by an expired certificate. Two fixes, two causes — and a new runbook step: check certificate expiry dates during incident triage.

## Key takeaways

- Test hypotheses with evidence; timing alone proves nothing.
- Expect multiple faults; verify fixes against the original symptoms.
- Diagnose safely: test elsewhere, use change control in production.
- Runbooks, observability and reviews spread expertise.
