## Why this matters

Most service problems start as communication problems: the customer says one thing, you understand another, and both walk away unhappy. Listening and clear wording are the cheapest tools you have.

## Active listening

Active listening comes from the psychologist Carl Rogers. German sources distinguish it from the echo technique, which only repeats the last words, and from paraphrasing, which returns the content of the message in your own words. Rogers names an empathic and open attitude, authentic behaviour and acceptance of the other person as its basis. Listening is not passive: it takes effort to take in the content and not only the sound. Typical error sources are ending the listening early and answering before the other person has finished.

## What can go wrong on the way

OpenStax breaks communication into sender, encoding, message, medium, receiver, decoding, feedback and noise. For a customer contact this means: your words are decoded by the customer using their own knowledge and experience, so a term that is clear to you may not be clear to them. Noise is anything that interferes, such as a bad phone line, a distracted reader or a very long email. Feedback tells you whether your message arrived as intended, so ask for it.

## Practice: four small habits

Our own working method for each contact. First, let the customer finish and note facts, wishes and feelings. Second, restate the request in one sentence ("You need the invoice corrected before Friday, is that right?"). Third, ask one clarifying question at a time and avoid jargon. Fourth, close with a summary and the next step, including who does what by when.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer calls: "Your app has deleted my data again!" A hasty agent starts explaining backup settings. The listening agent says: "You are missing data after the last update and this has happened before, correct? Which data is missing, and since when?" The customer feels heard, answers precisely and the agent finds the cause in a sync setting. The call ends with: "I will restore it today and write to you by 4 pm."

## Key takeaways

- Active listening means taking in content and feeling, and showing it by restating.
- Customers decode your words with their own knowledge: avoid jargon and check understanding.
- Ask one clarifying question at a time.
- Close with a summary and a next step with a time.
