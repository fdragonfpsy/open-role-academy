## Why this matters

At practitioner level the question is no longer what SEO is, but what to do on a given page and how to tell whether it worked. We keep to stable principles here: relevance, crawlability and user intent. We do not claim anything about how specific ranking factors are weighted, because search engines do not publish that and it changes.

## Search intent

OpenStax notes that search engines use algorithms to deliver results relevant to the search terms a visitor entered, and that search trends, how often a term is searched, give insight into who a company's customers are and what they look for. Our own working distinction for intent: people want to *learn* ('how to ...'), to *compare* ('best ... for ...') or to *act* ('buy ...', 'contact ...'). A page that answers a learning question with a sales page, or the reverse, mismatches the intent.

## On-page and off-page

The German Wikipedia article on SEO distinguishes on-page optimisation, which concerns the site itself including its texts, from off-page optimisation, which concerns links and mentions elsewhere; it also treats search terms as a topic of their own. For your daily work: make sure the page can be loaded and read by the crawler, uses the words your audience uses, has a clear headline and answers the question completely. Links from other sites to yours cannot be ordered; they follow from content others find worth citing.

## Paid search and landing pages

OpenStax explains that users who click a sponsored ad are directed to the company's website or a landing page. The page should repeat the promise of the ad. Mismatches lead to people leaving at once.

## Reading metrics together

Traffic by source shows where visits come from; conversions show how many became customers or leads; bounce rate shows visitors who view one page and leave. OpenStax notes that high bounce rates can indicate page issues such as slow loading. Read them as a set: many visits with few conversions suggest a mismatch between source and page. Measure with the least personal data that answers the question; tracking that uses cookies or personal data needs a lawful basis under EU rules (see the GDPR, Article 5 principles), so involve your privacy contact.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A training provider runs an ad for 'Excel course for beginners' that leads to the general course overview. Traffic is good, conversions low, bounce rate high. The team creates a landing page for the beginner course with the same headline as the ad and a clear sign-up step. The metrics are compared per source after two weeks.

## Key takeaways

- Match page type to search intent: learn, compare or act.
- On-page: readable, relevant, answers the question; off-page links follow from useful content.
- Ad promise and landing page must match.
- Read traffic, conversions and bounce rate together, and respect privacy rules in measurement.
