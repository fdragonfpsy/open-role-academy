## Why this matters

Customers rarely contact you out of curiosity. They have a question, a fault or a request, and how you handle that moment shapes how they see your organisation afterwards. Customer service is the part of the organisation that meets customers exactly then.

## What customer service covers

Customer service is the assistance and advice a company gives to people who buy or use its products or services, in person or remotely. German texts call it Kundendienst or Kundenservice and list typical tasks: complaints (a product is faulty or lacks promised features), maintenance, repairs, questions about using a product (often called a helpdesk) and requests for spare parts or accessories. What these requests share is the wish for a contact person who is reachable, understands the customer, helps quickly and finds a solution the customer is happy with.

## Satisfaction is a comparison

Customer satisfaction arises when what customers experience matches what they expected: expectation and perceived performance are compared. OpenStax puts it as satisfaction being essentially a function of perception. Two consequences follow. You can raise satisfaction by improving the service or by setting honest expectations. And the same service can satisfy one customer and disappoint another, because their expectations differ.

## Promises and the first contact

Every sentence such as "we will call you back tomorrow" is a service promise. OpenStax names the difference between what a company promised and what it delivered the communication gap, and notes that failing to deliver on a promise damages credibility. A practical goal for each contact, our own working definition: resolve the matter in the first contact where possible, so the customer never has to ask twice. Where that is not possible, say what happens next and by when, and then do it.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented company. A customer of an online shop writes that a kettle arrived without a lid. The agent answers: "A replacement lid is on its way. You will get a tracking email within two working days." The customer plans for about a week. The lid arrives after four days: the promise is kept and the expectation exceeded. Had the agent written "soon", the customer might have expected it the next day and been disappointed by the same delivery.

## Key takeaways

- Customer service helps people who buy or use your product with questions, faults, repairs and complaints.
- Satisfaction compares expectation with perceived experience.
- Every statement is a promise: say only what you can keep, and keep it.
- Aim to resolve in the first contact; otherwise name the next step and the time.
