## Why this matters

A lead who does everything personally becomes a bottleneck and gives the team no room to grow. Delegation means handing work to someone else on purpose. This unit covers the basics at concept level. It is education, not a rule for specific cases.

## Why delegate

The Wikipedia article on delegation describes it as distributing and entrusting work, decision-making and responsibility to another person. It lists reasons such as freeing your own time, having the most qualified person decide, getting another perspective and developing someone's abilities. The German article presents delegation as a concept of division of labour.

## What to delegate and to whom

Good candidates are tasks someone else can do well enough, recurring tasks, and tasks that help someone learn. Keep for yourself what only a lead can do, for example confidential personnel matters. Match task and person: consider skills, workload and interest. The Wikipedia article names matching people and assignments and showing how a task fits the big picture among factors for effective delegation.

## A clear handover

State in your own words:

- **Outcome:** what a good result looks like, not every step.
- **Authority:** which decisions the person may take alone.
- **Deadline and priority:** when it is due and how it ranks against other work.
- **Support and check-ins:** where to ask for help and when you will talk again.

Ask the person to repeat back what they understood.

## Responsibility and accountability

The Wikipedia article on delegation states that you can delegate authority for a task, but the ultimate responsibility is not transferred. Accountability, as the article on accountability describes it, is an account-giving relationship: someone has to inform others about actions and decisions and justify them. In practice, the person doing the task is responsible for doing it well, and you remain answerable to your own manager for the team's results. So delegating means you do not do the work yourself, but you do not stop caring about the outcome.

## Worked example

Illustration only; invented team. Tobias leads a team that prepares a monthly status report. He asks Aylin to take over the report. He explains the goal (a one-page summary the department head reads in five minutes), tells her she may decide the layout but must ask before changing the data sources, sets the deadline as the third working day, and books a 15-minute talk after the first run. When the first report has a small error, Tobias talks to Aylin about how to prevent it and informs his own manager himself.

## Key takeaways

- Delegate to free your time, use the best person for the task and develop people.
- A clear handover has outcome, authority, deadline and agreed support.
- Authority can be delegated; ultimate responsibility and the duty to answer for results stay with you.
- Match tasks to people and explain how the task fits the bigger picture.
